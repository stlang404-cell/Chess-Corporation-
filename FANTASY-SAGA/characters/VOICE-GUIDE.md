# DIALOGUE VOICE GUIDE

**Ilse:** short, dry, exact. Understatement. She answers questions with questions when cornered. She uses trade words naturally (latitude, give, chain, links, true). Swears mildly ("Hell's hinges," "bloody"). Her inner voice is wry and observant and catches *how things are made.*
> "It's not a lie. It's a very confident opinion about where a hedge ought to be."

**Brask:** blunt imperatives and rhetorical questions. Sentences that bite at the end. Rarely uses names; she calls students "Walker" or by surname.
> "Wrong. Wrong is good. Tell me how wrong, Marrow, and in which direction, and we'll make a surveyor of you yet."

**Teo:** warm, digressive and apologetic. Food and numbers. Long run-on sentences when nervous, very short ones when brave.
> "It's — I'm sorry, it's just that the stair is forty-one feet and a bit, and the bit is the part that worries me, because the bit is mostly air."

**Cass:** Margin cadence. Drops some articles and copulas. Blunt, profane, warm. "Hey-ho." "Paper-walls." "Road and rain." She uses "you lot" for College people.
> "Ground don't care what you wrote on it, paper-wall. Ground's got its own opinions."

**Hen:** bright, quick and laughing. Salt Steps idiom ("tide's out on that one"). She gets literal and quiet when she goes Quiet. Her speech flattens: no jokes, short declaratives. That flattening is the horror.

**Kasimir:** formal, complete sentences, no contractions when on his dignity. Dry wit when he relaxes. Legal precision.
> "I do not dislike you, Miss Marrow. I dislike that you are good at something you ought not to be able to do."

**Hallam:** gentle, warm, avuncular. "My dear." Calls the Reach "her." Quotes the Ninefold and talks in terms of duty and care. Not once sinister on the surface; menace comes only through the *logic* when revealed.
> "Every map is an act of love, my dear. We hold her still so that the children of Hobbet can find their way home."

**Halloway:** stammers on non-numbers and goes fluent on numbers. "Error is not sin."

**Calder:** clipped, theatrical pauses, precedent citations ("*Saffery v. the Parish of Wick*, 1193").

**Damask:** fluttery, delighted, whispery. "Oh! Look, *look*."

**Steyne:** smooth, institutional, "we," "the College," euphemism.

**Mother Nettle:** profane crone. "Bugger the crows; they're smarter than you lot."
