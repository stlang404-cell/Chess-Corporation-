# Concept Evaluation, Selection, Originality Audit and Story-Selection Gate

## 1. Comparative evaluation

Ratings are qualitative (Strong / Moderate / Weak). They are artistic judgments meant to compare the concepts against each other, not measurements.

| Criterion | A: Long Survey | B: Bell-Hollow | C: Tidewright | D: Lantern Moths | E: Itinerant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | Strong (land bound by records; a school its students hold in place) | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate (memory theft is common) | Weak (moving-beast settings are familiar) |
| Wonder | Strong (wandering rooms, land-whales, mutable creatures) | Strong | Weak | Strong | Strong |
| Emotional potential | Strong (land, home, dispossession, belonging) | Moderate | Moderate | Strong | Moderate |
| Character depth | Strong (a forger who fears she is fake) | Moderate | Moderate | Strong | Moderate |
| Mystery potential | Strong (three layers) | Moderate | Strong | Strong | Moderate |
| Academy appeal | Strong (a curriculum of genuine, learnable ideas) | Moderate | Weak | Moderate | Moderate |
| Adventure potential | Strong (expeditions into the Drift) | Weak | Weak | Weak | Strong |
| Earned progression | Strong (measurement is a real skill) | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Weak |
| World depth | Strong | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate |
| Seven-book sustainability | Strong (the scope widens outward each book) | Weak | Moderate | Weak | Moderate |
| Literary potential | Strong (map vs. territory; truth vs. control) | Strong | Moderate | Strong | Moderate |

**Selected: Concept A — *The Long Survey*.** It isn't the most complicated concept; its rules fit in seven laws. It was chosen because one premise, *land holds only as well as it is witnessed*, produces the academy, the training, the competitions, the exploration, the mystery, the creature ecology, the moral question and the ending at once.

## 2. Refinements to Concept A (weaknesses found and repaired)

| Weakness | Repair |
|---|---|
| "Observation fixes reality" drifts toward a quantum-physics cliché. | Holding requires *attested record*, meaning measurement plus a signed commitment and the surveyor's personal burden. It isn't mere observation. It reads as a legal and craft act, closer to a contract than to looking. |
| A forger-protagonist could make the College's acceptance look like a convenience. | She enters by her own choice, through the brutal open trial (the Walk), for self-interested reasons: collegiate privilege shields students from Concordance prosecution. Her mentor *suspects* her. Her rival *knows*. |
| Risk that the Book One villain is simply evil. | Magister Aurek Hallam is trying to save the Held Reach from a fraying he alone has measured. His crime is triage: he spends students and outlying villages to save the center. |
| Risk that Book One is only a prologue. | Book One fully resolves its question: who is making the land come apart, and why students are falling ill. The *series* question (why the oldest bindings are failing) is opened only in the last chapters. |
| The creature companion could become a mascot. | The ossel's mechanic (it takes the shape of the most confident description) is used in comedy, in tactics and in the climax. It also quietly carries the series' ethical question: is describing a being the same as owning it? |

## 3. Originality stress test

| Familiar combination | Risk | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Magical school with houses, sorting and sports | Very high familiarity | The Meridian has no houses and no sorting. Students are grouped in *survey parties* of four, chosen by the students themselves after the Walk. The recurring rituals (Morning Survey, Benchmarking, Errata Night, the Lantern Count) all grow out of the magic system itself. There is no ball game. |
| Dragon-rider or creature-bond hierarchy | High | There is no bonded mount and no telepathic link. The ossel isn't bonded at all: it is a free creature with almost no fixity that keeps choosing to stay. |
| Chosen one or prophecy | High | Ilse's ability is common aptitude (about one in four hundred people) plus a criminal skill she taught herself. Nothing is foretold. |
| Deadly academy (death-as-curriculum) | Moderate | The Meridian's danger is real but institutional and environmental (the Drift), not designed cruelty. No death quotas. |
| Maze-like changing environment | Moderate | The Drift Wing shifts *because students fail to hold it*. Its changes follow knowable rules, and students control it by measurement. It isn't designed by captors. |
| Demigod quest structure | Low | Not used. |
| Apprentice to a stern ranger-like mentor | Moderate | Brask is a scholar-surveyor with a ruined reputation and a philosophical quarrel with the College. She is not a woodland warrior, and her lessons are about doubt as much as skill. |
| Shapeshifting companion | Moderate | Similar to daemon or familiar traditions only on the surface. An ossel isn't part of anyone's soul and doesn't settle into a fixed form with age. It *obeys description*, which can be weaponized, and later turns out to be the juvenile of an intelligent people. |
| "Land that moves" | Moderate (fairy-road folklore) | Unlike whimsical fairy roads, here moving land is a measurable physical regime with law, economics and property consequences. |

Conclusion: no single distinctive mechanism has been borrowed. The strongest structures (a school its students hold in place, burden and stilling, a forger as the instrument of truth, a lease between civilizations) are independent inventions.

## 4. Story-selection gate

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compelling protagonist with meaningful desire | Pass | Ilse wants legitimacy and a piece of land no one can redraw. |

| 3 | Original institution | Pass | The Meridian and its Drift Wing, held in place by students every dawn. |
