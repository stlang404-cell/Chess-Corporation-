# Five Original Series Concepts

Each concept is summarized against the required fields. Evaluation follows in `CONCEPT-EVALUATION.md`.

---

## Concept A — *THE LONG SURVEY* (cartographic binding)

- **Premise:** In this world, land is only as fixed as its records. Settled country holds still because trained surveyors have measured and *attested* it. Beyond the Margin lies the Drift, where hills migrate, rivers change their minds and whales of stone swim through meadows. Ilse Marrow forges maps for desperate farmers. To escape prosecution she takes the open admission trial of the College that keeps the world in place.
- **Institution:** *The Meridian*, a college on Cape Verity. Half of it stands on bedrock. The other half, the Drift Wing, extends into unfixed ground and must be re-surveyed by students every dawn, or its rooms wander.
- **Protagonist:** Ilse Marrow, 23, a self-taught forger. She is precise, proud, funny under pressure, and convinced that she is a fraud.
- **Magic:** *Hold*. Measurement plus attestation binds land to a record. The cost is *burden*, which accumulates into *stilling*: the body and mind grow fixed.
- **Mentor:** Field-Surveyor Odile Brask, one-eyed and half-disgraced. She survived a lost expedition and refuses to attest any more maps.
- **Companions:** Teodor Anselm, a genial sounder-mathematician who is afraid of heights; Cass Tenter, a sharp-tongued wayfinder of the Margin-folk; Sorry, a mutable ossel that takes the shape of whatever is described most confidently near it.
- **Rivalries:** Kasimir Reyne, a Charting prodigy and the son of the Chancellor of the Concordance Court. He is the one who spotted her forgery.
- **Geography:** The Held Reach, a ring of settled land around the Zero Stone at Tessary. The Margin is a semi-fixed fringe. The Drift lies beyond.
- **Creature ecology:** Drift fauna have low hold. They include ossels, wayhorns, sounders (land-whales), cairn-crows, hollowings (predators that eat fixity) and the rumored Vey.
- **Central mystery:** Why is well-mapped land coming apart from the inside? And what did the nine founders, the Ninefold, actually do twelve hundred years ago?
- **Adventure structure:** Each book pairs academy life with a widening field expedition into the Drift.
- **Seven-book escalation:** From a sabotaged school, to a lost expedition, to a buried treaty, to a journey to the first city, to an institutional schism, to the world reclaiming itself, to a new kind of map.
- **Ultimate resolution:** The founding map was a *lease* signed with the Drift's people. It has come due. Ilse forges the founders' release and helps draw an *open* atlas that lets the world be both home and alive.

## Concept B — *THE BELL-HOLLOW* (resonance)

- **Premise:** A city-college is carved inside a colossal dormant creature-shell that rings once a century. Students learn *tuning*, the art of matching resonance with matter.
- **Institution:** The Conservatory of the Shell.
- **Protagonist:** Ossian Teague, a deaf stonecutter who feels resonance through his hands.
- **Magic:** Resonance tuning. Each substance has a note. Harmony repairs and dissonance breaks. The cost is hearing loss and emotional "detuning."
- **Mentor:** An exiled bell-founder.
- **Companions, rivalries, geography:** Canal city; rival schools of tuning; a coast of singing cliffs.
- **Creatures:** Choir-eels and shell-mites.
- **Central mystery:** Is the shell alive, and what happens when it rings?
- **Escalation:** The waking of the shell.
- **Resolution:** The city and the creature negotiate a duet.
- **Weakness:** Sound-based magic is hard to dramatize on the page. The deaf-protagonist angle risks being used as a gimmick without lived expertise. Seven-book sustainability is thin.

## Concept C — *THE TIDEWRIGHT LEDGER* (debt magic)

- **Premise:** All magic is borrowed against future luck. A college of actuaries prices and trades miracles.
- **Institution:** The Exchequer College, which doubles as a bank.
- **Protagonist:** Mara Lint, an orphaned bookkeeper who discovers that someone has been borrowing against her life.
- **Magic:** Debt contracts with an impersonal Ledger. The cost is misfortune that comes due unpredictably.
- **Mentor:** A disgraced auditor.
- **Creatures:** Collectors, which are the debts made flesh.
- **Central mystery:** Who holds the original debt?
- **Weakness:** Leans toward the "dense politics" and economics the brief warns against. Little exploration potential. The tone tends toward cynicism.

## Concept D — *THE LANTERN MOTHS OF ISK* (memory)

- **Premise:** Moths eat memories and spin them into silk lanterns. Archivist-students raise moths to preserve and to edit history.
- **Institution:** The Silk Archive on a glacier.
- **Protagonist:** Halden Rusk, a man who has deliberately fed his worst memory to a moth and cannot remember why.
- **Magic:** Memory extraction and weaving. The cost is the loss of self.
- **Mentor:** The blind Moth-Mother.
- **Central mystery:** What memory did the whole civilization agree to forget?
- **Weakness:** A strong single-book mystery, but structurally repetitive across seven books (each book uncovers another forgotten memory). Memory-erasure plots are common.

## Concept E — *THE ITINERANT COLLEGE* (moving school)

- **Premise:** A university travels across the continent on the backs of three great tortoise-like beasts. Students learn the trades of every land they cross.
- **Institution:** The Caravanserai Academy.
- **Protagonist:** Rhee Calder, a stowaway.
- **Magic:** "Custom-craft": each region's magic works only on its own soil.
- **Mentor:** The Quartermaster.
- **Creatures:** The bearer-beasts, which are dying.
- **Central mystery:** Where were the beasts originally walking to?
- **Weakness:** Magic is fragmented and loses coherence. Huge carrier-beasts invite comparison with existing works. Belonging is hard to build when the setting changes constantly.
