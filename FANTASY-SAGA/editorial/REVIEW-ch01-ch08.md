## Adversarial notes

### (a) As a demanding developmental editor
- **Part One is strong and front-loaded with craft.** Ch 1–5 is the best-made run I've seen in a draft of this kind: every scene turns something, and the chapter endings genuinely vary (dialogue, an image, mid-motion, a quiet turn). The weakest link in Part One is **Ch 6**, a four-set-piece chapter (party, Morning Survey, Ossel Fair, Benchmarking) running at the top of the length limit. Each piece is good, but the Ossel Fair spends a lot of words on comedy that Ch 7's evening scene does again. If the book needs words back, the Ossel Fair's flicker lecture and Teo's ferret mishap could lose a third without harming Sorry's introduction.
- **The lightness beat is the range's main repetition.** Ch 6 ends on how light her own mark feels, and Ch 8's night scene makes the same discovery again ("It was hers… Nobody carrying her"). I cut Ch 7's restatement. Ch 8's version earns its place only through the dramatic irony that the Morning Survey sheets *are* ghosted. Consider sharpening it so the reader feels that irony rather than a repeat, for example by having her hand pause over the memory of signing that morning's sheet.
- **Exposition on burden and ghosting** is explained in Ch 1 (the thread and Quince's trick), again in Ch 2 (the drawing of the mark), and touched again in Ch 5 and Ch 6. Each has a new angle, so I left them, but nothing further should be explained before Ch 15.
- **Ch 8 is the thinnest chapter** (3,425 words) and the most purely atmospheric. Its scenes work, but nothing in it forces a choice from Ilse. A small act of agency, such as asking Orle a second question or keeping the practice page *because* of Wil, would help the Part Two opening move.

### (b) As a skeptical adult fantasy reader
- The magic feels earned. The numbers are real, the costs are physical, and the Walk is legible because of them. The sounder breach is a genuine set piece.
- **I'd be suspicious of Hallam on sight.** He is a kind, gloved, bald, white-bearded master who blesses the Walkers, asks no hard questions, and is introduced two pages after a gossip mentions "a College gentleman in grey gloves". Many adult readers will mark him in Ch 3. The draft's defence is that Ilse herself doesn't connect the two, which is consistent with the notes, but a forger who "looked at how things were made before she looked at anything else" and has just heard "grey gloves, kid" *should* at least register it. A half-sentence in Ch 3 where she notes and dismisses it ("half the College would wear kid in a sea wind") might paradoxically disarm the reader better than silence. Your call; it touches the clue map.
- Kasimir risks being too fair too early. His Ch 5 courtesy ("I thought it was only fair") and Ch 7 reasonableness make him the book's moral centre before the book wants him to be. That works for the eventual romance but costs rivalry heat. He needs one moment of real coldness in Part One.
- Cass's dialect is consistent and never overdone. Teo's apologetic run-ons can tire on the page: there are three "sorry"s in his first speech in Ch 3 and more in Ch 4. That's keepable, but don't add more.

### (c) As a continuity and mystery specialist
- **Fixed in this pass:** the Ch 8 gloves-off leak (the most serious, because it would have spent the Ch 30 payoff in Gleaning); the Ch 6 mark set; Quince's mark; the Ch 3 Brask Walk count; the Ch 7 room layout and wardrobe; Kasimir's hair colour in Ch 1; the Marlow month in Ch 2; the forgery count in Ch 5/6; the currency; and the arithmetic of the Long Field shortfall.
- **The clue placement is correct for Ch 1–8.** Clue 1 (Ch 1), clue 2 (Ch 3 craft-only and Ch 5 frostbite), clue 3 (Ch 8 signing hand) and clue 19 (Ch 4 road-song) are all present, and none is pushed beyond the map. The Antechamber signing rule ("Master Hallam likes every sheet signed here") is planted in Ch 6 at the right volume. The Ch 8 Mess scene, where Hallam is saddened by Wil's being led out, is good dramatic irony and stays within the warmth requirement.
- **Knowledge states hold.** Ilse doesn't know Kasimir is bound for the Meridian until Ch 3, and doesn't know his mother until Ch 7. Brask knows nothing explicit by Ch 8 (she "weighed" the Long Field half-truth). Kasimir suspects her but has no proof (Ch 7). The Long Field isn't told to Kasimir (Ch 7) and only partly to Brask (Ch 8), as the tracker requires.
- **Watch beyond my range:** the stretch-geometry and folio-format conflicts (issues 1 and 2) will be visible to attentive readers because the book makes a virtue of exact numbers. Fix them in Ch 9 and Ch 15 before typesetting.
