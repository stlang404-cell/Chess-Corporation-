## The specific checks requested

### 1. Blind-ink and damp-lens mechanics (Ch 2 / Ch 9 / Ch 15)
- **Ch 2 canon:** the kit sewn into the hem is the blind (a corked bottle rolled in flannel), the damp-lens in its leather, a twist of alum, and the fine steel pen Quince ground for her. The examiner's method is to wet the sheet, set a damp lens over it, and blind ink shows.
- **Ch 9 (kept):** wet paper alone brings blind ink up faintly violet. That is how the tea exposes the line, and it is what Ilse has seen "under the damp edge of her own forgeries". This is consistent with Ch 2.
- **Ch 15 (fixed):**
  - The kit now includes the **flannel** and the **damp-lens in its leather**.
  - **Quince's ground steel pen** replaces "two steel nibs".
  - The lens is laid **flat** on the sheet, as in Ch 2 and Ch 21, instead of being held "an inch above the paper".
  - I added a three-line explanation tying the three methods together: wetting the sheet (the tea; the Court examiner in the tithe barn) shows the line but cockles the paper, so a forger uses only a damp alum film laid flat. This is the one bridge the book was missing between Ch 2's examiner, Ch 9's tea and Ch 15's lens.
  - I removed "a whisper of quicksilver" from Ch 15. Quicksilver is the Ch 17 payoff (Sorry's refusal, clue 10), and Ch 17 states it in full.
  - The seam is now Hesta's stitching from Ch 2, "eleven stitches to the inch", since resewn by Ilse. That matches both Ch 2 and Ch 14.
  - Ch 15's recall of Ch 9 now says Coyle's *blotter* took the line. The draft said it "dried away", but in Ch 9 it is blotted.
- **Quince's mark** varied between "a knot, a loop doubled back with a tail" (Ch 8), "a crooked Q with a hook" (Ch 15), "a hook through the ring" (Ch 21) and "a crooked Q with a hook caught through its ring" (Ch 17 as currently revised). Ch 15 now reads "a crooked Q, a loop doubled back on itself with its tail hooked through the ring like a fish on a line", which satisfies all four.
- **The watch-glass lens in a brass ring and the vellum scrap** are kept, because the Ch 17 editor has already aligned Ch 17 to them.

### 2. The watermark date (Thaw 1201)
This is consistent across Ch 12 (wheat-sheaf in the last week of Candlemonth 1200, bridge from Thaw 1201), Ch 15 (knots present from the first week of Thaw 1201 and absent in Candlemonth 1200; "nearly three years") and Ch 23's back-reference. Coyle's "the Master changed the supplier" is the Register-order clue (clue 6), and its misdirection ("cheaper") is intact. No change was needed beyond line edits.

### 3. Hen's progression toward the Quiet (Ch 9 seed, Ch 16 landing)
- **Ch 9:** the seed is sharpened. The draft had "Each needed one mark" and Hen offering to sign one extra sheet. That contradicted Ch 15 ("two marks at least… Hen always signed all four") and the arithmetic in Ch 16 (four marks a morning over sixty mornings is 240). Ch 9 now establishes the rule ("Each wanted two marks at least and would take four"). Hen says "I'll sign all four… I'm quickest", Teo replies "You signed all four yesterday", and Hen answers "I'm quickest every day." That makes Ch 15's "It had been a joke in Gleaning" literally true.
- **Catchphrase:** it varied between "I'm quickest" (Ch 9, Ch 17, Ch 18) and "I'm fastest" (Ch 15, Ch 16). It is now **"I'm quickest"** throughout, so the Ch 16 echo lands on the Ch 9 line.
- **Path through the range:**
  - Ch 10–12: Hen is fully alive. In Ch 12 her cold stockinged feet are pushed under Ilse's leg, which pays off in Ch 16 as the cold hand and the empty window seat.
  - Ch 15: she goes to bed after supper, "unlike her".
  - Ch 16: the landing.
- **A logic hole in Ch 16, fixed.** The draft said that because Ilse signed less, "Hen had signed the rest". Hen was already signing every sheet, so she could not pick up more. I replaced it with a law-consistent line: whatever comes down a sheet is shared among the hands that signed it, so when Ilse took her mark off a sheet, the others carried more. This is within Transfer ("partly or wholly"). **The lead editor should confirm** that this reading is acceptable.

### 4. Brask's Sallow story in Ch 14
It matches canon. The expedition was 1183, with seven members:
- Brask, as leader.
- Hallam, as second.
- **Wenna Rook**, nineteen and a grain-reader, the third survivor.
- The four who were lost: Ansel Corrow, Mira Tey, Jory Paske and Benedek Vass.

Brask lost her right eye to a hollowing, Hallam carried her twenty leagues, and the Board ruled it hallucination.

**One change:** I cut Brask's "Aurek brought his hands." Ch 26 (Brask: the gloves "started… three winters back"; he had pink hands after the Sallow) and canon (gloves since about 1200) mean that Brask knows the hands are *not* from the Sallow. Her flat endorsement in Ch 14 was a continuity error, and it also reinforced the Ch 5 misdirection in Brask's own voice. Her evasive "It was cold enough out there" now stands alone. It is truthful, in character, and does not exceed the clue map.

## Adversarial notes

### (a) As a demanding developmental editor
- **Ch 9** does its job efficiently, but it has four scenes and three "warmth" beats for Hallam: the lecture joke, the ossel anecdote, and the copying offer with its praise. That is deliberate for the Ch 25 turn, but the uncle-touch on the shoulder at the close tips toward over-insistence. I would let one of the anecdotes go in a later pass.
- **Ch 10** is the best-shaped chapter in the range. Each scene changes Ilse's relationship to error, and the closing "plus or minus" lands.
- **Ch 11** is a superb set piece. The risk is numerical density in the middle. I would protect it; it is the book's proof that numbers make the magic.
- **Ch 12** is four loosely joined errands: copying, the door, the letter, and the night class. The watermark and the Unheld Stacks door each earn their place, and the letter is the emotional spine. The wayhorn night class is lovely but is mostly atmosphere and one Brask line. If the book needs words back, take them from there.
- **Ch 13–14** form a strong pair. Ch 14's "trust has no lining" coda is slightly long, but it is the thematic hinge for the confession in Ch 17.
- **Ch 15** carries a heavy exposition load: the kit inventory, the lens method, and how ghosting works. I trimmed what I could. The Kasimir scene is the payoff and works.
- **Ch 16** is the emotional peak of Part Two and earns its last line. The night walk restates the decision calculus a little more than needed ("She could go to Brask… She could go to Master Hallam… She could say nothing…"). It is the outline's beat, but a reader might feel three paragraphs where two would do.

### (b) As a skeptical adult fantasy reader
- I believe the magic in this range, because it costs something and has rules I can check. The Ladder's parallelogram law and the Brine End misclosure are exactly the kind of thing that sells it.
- **Where I would roll my eyes:** Ilse "files away" suspicious details three times ("the long untidy cupboard at the back of her mind" in Ch 12, Ch 15 and Ch 16, plus a "drawer" in Ch 19). It is a motif, but the fourth use reads as the author holding the plot back. Consider cutting the Ch 16 instance.
- **Ch 15's blind spot.** Ilse sees "Thaw 1201" in both the watermark and the knots, and fails to connect them only because the thought "snags". A sharp reader will connect them before she does. That is acceptable for dramatic irony, but she is meant to be exact.
- **Ch 16's guilt.** It is affecting, but Ilse's choice to sign *less* while knowing what the knots do edges toward making her unsympathetic. The new burden-sharing line makes the consequence concrete, which helps the reader understand it. It may also make her look worse. That is defensible, since this is the low point of her arc, but the lead editor should be aware of it.

The tracker gives "~Ch 16" for her suspecting that ghosting causes the Quiet.
