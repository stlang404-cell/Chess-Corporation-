## Checks requested by the lead editor

**1. Off-register signing: now described consistently.** The canonical wording, from Ch 17, is "a sixteenth of an inch high of the rule, turned a little sunwise". Ch 22 ("a sixteenth high and a bit sunwise"), Ch 23 ("signing skew") and Ch 25 already agree with it.

What I fixed was the *mechanism*, which Ch 17 described differently from Ch 15 and Ch 24.
- Ch 17's original line said a ghost-line ran "beneath the place where a student set their mark … stroke on stroke. When you signed, you traced it." That is Quince's hand-ghosting, not a printed plate. It also cannot work under a hundred different marks.
- It now reads: "a knot of blind ink printed on the rule of every signing box … When you signed on the rule, as you were taught, you rode it." This matches Ch 15 (an identical printed knot under the attestation rule) and makes the skew logical: a mark set off the rule crosses the knot "at a point or two".
- Ch 17's evening check said the line ran "in from the left edge toward the binding". It now reads "The violet knot came up fine as a hair on the rule, its thread running off toward the binding", matching Ch 15 and Ch 24.
- Ch 22: Teo's old, still-pulling sheets were dated "before Frost 4". The skew began at First Bell on Frost 3 (Ch 17), so this is now "before Frost 3".
- Ch 24: the raking-light knot was "where a fine pen had laid down blind ink". It is now "where a plate had pressed blind ink into it", because Ch 15 establishes that the knots are printed.

**2. Quince's mark (a hook through a ring): harmonized.**
- Ch 17 now reads: "a crooked, old-fashioned Q, its tail doubled back and hooked through its own ring."
- Ch 21's "the hook through the ring" is kept.
- Both are compatible with revised Ch 8 ("a crooked, old-fashioned loop with a hook caught through it") and with current Ch 15 ("a crooked Q, a loop doubled back on itself with its tail hooked through the ring").

I also aligned Ch 17's kit with Ch 2 and the current Ch 15:
- a bottle the size of her thumb;
- the watch-glass damp-lens in its brass ring (not "a square of glass bound in rag");
- chamois;
- Quince's mark on *vellum* (not oiled silk);
- "the fine steel pen Quince had ground for her the year before he died" (Ch 2), replacing "a nib she had ground down herself". Ch 21's "her own ground-down nib" is changed to match.

*Warning:* Ch 15 was being revised by another editor while I worked, and its kit wording changed twice. My Ch 17 matches the version current at the end of my pass. If Ch 15 changes again, re-check Ch 17 line 14.

**3. The Moot (Ch 20): it obeys Precedence, Fidelity and Latitude.** The rules were already sound.
- **Latitude:** "any position within its latitude … about a yard". You may not chart the stone on end or out of the bed.
- **Precedence:** attestation is simultaneous, so age cannot decide, and "the record of greater fidelity prevails, and age prevails only between equals".
- **Release:** release before each bout is done by the winner's own hand on the original sheet.
- **The Cass/Piety bout** shows the subtlety correctly: Cass's lean was right but her measurements were sloppy, so Piety's more accurate standing claim won.

There was one gap. The text never said *why* Ilse's chart, which moves the stone, has greater fidelity than Kasimir's perfect record of where it lies. I added three sentences to her reasoning: "the Law of Fidelity did not ask how true a charter was to the last sheet. It asked how true it was to the land. Somewhere inside the stone's yard of latitude was the place the ground would choose for itself. Chart that, true to a hair, and there would be nothing in the bed to pull against it." This makes the win a deduction from the Laws rather than a feeling. It stops short of Ch 29's general open-map insight.

I also fixed a quote-mark error: Calder's speech ran across two paragraphs, and the first one was wrongly closed.

**4. *Saffery v. the Parish of Wick* (1193): consistent, with one fix.**
- The details agree across Ch 1, 7, 20 and 21: Fask re-charted the parish in autumn 1192, when Ilse was 12; the appeal was in 1193, when she was 13; "newer … but better made"; her father died of fever in 1195; the savings were 44 marks, sixteen short of the 60.
- The problem was the case title. Ch 21 had Kasimir say "The parish appealed … and lost", while Ilse said "He appealed. He spent every mark we had." It now reads: "He appealed. The vestry lent the appeal its name and not a penny more, so he spent every mark we had on it. Your Court heard it in Tessary and found for Saffery." This explains the title and reconciles both lines.
- Ch 21 also put the parish chest and clerk "at Hobbet", while the same chapter says "the parish chest of Wick Fallow" and Ch 2 puts the chest in the chapel at Wick. Both are changed to Wick. In Ch 18, "the Hobbet parish clerk" is now "Jem Rudd, the parish clerk".

**5. The Steyne red herring (Ch 23): Hallam is no longer made obvious.** The risk was the stores-ledger entry "Approved: A. Hallam, Mag. Reg." (a writer's addition, not in the clue map). Together with Coyle's visible unease, Ch 19's grey hands and Ch 13's inspection, it pointed straight at Hallam two chapters early. Changes:
- After the entry, added: "The same approval stood under every other entry on the page, down to the gall and the tape. The Master of the Register signed for the Register's string." The clue stays fair but reads as routine.
- Ilse's case against Steyne now includes "that his office had cut the money the winter the paper changed". This ties his audit to the paper and strengthens the misdirection.
- Teo's "drew a ghost-line under every mark" contradicted Ch 15 (the lines are printed). It is now "cut a plate that puts a ghost-line under every mark … Not one slip". His "patient and good with a pen" argument stays.
- The chapter's last line was "thinking about patience, and about pens", which edged toward a master draftsman. It now ends "…and about pens, and about who could afford to hire both". That echoes Cass's "bearing for hire" and keeps Steyne in frame.
- In Ch 24, Ilse's theft reasoning had a garbled line ("Coyle had seen her in the Seedtime stacks…"). It now reads "Coyle had found her with the stores ledger open, and the Provost had known about it within a day", so her suspicion points at Coyle and the Provost, as the Ch 25 reveal needs.

**6. The Ch 24 tea scene: it reads as warm.** He is warm because the warmth is specific: the seed-cake from "my sister's girl", "Mind the step … it has never once moved", sitting an hour with Hen ("The politeness. One would almost rather they wept"), his offer to speak for her hand, the story of Brask crossing the bog, and steadying her elbow at the step. I changed little:
- "Magister" became "Master Hallam" (Ilse's usage elsewhere).
- "drawn a hundred of them" became "dozens" (Ch 29 says about forty ghostings).
- Ilse's protective lie now covers Orle's ledger, not Orla Devane, who knows nothing. This also makes sense of Hallam's next question, "Not Dr. Orle?"

His questions (told anyone? Mr. Reyne? where is the sheet?) still read as care. Cass's "Big House looks after Big House" now uses "Marrow", which is Cass's habit.

**7. The Ch 21 → Ch 22 join (two drafters): smoothed.**
- Ch 22 now opens "On Frost 22 the crow found her…", which places it after Ch 21's Frost 14.
- Brask had been barred for "eight days". She was barred at sundown on Frost 13, so this is now nine days. Kasimir's "eight days" of not looking at her (since Frost 14) is kept.
- **Hen's bed.** Ch 17 and Ch 21 have it "stripped to the ticking", but Ch 22 had it "made up tight and square". It now reads: "Someone, probably Teo, had made it up again over the bare ticking…"
- **Teo's "Marrow".** Ch 22 said Teo "only called her that when he was worried", but Teo calls her Marrow throughout Ch 17–21. It now reads: "He said it the way he said it at the bottom of a column that would not add."
- **Ilse's burden.** Ch 22 said she "now carried, honestly, herself" the ache she once sent down Quince's thread. That is not true: her old forgeries still run to Quince. It now reads: "The Brine End charter and the Moot-stone she carried honestly, herself."
- **Wil Ashdown.** Ch 22 had Wil "on his bench". Ch 18 has him in a chair by the window, so it is now "in his chair by the window", and Teo's echo line matches.
- Register and diction now match on both sides of the join: the "Recorded" ritual set up by Ch 17's "I'd like it recorded", Cass's "Road and rain", and Hallam's "my dear".

## Unresolved issues for the lead editor

1. **The witness-mark set conflicts between editors.** The Ch 1–8 review aligned Ch 6 to the *later* set: Hen's gull's wing, Teo's pothook T, Cass's zigzag and Kasimir's R. During my pass, the Ch 9–16 editor changed Ch 15 to the *old* set: Hen's "wave", Teo's "ringed dot, his pie seen from above", Cass's "three slanting strokes". Ch 15 line 98 still says "gull-wing", so Ch 15 is now inconsistent with itself. My range (Ch 24: pothook T, zigzag, gull-wing) and Ch 25 ("the ghost knot under his own R") follow the current Ch 6. Please settle one set book-wide.
2. **Forgery count.** Ch 17 has "thirty-four charters in nine years", while Ch 5/6/31 have 31 wren's-foot charters. I left Ch 17 alone, since it is compatible only if three charters predate 1200. Ch 10's "twenty other small corrections across three parishes" (about 23) also conflicts and belongs to the Ch 9–16 range.
3. **Ch 25 (outside my range):** Mother Nettle is described as "a heap of shawls with a face … like a walnut". This duplicates Ch 19's Nan Ottery: "a heap of washing", "a face like a walnut". Ch 19 has priority, so Ch 25 should change. Ch 19 itself gives Mother Nettle "a bundle of kindling tied up in a shawl".
4. **Brask's cottage.** Ch 14 puts it on Netmakers' Row, by the old harbour, with a blue door. Ch 26 puts it "last on the cliff lane, whitewashed, lobster pots". This is outside my range, but Ch 21–22 send her home there.
5. **Ch 24's Frost 1 folio "carried four marks, one in each printed box".** Ch 16 has Hen signing the first round and Ilse signing the remaining three sheets herself on Frost 1, and the Ch 9 vs Ch 15 sheet arrangements already disagree (see the Ch 1–8 review, point 2). This is low-visibility, so I left it.
6. **Ch 24's "the line from Hen's gull-wing … was deeper. Hen had signed extra."** On a single sheet, each mark is signed once, so a physically deeper bruise doesn't follow. It reads as Ilse's emotional inference, so I kept it, but a literal-minded reader may object.
7. **Seed-cake and caraway** appear twice: Teo's in Ch 18 and Hallam's in Ch 24. This could be deliberate (Hallam out-kinding the party), but if not, change one.

## Adversarial notes

### (a) As a demanding developmental editor
- **Part Three has a clear spine and every chapter turns something:** confession and the trick (17); an ally and a suspicion (18); the moving map and the first refused clue (19); public victory (20); ruin (21); absorption into the community and rising stakes (22); the false trail (23); misplaced trust and the theft (24). This is the strongest stretch of the draft.
- **Hen's flatness is played four times:** Ch 16 ("tide goes out twice a day"), Ch 17 ("hello, Ilse"), Ch 18 ("It's cake") and Ch 23 ("It was a pie"). Each adds a degree of grey, but the joke shape (food, flat noun) repeats. If anything is cut in a later pass, cut the pie exchange in Ch 23 and keep the chain ("Twenty … That's a fifth"), which is new and better.
- **Ch 18's middle scene (Cass and Steyne) happens offstage behind a door.** That fits close-third, but it is the second time Cass's suspicion is reported rather than dramatized. Ch 23 then gives Steyne a full on-page scene with Ilse. The pairing works, but Steyne's menace is spent mostly in Ch 23.
- **Ch 21 carries three big scenes** (Board, Brask's packing, the snow fight). The Brask scene is the best writing in the range. The fight leans on Kasimir's speech, which is long but earns its length.

### (b) As a skeptical adult fantasy reader
- **Hallam is hard to miss for genre-savvy readers.** Ch 19's grey hands come with explicit mental cross-references (gloves the colour of a dove's breast, *old Sallow frostbite*), and Ilse's refusal is very knowing ("She knew how not to take a measurement"). Ch 23 previously added his signature on the paper order; I neutralized that. The reader is likely to be one step ahead of Ilse from Ch 19. The draft turns that into dramatic irony rather than surprise, and the warmth of Ch 24 makes the irony painful rather than smug. I think that's the right bet, but the lead editor should know the twist is a "when", not a "whether", for many readers.
- **Steyne is a credible villain for Ilse but less so for the reader.** The narration notes his pink, unmarked hands ("Hands that had not signed much of anything"). That is fair play, and it quietly tells the attentive reader he isn't the root. I kept it, because fair play is worth more than a few extra pages of doubt.
- **The Moot is now airtight on its own Laws,** and the creep-rate annotation is a satisfying plant. The off-register trick is clever and properly costly (the wobbly stair, the broken promise, Teo's old sheets).
- **Sorry's heron** is the best single image in the range, and the restraint ("Nobody had said *heron*") is right.
