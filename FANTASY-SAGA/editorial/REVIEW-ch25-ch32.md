## The two joins

### Ch 25 → Ch 26 (writer A → writer B)
- **Notebook staging.** Ch 26 said Kasimir opened his notebook “on a perch-board spattered with crow-lime”. In Ch 25 he had it on his knee, sitting on a crate in the red light. Fixed.
- **Brine End void date.** Ch 26 had “Fogmonth 10”; Ch 25 and Ch 13 give Fogmonth 9. Fixed.
- **Warrant sources.** Ch 26 said anyone might read the bursar’s daybook “because nobody had ever thought to forbid it”. That contradicts Ch 25, where Orla distracted the clerk so Kasimir could read it upside down and he used his assessor’s commission for the posting-house ledgers. Rewritten to match Ch 25.
- **Ghost-line on the sheet.** Ch 26 had the line “coming in from the left edge” and going “off the right edge”. Ch 24 and Ch 25 establish a knot under each mark, a line running under the rule toward the binding, and a gathering in the margin. Ch 26 now describes it the same way.
- **Quiet case list.** “Two in the second year” didn’t match Orle’s ledger, which has two more cases in 1201 and four in 1202. It now reads “Two more that year, four the next.”
- **Brask’s cottage.** Ch 26 placed it as “the last on the cliff lane, low and whitewashed”. Ch 14 (binding) puts it on Netmakers’ Row near the old harbour, with a blue door and a plane-table board for a table. Fixed.
- **Equinox folio tenure.** Brask said Hallam had prepared the Equinox folio “for thirty-odd years”, but he has been Master of the Register only since 1189. It now reads “every year since he took the Register.”
- **Location of “every map is an act of love”.** Ch 26 put the line “in the Antechamber”; Ch 24 has it by his study fire. Fixed.
- The “four sentences in Brask’s shed” flag from the Ch 22–25 notes was already resolved in the text.

### Ch 29 → Ch 30 (writer B → writer C): a real contradiction, now fixed
**Problem.**
- Ch 29 has the merged party arriving at the Gate at 5:20 a.m. with the Wing already drawn open. It says “seven marks” are on the last sheet, and Calder hands the charter to Hallam.
- Ch 30 opened at **4:20 a.m.** with Ilse and Kasimir still finishing the open Wing charter indoors, an hour before they had arrived.
- Ch 30 also had Ilse sign “under the seven marks of the merged party”, but she is one of the seven.
- Worse, if the open Wing charter had been attested at 5:20, then by Precedence Hallam’s folio could never have taken at dawn, and Ch 30’s sequence would be impossible.

**Fix (no change to plot events):**
- **Ch 29.** The seven marks now sit at the foot of the *route*. The Wing sheet is left unsigned “as every party did: by the articles the Wing was the College’s to attest, under the Gate, at the Equinox.” Ilse asks Kasimir, at the Gate end of the causeway, to draw the Wing twice, and he does without asking why. This gives Ilse forethought and agency.
- **Ch 30 opening.** It now takes place at half past five in the Hall of Instruments, with the *second* Wing sheet. “The first sheet was in Hallam’s gloved hands. This one was theirs.”
- **Ch 30 signing.** At the ceremony Ilse attests that second sheet *alone*, on a sheet nobody has yet signed. She knowingly breaks custom (“The Wing was the College’s to attest. She would do it alone.”).
- **Ch 31.** The noon co-signing now reads “three hundred marks under her wren’s foot” instead of “under the merged party’s seven”.

**Other end-state continuity, Ch 29 to Ch 30:**
- **Absalom.** Ch 30 had him walking “with Absalom’s arm over her shoulder”, but Ch 29 has him on a litter with a broken leg. Now “one pole of Absalom’s litter on her shoulder”.
- **Teo’s grey.** Ch 30 said it “had crept past the first knuckle in the night”, but Ch 27 already had it at the second knuckle. Now “had not gone back a hair.”
- **Kasimir’s right hand,** burdened in the fold, is now shown still wrapped in his scarf. **Ilse’s rope-burned fingers** from the belay are now on the pen.
- **Sorry** is asleep at the end of Ch 29. In Ch 30 it wakes early, which the Ch 30 notes record as deliberate. I kept it.
- **What held the Wing overnight.** Ch 30 said the Wing “had held all night on the last of last year’s charter”, which ignores the daily Morning Survey. Now “on yesterday’s Survey and the last of last year’s Equinox Charter.”

## Magic logic: open charter and Conduit (checked against Ledger A.8 and MAGIC-SYSTEM §9)

**Ch 29 deduction.** Already sound. It is built on Fidelity’s “accuracy *and completeness*”, Latitude as a range of states, then Precedence and Burden, so it is a deduction rather than a new power. The weak point was the ghost-proofing (A.8b): the bounds of an open charter are still ink lines on paper, so why can’t a ghost register to them? I rewrote that passage:
- A ghost must keep register at *both ends*: under the drawing, and through the Conduit with the fixed Zero lines. It “carried weight from one *here* to another *here*.”
- An open charter has no *here*. Its lines mark where the ground cannot go, and its hold lies in the range between them, “and no Zero line in the world described a range.”

Also corrected in Ch 29:
- “perhaps forty” Quince ghostings is now “thirty-odd”, to match Ch 31’s 31 forgeries.
- The skew “spared Teo his last few weeks of slivers” is now “every sliver since Frost”.

**Ch 30 supersession (A.8c).** The writer already applied Precedence correctly: fidelity is asked first, age only breaks ties. I tightened two points:
- *Why the ghost lets go:* “a drawing bound nothing. A ghost rode an attestation”, so a superseded folio has no live hold for the ghost to run along.
- *Why Ilse must act before Lisbet signs:* she does not know how much a ghost can pour into a hand at the moment of signing.
- Hallam’s line “Nothing is under it any longer” was confusing, because the violet lines are visibly under it. It is now “It holds nothing any longer.”

**Ch 30 tearing (A.8e).** I added the two missing links:
- “A ghost lived by registration, and a torn sheet lay in register with nothing.”
- The burden returning to Hallam is not punishment by magic but restitution: “The weight had been his before it was theirs. He had tied his own mark into the Zero Sheets, and the Conduit had only lent it out.” This matches A.2–A.3.

**Ch 31 noon co-signing (A.8b in practice).** Coyle rakes the open charter by Calder’s order and finds it clean, and Ilse notes it would not have mattered: “Nothing in blind ink could keep register with a range.”

**Ch 31 release rule (original sheet plus own hand).** It was already obeyed: the sheets were gathered by Court writ, and Ilse unsigns each one with her own pen. I made both halves explicit:
- Calder: “the sheets themselves and not copies.”
- Ilse: “The original and the hand, Magistra. That’s all release asks. It doesn’t ask me to be standing in the ditch.”

The second line also covers an apparent clash with Ledger A.5, which says Hallam had to be present to “release” parish charters. That was redaction of *other people’s* charters, which needs the land; unsigning your own mark needs only the sheet and your hand. The two missing forgeries (Fennock and the Marlow road gate) correctly stay unreleased until their sheets are found.

### On the final line
The final line is earned, and now more explicitly:
1. It closes the structural echo of Ch 1’s first line (“The hedge moved at a quarter past two in the morning, and it did not want to.”). The forced, unwilling move of a forgery becomes the free, willing move of a true open record.
2. Its timing is planted. Cass’s “Tide’s on the turn” at the Morning Survey and the open charter’s “out on the ebb” mean the quarter-past-six move is the tide turning, not an arbitrary clock time.
3. “And it wanted to” is set up by Ch 30: “the level of a thing that was allowed to move.”
4. “And so did she” needed a beat showing Ilse’s *own* will to move, beyond the general “next year we find out”. I replaced the “did not sleep for a long time” line, which also used one of the capped phrases. Ilse now reads Brask’s Sallow field-book on the roof by the stair-head lamp “until the stars went pale, and she did not want to stop.” That plants her desire to go, without stating it, two scenes before the last line.

## Unresolved issues for the lead editor
1. **Knowledge tracker gap.** CHARACTER-KNOWLEDGE says Brask learns that the open charter defeats ghosting “by crow” in Ch 29. No chapter shows it. Either add a line to Ch 31 or Ch 32 (for example, Brask has already read Halloway’s paper) or amend the tracker.
2. **Kasimir’s burdened right hand.** It is pewter-grey from the ten torn charters in Ch 29. That is his own burden, not ghosted, so tearing the Conduit would not clear it. Ch 31 and Ch 32 never mention it. I suggest one line in Ch 32 (the chain catch): grey fading at the knuckles, or not.
3. **Brask’s Ch 32 line** “Not Halloway. Not Calder. Not Damask. Not me.” is close to the restricted “Not X. Not Y.” pattern. I left it because it is a list of names, not the tic, but check the book-wide count.
4. **Ledger A.5 wording.** It says Hallam needed to be present because “the release must be done near the land”. The text now distinguishes redacting others’ charters (needs proximity) from unsigning your own (needs only the sheet and your hand). I recommend amending A.5 to say “redaction”, so the bible matches the book.
5. **The Hall of Instruments** is both Ilse’s pre-dawn workroom (Ch 30) and the tribunal room (Ch 31). That is fine, but someone might want a different room in Ch 30.
6. **Hallam calls her “Ilse”** once in Ch 30 (“The centre is failing, Ilse.”). I kept it as the mask dropping, but it slightly dilutes Kasimir’s single “Ilse” in Ch 26.
7. **Notes files** for Ch 29 and Ch 30 should be updated to the new end state: Wing sheet drawn twice and left unsigned; Ilse attests the second copy alone at the Gate. I did not touch the notes, per the brief.

## Adversarial notes

### (a) As a demanding developmental editor
- **Part Four repeats one emotional beat too often:** “the kindness was real *and* he did this.” Ilse arrives at it in Ch 25 (“Two true things”), Brask restates it in Ch 26, Ilse again in Ch 30 (“There was no cruelty in him”), and Ilse says it aloud to the benchmark in Ch 32. I cut the verbal tics, but the beat still plays four times. Ch 30 and Ch 32 earn theirs. Ch 26’s version (“He believed every word”) could go entirely if space is needed.
- **Ch 27 has three scenes plus a coda**: Coyle, the ethics argument, Hen and the Lantern Count. It is the most diffuse chapter in the range. The Hen visit is lovely and justifies Cass leading in Ch 28, so I kept it.
- **Ch 30 runs at the 3,950 ceiling** and could lose a few hundred words without harm. Hallam’s first speech and Ilse’s confession are slightly over-explained, partly because the reader already knows everything she says. A bolder cut would let Ilse’s accusation partly happen in summary, saving the full voice for “I know it because I am one.”
- **Agency.** Ilse’s agency is now stronger at the key moment: she plans the second sheet and breaks custom to attest alone. The Conduit retrieval, however, happens entirely offstage; it has to, because of POV. Ch 31’s roof scene with Cass recovers some of it.

### (b) As a skeptical adult fantasy reader
- **Why not just attest the open Wing at 5:20 and skip the drama?** The revised Ch 29 answers this: by the articles the Wing is the College’s to attest at the Equinox. But a sharp reader may still ask why Ilse doesn’t break custom then, privately, before Hallam can sign. The best unstated answer is that she needs the folio exposed in public and the Conduit in hand. One clause in Ch 30 (“She needed him to sign it where they could all see”) would close the gap; I did not have the words to spare.
- **The ghost-proofing deduction** now holds up to scrutiny; the register-at-both-ends argument is the strongest version available inside the Laws. Readers will accept the noon co-signing as safe.
- **Hallam’s stilling** is magically clean (restitution of his own burden) and emotionally earned. The gloves splitting “like the skin of a ripe plum” is the book’s best payoff image.
- **The Board verdict** (*Errata v. Pell*, “the use the College most needs”) will strike some readers as convenient. The restitution scene, with the Tull release as the costliest act, does most of the persuading, and it works.
