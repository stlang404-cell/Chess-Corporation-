# Notes — Chapters 2–6

## Chapter 2 — "The Assessor's Notebook" (Reaping 15–17) — 3,887 words

**Summary.** Continuous from Ch 1. At the hedge Kasimir introduces himself: junior assessor attached to the Concordance inspection of the Fallowmarch voids, under Assessor Pollard. He takes Ilse's account of the Little Wick lane, and she declines to admit she felt "a pressure" (which would reveal that she is levelled). He shows her the root-tear (41 yards 9 inches, rootlets broken, dew on top of the turned soil), notes two rods is "not a number the weather chooses", and smells oak-gall on her. She covers that with the mourning-dye story. She notes his ruined Tessary boots and gives her real name because a lie would be checkable. Next morning (Friday, Reaping 16), delivering the vestry linen, she overhears Kasimir and a bored Pollard at the parish chest. Kasimir calls the Tull amendment contrary charting and requests a drawing of the mark; Pollard grumbles, signs, and says three weeks at least. Kasimir also checks the Little Wick lane charter (looked at Tuesday by "a College gentleman"): "It is in order, and the lane is not." He seals the chest with Concordance wax, which closes off her options of burning or releasing the sheet, then greets her through the door. That night Hesta says "You could run". Ilse surveys her options "station by station" and remembers Quince on collegiate privilege and the Walk. She spends 38 marks of the Long Field money on the express mail and leaves 6 marks 6 in the tin. Hesta gives her Daun's good coat, and they sew her forger's kit into its hem. At dawn on Reaping 17 Ilse rides the mail roof. Kasimir, packing at the Bell's window with a post-chaise waiting, sees her and her bag and the TESSARY — VERITY CROSS board. The chapter ends on the Fallowmarch hedgerows, each one a line someone else wrote.

**State at end.** Ilse is on the westbound mail with her surveyor's bag (chain, levelglass, sightvane, Quince's folding rule, inks). Sewn into the hem of her father's brown broadcloth coat are the blind (corked bottle in flannel), the damp-lens in leather, a twist of alum and Quince's ground steel pen. She has the 6 marks 6 Hesta secretly slipped back in beside the bread and cheese (Ilse finds it at Tessary). The Tull amendment is sealed in the Wick parish chest pending a Court drawing of the mark. Kasimir has seen her wren's foot on the Tull sheet and has her name. Ilse knows his name and post, but not that he is bound for the Meridian, nor that he is the Chancellor's son.

**Invented details.** Jem Rudd (parish clerk; "Jem" from Ch 1). The Ninefold chapel at Wick and its vicar (the vicar's late uncle once held a Patent). The Bell (Hobbet coaching inn). Old Pettifer's funeral (a cover story, though true). The Statute of Small Holdings, 1061 (a freeholder may survey her own holding). Hobb, a Fennock wheelwright and ditch-forger. Ilse saw him "drawn" at 16 and given nine years in the Tessary gaol. Drawing of the mark as a procedure: wet the sheet, set a damp lens over the signature, and follow the thread to whoever carries the burden. The Hare and Lantern (Quince's pub). Cousin Barnaby, a canal-warden in Lisk. The Gethins' ditch, the chapel glebe and Widow Ames's goat-run (Ilse's earlier forgeries). Lady Muck: Quince's term for the Court's head. The express mail is maroon and black with a crowned horn and runs day and night. Hesta's laundress stitches run eleven to the inch.

**Deviations.** The outline says the Walk is "seven days away". The scene is set on the night of Reaping 16, so Ilse says "the twenty-second; today's the sixteenth". The lane charter in the parish chest is deliberately *in order* rather than missing, to match the Ch 13 pattern (the parish copy is fine and the Register master is gone) and to avoid over-planting. Pollard blames "the Margin wagons". The line about Hesta's returned 6 marks 6 was added to the file by another hand during drafting. I kept it, changed "she said" to "Hesta said" for clarity, and changed the distance to Tessary to 250 miles (the original said 500, which is closer to the distance to the Cape).

## Chapter 3 — "Cape Verity" (Reaping 17–22) — 3,895 words

**Summary.** The journey comes in fragments. Two days and two nights of Eastern Post lanes to Tessary. The hedge-preacher Brother Amos swears on the Atlas at everything. Ilse's level deepens toward the centre to a smug bass note. At dawn on the third day she glimpses Tessary's green copper roofs and the Zero Stone: a plain obelisk about three men tall, darker than the stone around it. Then comes the ruled-straight Great Meridian Road west. The land "thins" and the levelglass bead wanders at Garrow. On the last day the Road skirts the Margin for forty miles, and every milestone has a freshly re-cut benchmark arrow; Amos says the College men re-sign the Road every few weeks now. At dusk on Reaping 21 she sees the sea and the Meridian: the honey-stone Held Side, the steady lantern on the Gate, and the Drift Wing curving out over the heath like a sleeping animal, lit windows all along it. One window slides a few feet along the dark. At the Chain & Anchor she overhears students talk about Walk odds, Brask taking the Walk, a chains party's turned stair ("Lost your stair, chains!") and a door opening on gorse. She sleeps badly and watches the lantern from the window. Before dawn there are 19 Walkers at the Gate under WE RECORD WHAT IS. We meet Oskar Fell (silent quarryman), Yara Penhallow (cheerful dairy farmer's daughter), Teo (treads on her foot, carries his mother's eel pie "for the road, or for my wake") and Cass (braids and route-cords, sling-stone, "paper-walls"). Morning Survey parties pass through the Gate counting. Brask arrives from the Drift side and gives the briefing and the rules ("Don't die. It's paperwork."; "Food is not an instrument"; don't follow voices or shortcuts). Hallam blesses the Walkers warmly, with a Ninefold quotation; Ilse notes the fine stitching of his grey kid gloves. In the gallery, Kasimir, now in a student's black coat and iron ring, understands why she is there and gives her a duellist's nod. Brask sees it: "Friend of yours, Walker?" "No." "Good. Friends are heavy." They walk out into the fog.

**State at end.** Ilse is leaving the Gate with Teo and Cass beside her. She has about 4 marks 6 left (after 2 marks for the room). She now knows Kasimir is a Member and has guessed her plan. She has seen Brask's right-eye patch, grey left hand and the Long Lie, and has met Hallam (no connection made to the Ch 1 "grey gloves"; the gloves are noted only as fine craft).

**Invented details.** Brother Amos (hedge-preacher). Coldharbour and Garrow (stages on the route). The Eastern Post road (Hobbet to Tessary). The Great Meridian Road begins at the Zero Stone. The Chain & Anchor sign (a chain coiled round an anchor); a room there costs 2 marks. Debenham (a loud student). Hollis's lot (a chains party that lost its stair). The Gannet Stair (another party's stretch). The Gate's lettering is "as tall as a child" and the arch is about 60 feet high. A gallery is cut into the inner face of the arch. A sour clerk takes the Walkers' names. The boy with the new chain still has the chandler's tag on it. Yara is from a dairy farm at Holm, beyond Tessary, with a bull called Hector. Oskar Fell is gaunt, about 30, in a quarryman's jacket. Hallam's quotation: "*we do not ask the ground to love us, only to know us.*" Hallam's gown is dark green. Brask calls the Long Lie "the only honest thing in the building, and it lies, because it's held."

**Deviations.** None of substance. The Hallam glove detail is kept to craftsmanship so that the Ch 1 clue is not reinforced beyond the ledger.

## Chapter 4 — "The Walk" (Reaping 22) — 3,805 words

**Summary.** The Margin moves Ilse's chaining-pin: her back-sight is out by a degree and a half. Her solo chaining (peg, walk out, pin, walk back) is perfect but too slow. She works out a method of short legs, near-simultaneous fore- and back-sights, a closing triangle every second station, and the minute written beside each station. The heath breathes, rising about an inch and sinking on a long slow cycle. Cass lopes past without chaining and says "Ground's leaning"; everything slides southwest "like a plate on a tilted table". Ilse feels the lean faintly, chains along it, and gets a perfect back-sight. Near noon Teo is frozen at a salt creek that keeps widening (12 links to 30). Ilse makes him calculate. He finds it widens in "breaths" of 41 seconds and closes about 3 links on each fall. She plans a two-ended chain crossing that both of them witness ("We're all Walkers. A chain's got two ends for a reason."). Cass mocks them, then points them upstream to where the banks are *narrowing*. They cross at 11 links 9, and the three go on together. In fog, a song comes up through their feet. A sounder breaches from a whaleback hill 200 yards off: turf peels away, the thorn trees topple off a grey back, and an eye the size of a cartwheel, ringed pale, turns and looks at them. Then the hill closes like water and ripples lift them a foot at a time. Ilse's level goes silent. Cass kneels and blesses it ("Go easy, old one"). Teo weeps (his grandfather saw one). Ilse realizes she drew the hill as though it would stay. Yara's voice calls from the fog about a dry shortcut round the mere. Ilse shouts at her to wait, and there is nothing. At dusk they reach the cairn, where a cairn-crow is building its own pebble cairn. Ilse closes 171 stations with a 9-link error. Over Teo's pie, cut into twelfths, Cass sings the Corrow road-song, and Ilse dismisses the idea of a map that moves. They talk about why each came. Ilse: "I wanted to be a surveyor." Cass: "Liar. Nice one, though." Ilse lies awake listening to the ground breathe.

**State at end.** The three are camped at the Walk cairn, with other Walkers' fires nearby. Yara is lost near the mere. Ilse's board holds 171 stations at ten chains to the inch, closed to 9 links. The pie is down to three twelfths. Ilse has a sense of the "lean" (grain), though no word for it yet.

**Invented details.** The ten chaining-arrows (pins) count as part of the chain. The benchmark post sits at the foot of the headland steps. Ilse's scale is ten chains to the inch. Teo carries a watch. The cairn plate reads MERIDIAN — WALK CAIRN — HELD. The extra verses of the Corrow road-song add the white thorn and the crow. Teo's mother makes forty eel pies on a market day. Cass's blessing is in a language Ilse doesn't know.

**Deviations.** None. The Law of Witness is honoured by having Teo and Ilse both hold and read the chain on shared legs, and by each keeping their own board.

## Chapter 5 — "A True Survey" (Reaping 23) — 3,787 words

**Summary.** At dawn the land has walked in the night: the mere lies further west, the sounder hill is on the wrong side, and the creek is gone. Only the cairn is held; the levelglass bead sits dead centre on it. They must re-survey about 111 stations of the return. At a quarter past two, at a peaty brook, the triangle won't close: the bank is 14 links out, re-chained three times, and Cass says the ground is "leaning both ways here". Teo boxes and annotates the misclose. Ilse, exhausted and proud of the best sheet she has ever drawn, scrapes the bank-line off with her knife and redraws it clean and continuous ("a good lie is ninety-nine parts truth"). To her it feels like making the sheet tell the truth. She tells Teo "I've drawn it as near as may be". They reach the Gate at four o'clock to a ragged cheer. Hallam greets them warmly (they make nine of nineteen), and when Teo asks about his gloves: "Old Sallow frostbite, my boy." Ilse attests under the arch. She chooses *not* to use the wren's foot or ghost Quince beneath it, and signs plain "I. Marrow". She feels the thread run out to the cairn and catch at the stream "like a stitch on a splinter". In the Hall of Instruments Brask passes Teo narrowly ("Your lines wander, Lisk. Your numbers don't.") and Cass ("True. Hideous. True."). On Ilse's sheet she stops at the stream, lays Teo's sheet edge to edge beside it, looks for a long time, and says "True enough." Eleven pass and six fail. Fell and Yara are missing. Brask goes out alone into the dusk with a lantern and the Long Lie over Hallam's mild protest. The steward gives Ilse her iron ring with its brass meridian line. Kasimir meets her in the Old Court to say he has written to Hobbet asking that the Tull inquiry go to the Board of Errata. Ilse has bought a slower trial.

**State at end.** Ilse is a Member of the Meridian and wears an iron ring (slightly too big). Her Walk survey is attested with a plain "I. Marrow", and the stream-bank correction is on it. Brask has compared it with Teo's honest sheet and said nothing. Kasimir has asked for the Tull matter to go to the Board. Ilse knows Yara and Fell are missing and that Brask has gone to search.

**Invented details.** The Hall of Instruments has oar-length sightvanes, the Ninefold's chains in glass cases and map-tile floors. Attestation is at a stone lectern under the arch with a College pen. Brask has run the Walk day "nineteen times". "The Rushes": a place past the cairn where Brask guesses Fell has gone. One of the two other passers is a quiet girl from the Salt Steps; the other is a broken-nosed man from the Ammonite Hills. The steward is a thin old man in a black gown and is unnamed. The formula is "Ilse Marrow, Member of the Meridian, Walk of twelve hundred and three."

**Deviations.** Brask's detection is grounded concretely: she compares Ilse's corrected sheet with Teo's honest record of the same chained legs. Ilse signs the Walk sheet with plain initials, not the wren's foot, so that registering the wren's foot in Ch 6 stays the first exposure of that mark at the College and Benchmarking remains her first honest signature.

## Chapter 6 — "Benchmarking" (Reaping 24–30) — 3,898 words

**Summary.** In the Mess (gold-leaf star ceiling), the Patent links have already sorted into fours. Hen Solberg crashes her tray down and joins Ilse, Teo and Cass ("the others looked like they wanted to marry each other"); her huge laugh startles Teo. Proctor Arnaud assigns them the Wren Stair, top landing, with the Wren Stair and Long Gallery as their 3 ch 41 l stretch. Patent boys mock it ("it hops"), and Ilse privately thinks of her wren's foot. In the morning the black oak wardrobe is a foot from the wall. At the first Morning Survey the right-hand spiral stair now turns left and the Long Gallery is 1 ch 63 l instead of 1 ch 60 l ("like bread"). They argue. Cass reads the lean: the gallery is sliding west "like a drawer", and the stair twisted with it. Ilse drafts the stretch back to the record, which is her old trade done in daylight; Cass calls it "holding", and Ilse privately has another word for it. They sprint to the Register Antechamber. Merrin Coyle (ink-stained cuffs, thick folios) gives them three minutes: links sign with initials until Benchmarking, and "Master Hallam likes every sheet signed here." The stretch settles "like a door closing in another house". Other parties fail: a stair ends at a blank wall, and a door opens onto gorse and a wayhorn. At the Ossel Fair at the Verity Cross gate, Tenting sellers describe ossels into foxes and rabbits, and Cass explains flicker and the ethics. Ilse steps on an indeterminate creature, says "Sorry!", then describes it as "a small grey cat. With ridiculous ears", and it becomes one, amber-eyed. She buys it for a penny from Mab, a Tenting girl. Teo calls it a ferret and it flickers in distress; all agree: cat. It answers only to "Sorry". At Benchmarking on Reaping 30, Halloway stammers until asked how deep to cut, then goes fluent (an eighth of an inch at sixty degrees, an eleven-ounce mallet). Hen cuts a wave over a line, Teo a ringed dot (a pie from above), Cass three slanting strokes. Kasimir cuts a square with one diagonal. Ilse weighs a fresh mark, then registers the wren's foot, thinking of Quince carrying it for three years. Kasimir looks at her mark a moment too long. Hallam praises the clean mark and her "draftswoman's hand" (he noticed it on her Walk sheet). Ilse charts her lot-drawn flagstone (2 ft × 3 ft, cracked corner, 11.4 links from the nearest meridian stud) and signs with nothing under the mark. The chapter ends on how light her own weight is, with Sorry watching from the Mess steps.

**State at end.** The party is formed and lodged on the Wren Stair. Sorry is Ilse's (a small grey cat with too-big ears and amber eyes, illicit). The wren's foot is now Ilse's registered witness-mark, cut in the Old Wall and entered in the Register of Marks, and Kasimir has seen it. Ilse holds one honest charter: a sandstone flag in the southeast corner of the Old Court, to be kept in place for a year. She has signed one Morning Survey sheet (initials) in the Antechamber. She knows Coyle by name and knows of Hallam's signing rule, but sees nothing odd in either.

**Invented details.** The Mess ceiling is a gold-leaf star chart. Teo's uncle sells heifers at Ambry Market. The Wren Stair is said to "hop" (it turned round twice last year). The sitting room has four mismatched chairs and a black oak wardrobe. The record charter for the stretch: the stair is a right-hand spiral of 41 steps, and the Long Gallery runs 1 ch 60 l to its double doors, lined with portraits of disappointed Masters. The Dawn Bell is the deadline for signing. Links sign with initials until Benchmarking. Mab is the Tenting girl. Names tried for Sorry: Pebble and Pie. The Old Wall is the north wall of the Old Court, and its oldest mark (a bird in flight) is dated 4 H. The Register of Marks. Benchmark cutting: ⅛ inch at 60°, an 11-ounce mallet, struck from the wrist. Marks: Hen's is a wave over a line, Teo's a ring with a dot, Cass's three slanting strokes, Kasimir's a square with one corner-to-corner diagonal. Paving stones are allotted by lot.

**Deviations.** The outline's "first honest attestation" is the flagstone, but Ilse also signs a Morning Survey sheet earlier in the same chapter. To reconcile the two, the Morning Survey and Walk sheets are signed with initials, and the flagstone is the first time her own registered mark stands alone with nothing ghosted beneath it. The Patent links are said to have been at the College a week before the Walk.
