## Chapter 12 — “Watermarks” (Fogmonth 2–6)

**Summary.** Ilse copies the 811 Bellwether charter on Register Level Two, a vault that hums with held places. Hallam brings her tea and ginger biscuits, notices that she kept the drunk copyist’s “Friday lean,” and says she holds her pen like someone taught by a craftsman. She deflects. Merrin Coyle asks her to bundle Morning Survey folios for the year-store. Holding sheets to the lamp out of forger’s habit, she sees the watermark change at the turn of 1201, from Dunmore & Sons’ wheat-sheaf to a three-arch bridge. Coyle says Master Hallam changed the supplier to the cheaper Bridge Mill of Lisk, and she files it as a detail. Walking home through the Second Range, she finds the FAR RANGE — CLOSED BY ORDER 1105 door, with Sorry sitting rapt in front of it. Archivist Damask stops her touching it, tells her the Unheld Stacks’ history (“comes and goes like a cat”), is thrilled that she can feel it *sway*, and invites her to sit in on his seniors’ Palimpsest class on Thursdays. Hesta’s letter reports that Corwin Fask is asking about the Tull hedge and that an inquiry is coming “from the Big College,” and ends “Your Long Field money. I hope it bought you something.” Hen comforts Ilse without asking questions, and her gull laugh is established. Brask’s night Fieldwork class takes bearings off migrating wayhorns’ antlers, chains out to a cairn-crow cairn, and finds the lodestone twelve degrees out. Brask delivers her “it isn’t trying anything… also a lie” line.

**State at end.** Ilse knows that the watermark changed in Thaw 1201 and that Hallam changed the supplier (stored, not connected to anything). She has seen the Far Range door. She has an open invitation to Damask’s Thursday seniors’ class. Her mother’s letter is kept inside her shirt; Sorry licked the word “wretch” off it. Ilse knows Kasimir has written for the Tull papers. No injuries.

**Invented details.** Bellwether (Ammonite hill parish, chartered 811) and its drunk copyist Oswy Tarrant. Register boxes named Crane Abbas and Cullingford. Copyist pay of four marks a month. Watermarks: Dunmore & Sons of Tessary (wheat-sheaf with ribbon, “D & S”, chain lines 1¼ in.) and the Bridge Mill on the Ambry at Lisk (three-arch bridge, no letters, chain lines about 1 in., harder sizing). The changeover falls between the last week of Candlemonth 1200 and the first week of Thaw 1201. The corridor from the Second Range stair to the Wren Stair is 88 paces by day. The stain on the plaster is shaped like Lisk. The knife-scratched “and stay closed” under the brass plate. The Far Range door was last in the Second Range eleven years ago and once spent a week behind the Provost’s dressing-room mirror. The 1105 quarrel was between the Provost and the Master of Works over the connecting gallery. Links don’t take Palimpsest until the winter term, so Damask invites Ilse to his seniors on Thursdays at the fourth bell. In Hesta’s letter: Jem at the vestry; Agnes Tull’s damson jam; Tam Brewin’s mare is mended; the parish has petitioned the Hundred about the lane. Hen’s mother writes about the salt, and the rival salt family is the Pellows. The wayhorn herd of about 80 gave bearings of N61E, N54E and N48E. The cairn was 11 chains 40 links off, with eleven stones and a white feather. Teo’s uncle sells wayhorn velvet for gloves.

**Deviations.** None of substance. Hallam appears briefly and warmly, and no travel plans are mentioned, so that the Ch 13 “away on inspection” clue stands alone.

## Chapter 13 — “Brine End” (Fogmonth 9–10)

**Summary.** At breakfast Hen reads out Coyle’s notice that Hallam is away on inspection in the Salt Steps until Fogmonth 14, so copying is cancelled (clue). Lisbet Arnaud summons Ilse to Brask. They ride six miles south in a dog-cart with a crow-basket from Mother Nettle, and Brask’s pipe stays unlit. Ilse feels the void as a hole in the level and points to it. At Brine End: the well has moved 40 yards (chained at 1 ch 82 l, S70E) into an onion bed, leaving a dry ring. A garden wall is unknitting course by course. Hens circle the coop’s old site. Marta’s mother’s doorstep has turned round. The parish copy is fine, but “a copy is a picture of a promise.” Brask sends a crow, and at nightfall Coyle replies that the Brine End master is missing from the Register. Ilse thinks of the Little Wick lane. Next morning villagers want to go after a Wainwright camp. Brask bows to the elder Tansy Wainwright and speaks in Tenting idiom. Tansy says the ground has been letting go up and down the Margin since Thaw, and “Rent’s coming due,” and Brask goes very still. Brask defuses the villagers in the chapel. She then has Ilse chart the village *as it is*, a stitch rather than a repair, refusing to let her “guess with their water” by moving the well back. Ilse records a 3-link misclosure honestly. Brask refuses to sign, so Ilse attests with her wren’s foot, and the village takes hold. Ilse and the reeve’s son turn the doorstep back by hand with a crowbar. At dusk a hollowing approaches the fresh charter. Ilse reaches through its edge to save the sheet and loses five minutes. Brask drives it off with a low Tenting road-song and ends: “And not a word of that song to the Provost.”

**State at end.** Ilse and Brask are at Brine End at dusk on Fogmonth 10, about to drive back. Ilse is un-pinned: five minutes lost for good, the first two fingertips of her right hand white and numb (recovering by Ch 14–15), and nausea. She now carries the burden of the Brine End holding charter (14 ch 6 l, nine stations), which causes a dull ache from wrist to elbow. She knows the Brine End master is missing, and suspects in passing that voids and missing charters are linked, but connects nothing to any person. She has heard “rent’s coming due” and knows Brask can sing hollowings off.

**Invented details.** The reeve Tobin Sallis. The weeping woman, whose mother scrubbed the step for forty years; she is unnamed in text (named “Marta” in planning only) and has a son. The Brine End charter dates from 1006. Seven cottages, a cart-shed chapel and eel traps. Elder Tansy Wainwright: small, with grey braids heavily corded. She mentions voids at Little Sallow (a ditch) and at “the Hollin camp at Dunny Water.” Brask is identified by Tansy as from Thessaly Ford. The fat dun pony. Nettle’s crow swears. Lisbet Arnaud has “a voice like a ruled line.” The hollowing is “the size of a haycock,” like a thumbprint on a lens.

**Deviations.** The outline puts the crow’s reply “by nightfall” in Scene 2 and the hollowing “at dusk” in Scene 4. I spread the visit over two days (Fogmonth 9 for the arrival and reply, Fogmonth 10 for the confrontation, charter and hollowing), which matches the outline’s date range. The “freshly re-attested patch where Brask and Ilse worked” is a holding charter signed by **Ilse alone**, because Brask refuses to attest new maps (character bible). The doorstep is turned back by hand rather than by charter. That is my addition, a small humane beat that keeps “record, don’t correct” intact.

## Chapter 14 — “What Brask Knew” (Fogmonth 11)

**Summary.** Brask leaves a note summoning Ilse to her cottage. Ilse still has two numb fingertips, and her friends report that she repeated herself after the hollowing. Ilse wears her father’s coat, with Quince’s kit sewn into the hem. The cottage on Netmakers’ Row in Verity Cross has a blue door and a cairn-crow on the lintel. Inside are waist-high stacks of books like cairns, crow feathers in jam jars, a cold hearth and a plane-table for a dining table. Brask boils tea leaves in a pan (tea “like a boot”) and lays Ilse’s Walk survey under a lens. Ilse had shaved 14 links out of the stream bank and gone over the line twice. Brask says: “Nobody closes a Margin traverse to nothing. Nobody honest… Forgers correct. Surveyors record.” Ilse calmly works out the coach fares home and says “Yes.” Brask is relieved: “Good. So was I, once — every surveyor who ever chose what to leave out.” She refuses to hear parishes or names. She tells part of the Sallow story: seven went in 1183, they found the sounders’ singing-grounds and something that “was trying to talk to us,” four were lost, she lost her eye to a hollowing, and Hallam carried her twenty leagues and “never once said I was heavy.” The Board ruled it hallucination. She explains the song (Wainwright women at Thessaly Ford; hollowings hate song and moving ground) and says: “The map is a promise, not a cage. Know which one you’re making.” The bargain is that she keeps Ilse’s secret on condition of no more lies on any College sheet, and Ilse means it. Brask warns that Kasimir came to the shed asking about the Walk survey and where Ilse learned to draft, and wrote it all down in a black book. Brask also passes on that the Brine End well held. Ilse walks back in the dark and realises she has been trusted, with nothing to line it with. Feeling returns to her fingers as pins and needles.

**State at end.** It is night on Fogmonth 11, and Ilse is walking back to the College. Brask knows Ilse forged (the shape of it, no specifics) and keeps the secret, though she will not lie if the Board asks her directly. Ilse has **not** told Brask about ghosting Quince’s mark or about the blind-ink kit. Feeling is returning to her two fingertips. Ilse knows Kasimir questioned Brask. Hallam reads warmly throughout (he carried Brask), and the gloves are left as Sallow-related.

**Invented details.** Brask’s address is Netmakers’ Row, Verity Cross, by the old harbour, with a blue door. **The seventh Sallow member is named Wenna Rook**: a nineteen-year-old grain-reader who survived along with Brask and Hallam. The bible gives seven members, but only Brask, Hallam and the four lost are named. *Flag for continuity/series planning; rename or remove if Book Two needs otherwise.* Corrow and Tey were lost “in a fold that closed,” and Paske and Vass “in the night.” Brask lost a day and a half to her hollowing and was three days from the Margin when it struck. A Wainwright woman wintered at the Brask family gate at Thessaly Ford. Brask’s “Patent boy try to chain a sounder” anecdote. Ilse’s fare calculations: the express is 38 marks and the slow coach is 11 marks and nine days.

**Deviations.** None. I chose to keep the Ninefold, the Vey and the “Landlords” entirely unspoken. Brask’s description stays at “something… trying to talk to us; the ground moved when it spoke,” consistent with her recorded testimony.

## Chapter 15 — “Blind Ink” (Fogmonth 18–20)

**Summary.** At a foggy Morning Survey the stretch comes out to 3 ch 42 l plus Teo’s estimated “hair.” In the Antechamber Hen takes the first round and signs all four sheets (“I’m fastest”), as she always does. Each sheet needs at least two marks and takes up to four, and Ilse signs two. Ilse stares at the printed attestation box and remembers the violet line she saw in the rain on Gleaning 10. That evening, alone on the Wren Stair (Hen has gone to bed early and tired), she unpicks the coat hem and lays out Quince’s kit. She mixes an alum-water damp-lens on Quince’s watch-glass and tests it on a blind-ink line. She rationalises that looking isn’t lying, and notices herself rationalising. On Fogmonth 19, after copying (Hallam leaves a note instead of tea), she stays in the dark Antechamber. Under the lens, every mark on every Morning Survey sheet sits squarely on a printed blind-ink knot. The knot runs exactly beneath the printed rule of the attestation box, turns into a fingernail-sized figure under the mark, and runs off to the binding edge “as if it continued on some other sheet.” This includes her party’s marks and Kasimir’s party’s. The knots are identical and therefore printed from a plate, not hand-drawn. She recognises them as ghosting and as a channel for weight, and thinks of Wil Ashdown’s grey signing hand. The knots are present back to the first week of Thaw 1201 and absent from Candlemonth 1200. Something about Thaw 1201 snags, and before she can reach it she realises that telling means exposing herself. A page turns. Kasimir steps out from the far presses with a half-shuttered lantern and a red-spined ledger he keeps out of sight. Each accuses the other. He says “You are very frightened now… for someone who is merely copying,” warns her that Coyle counts, and leaves. The chapter ends on his footsteps going.

**State at end.** It is night on Fogmonth 19, and Ilse is alone in the Antechamber with the bundles out and the lens up her sleeve. She knows that every Morning Survey sheet since Thaw 1201 is ghosted beneath each student’s mark, that the burden almost certainly flows *into* the signers, and that the other end of the channel is off the sheet. She does **not** yet consciously connect this to the watermark or the supplier, or to Hallam; the thought snags and is interrupted. Kasimir has seen her with the folios but not the lens. Ilse saw his red-spined ledger. Her two fingertips have fully recovered. The kit is out of the coat lining.

**Invented details.** The stretch breakdown: Wren Stair 1 ch 9 l plus Long Gallery 2 ch 32 l, making 3 ch 41 l. Four Morning Survey sheets per party per morning: stair plan, stair section, gallery plan and tie-sheet. Each sheet needs 2–4 marks. The printed attestation box is slightly larger than a playing card, with “Attested” above it. The Antechamber has eight sloping desks with chained brass inkwells, filing presses with brass mesh doors, and an alcove behind the last presses. Quince’s kit is an oilcloth packet the size of a prayer book. It holds the blind ink, two fine nibs, chamois, a paper twist of alum, and a two-inch watch-glass lens in a brass ring with a handle, made by Quince from a pocket-watch and a curtain ring. The lens method is to grind alum, dissolve it in warm water, film the glass with chamois and breathe on it twice; Quince called it “asking the paper politely.” **Quince’s mark** is a crooked Q with a hook through it like a fish on a line, kept on a scrap of vellum. **Students’ witness-marks** (please reconcile with Ch 6 if that writer described any): Hen, a gull’s wing over a line; Teo, a looped T like a pothook; Cass, a quick Tenting zigzag; Kasimir, a beautifully drawn R with a level line through it; Orla, a flourish; Absalom, a laborious cross; Piety, a spiky little steeple. Cass is out at Verity Cross with Orla Devane, a small seed for the Ch 22+ spark.

**Deviations.** The outline says the ghost-line lies “beneath each student’s mark” in “perfect registration.” I rendered the registration as the blind-ink trace lying exactly under the printed attestation rule, so that any mark set in the box crosses its knot. This is consistent with Coyle’s later “registration guides” (Ch 27) and with the ledger (the under-drawing is printed on the sheets). I avoided showing the delta or root direction, which is reserved for Ch 24.

## Chapter 16 — “Hen” (Fogmonth 25 – Frost 3, past midnight)

**Summary.** On Fogmonth 25 Hen answers Teo’s Lisk-bread joke with “Yes.” Teo notices first and tells Ilse she isn’t laughing. We learn that since Ch 15 Ilse has signed only one sheet a morning, the minimum, and Hen has silently signed the rest. Ilse wrote an anonymous note to Dr. Orle and burned it, and keeps telling herself “one more week.” On Fogmonth 27 Hen flatly says “It’s a cat,” and Sorry’s ears shrink to ordinary. Ilse re-describes it with “ridiculous ears.” On Fogmonth 28 Cass tries to provoke Hen, insulting the Salt Steps and taking her prized pen, and Hen says “You can have it.” Cass breaks down on the stair and begs Ilse to tell her what’s wrong, and Ilse doesn’t. At Morning Survey on Frost 1 Hen counts in a flat, even voice. In the Antechamber she takes all four sheets (“I’m fastest”), and Ilse sees the slate-grey on her first two fingertips. Ilse realises Hen is the most diligent signer, about 240 marks in some sixty mornings, and has been carrying Ilse’s share too. Quince’s maxim now reads as an accusation. Ilse takes the pen, Hen’s hand cold as a stream stone, and signs the remaining three sheets herself, knowingly. Coyle looks at Hen’s hands a moment too long. At breakfast Hen says “I’m cold,” and they take her to the infirmary. Dr. Orle says “Drift-fatigue… that is what I am to call it,” and that Hen is the fourth. Wil Ashdown, grey now to both sets of knuckles, lies in the second bed. Teo waits outside with an eel pie he can’t bring himself to take in. At the bedside Hen answers “The tide goes out twice a day” and says “I can’t remember why things were funny… I just can’t remember why.” Ilse’s guilt over nearly two weeks of silence. When Ilse says sorry, Hen asks “What for?” That night Ilse walks the Drift Wing, weighs Brask, Hallam and silence, counts the whole cost honestly, and knocks on her own door after one o’clock. The last line: “I have to tell you something about myself, and then something about this College, and you’ll like neither.” End of Part Two.

**State at end.** It is just after 1 a.m. on Frost 3, on the Wren Stair, with Teo, Cass and Sorry. Ilse is about to confess, which leads into Ch 17. Hen is in the infirmary in the end bed by the west window, Quiet, with her first two right fingertips grey. Ilse has signed three ghosted sheets on Frost 1 by choice. Teo and Cass know Hen is ill but nothing of the cause. Dr. Orle is visibly angry at the official diagnosis but has not yet confided in Ilse; that comes in Ch 18. Coyle has noticed Hen’s hands.

**Invented details.** Teo’s grandmother “sold the same loaf three days at three prices and called it a vintage.” The joke about “the eel and the bishop,” told at the Chain & Anchor after the Ladder. Sorry’s ears grow when Hen calls them “two mainsails.” Hen’s good pen was sent by her father from the Steps in a beribboned box. An unnamed third Quiet girl lies in the infirmary. Hen’s bed is the end bed by the window, looking west over the Margin. Ilse wrote and burned her note to Dr. Orle on Fogmonth 21. The Wing corridor measured 93 paces that night.

**Deviations.** (1) The character bible says Hen is in the infirmary from Ch 18, but the outline (Ch 16, Scene 3) has her taken there in Ch 16. I followed the outline. (2) The outline says the knock comes on the night of Ch 16 (dated to Frost 2), and Ch 17 is Frost 3. I set the knock just after the one o’clock bell, so it is strictly Frost 3 and runs continuously into Ch 17’s confession. Ch 17’s writer may open in the common room that same night, or move to the Mess kitchen. (3) I added that Ilse shifted her own signing onto Hen after discovering the ghost-lines (passively, by signing only the minimum). This sharpens the outline’s guilt beat (“she knew about the lines for two weeks and said nothing to protect herself”) and turns Quince’s maxim against her. It also gives the Ch 17 off-register trick a clear motive.

## Cross-chapter continuity notes for other writers
- **What Ilse knows by the end of Ch 16:** watermark and supplier change in 1201 (not yet linked); the Brine End master is missing; hollowings come for fresh attestations and flee song; Brask knows she forged and has bound her to “no lies on College sheets”; Kasimir has questioned Brask and was in the Antechamber at night with a red-spined ledger; the printed blind-ink knots run under every Morning Survey mark since Thaw 1201; Hen is Quiet. She has **not** connected any of it to Hallam, and she has not told anyone about the ghost-lines.
- Ilse attested the Brine End holding charter (Fogmonth 10), so it adds to her own burden.
- Quince’s kit (including the damp-lens) is now out of the coat hem and in Ilse’s possession on the Wren Stair.
- Hallam appears in Ch 12 (tea, warmth) and Ch 15 (a note only). He is away Fogmonth 9–14 “in the Salt Steps.”
- Images not to repeat elsewhere: iron filings (wayhorns), the unknitting sock (wall), the thumbprint on a lens (hollowing), the stone from a stream (Hen’s hand), and “a hand drawn by someone who had only been told about hands” (Hen’s fingers; the Brine End sketch uses a related “told about it once, in a pub” joke).
