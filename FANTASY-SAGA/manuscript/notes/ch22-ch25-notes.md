# Notes: Chapters 22–25

## Chapter 22 — “Errata Night” (Frost 22, 1203) — 3,893 words

**Summary.** A crow brings Ilse a note from the suspended Brask: “Confess something useful. Not the obvious thing.” The Mess is lit by hundreds of candles. Lisbet Arnaud opens the Errata Slate and states the custom: nothing said tonight is repeated or held against anyone, and the Slate is washed at midnight. Steyne opens with a safe joke about the Refectory chairs. Halloway confesses his 1189 sheep-fold error (eight and a quarter inches out of true for fourteen years; he goes to look at it every summer). Orla confesses she reads Piety’s letters and calls hope the error; Moy confesses his invented choir exemption; the Sounders’ Choir sings the *Meridian Hymn* while the infirmary doors stand open. Cass says “I love the Big House, may the Road forgive me,” to roars. Teo confesses his fear of heights (“*We know*”) and then, more quietly, that he has never told Hen her laugh is the best sound in the building. Ilse stands and says, “I’ve lied on paper. For years. Well.” The College answers “Recorded,” exactly as it did for everything else. Kasimir says it alone, two heartbeats late, to the candle in front of him. The Slate is washed and the toast given. On the Wren Stair, Teo shows Ilse his first two fingertips, faintly grey. The off-register trick stops new lines taking, but his pre-Frost 4 sheets are still filed and still pulling.

**State at end.** Night of Frost 22, the Wren Stair: Ilse, Teo, Cass and Sorry. Hen is in the infirmary; Brask is at her Verity Cross cottage, barred from the grounds. Teo’s right index and middle fingertips are grey to just short of the first knuckle and cool to the touch, first noticed about Frost 18, with no worsening since. Ilse knows the grey comes from his old ghosted sheets. Kasimir’s accusation stands.

**Invented details.**
- The **Errata Slate**: a framed schoolroom slate with a sponge on a string, kept by the senior proctor. One chalk stroke is made per confession, and it is washed at midnight with the whole hall standing. By custom what is washed “is not repeated, not entered, not minuted and not held against anyone in any court or any Board.” The Provost confesses first.
- 109 confessions in 1203.
- Halloway’s error: a dry-stone sheep-fold at **Callow Edge**, Ammonite Hills, his third-year practical in spring 1189. It is an irregular heptagon with a perimeter of 4 chains 61 links. He carried a seven for a one on the fourth leg, and a cancelling error hid it.
- The Refectory chairs: varnished gift chairs from a Tessary benefactor, arrived Gleaning.
- Mother Nettle feeds the Provost’s message-crow. The *Meridian Hymn*. The choir wears dark felt collars with brass pins.
- Brask traditionally stood at the far end of the dais on Errata Night, refusing a chair.
- Snow loops and spirals past the Lantern Gate while falling straight in the Old Court.

**Deviations.** None of substance. Kasimir’s late “Recorded” is shown as two heartbeats after the hall finishes, so “a beat” is avoided. Teo’s confession adds a line about Hen’s laugh, drawn from his character bible.

## Chapter 23 — “The Infirmary Ledger” (Deepwinter 3–5) — 3,772 words

**Summary.** Ilse reads Salt Steps tide-tables to Hen, whose grey has reached the second knuckle and who answers “It was a pie.” In her office, Orle shows Ilse the green-cloth ledger: the first case is Wil Ashdown, a link, on Seedtime 9, 1201; there is nothing before 1201, and every case begins on the signing hand. She also shows the Provost’s “one voice” letter. Ilse connects it: the ghost-lines and the Bridge Mill paper began on Thaw 1, 1201 (she found this in Ch 15), and the Quiet began thirty-nine mornings later. In the Register stores ledger she finds the Candlemonth 12, 1200 requisition approved by Hallam. Coyle, catching her there, explains that the change was really the Provost’s audit-year economy: Hallam “signed under protest.” Coyle is visibly uneasy and tells her to ask the Provost’s office. Next day Steyne summons her to his study, which holds a nine-foot model of the Meridian with a sliding Drift Wing. He offers to have the accusation deferred into oblivion, and Brask’s suspension revisited, if she stops upsetting Orle and stops asking about the paper supply: “The College’s survival depends on calm.” He also pointedly repeats her Errata Night confession. Ilse feigns agreement. On the Wren Stair, Cass is sure it’s the Provost. Teo isn’t: “He’s a coward, not a — whatever this is.” The work is too patient for Steyne. Sorry eats the Provost’s summons card.

**State at end.** Deepwinter 5, the Wren Stair. Ilse suspects Steyne, at least of ordering the scheme. She knows the Quiet began with the new paper and that Hallam’s signature approved the change, but she believes Coyle’s account that it was the Provost’s economy. She has not told Orle about the ghost-lines. She plans to use Damask’s raking-light class. Teo is unchanged.

**Invented details.** Orle’s office: a cupboard over the coal-yard with a skeleton called “Mr. Bones.” Ledger entries: two more cases in 1201 (Haysel, Frost) and four in 1202. The Provost’s seal is a meridian line crossed by a quill. The stores-ledger entry reads: “Candlemonth 12. Survey folios, linen-rag, forty reams, of the Bridge Mill, Lisk, in place of Dunmore & Sons, Tessary. Approved: A. Hallam, Mag. Reg.” The Assembly audit is said to have cut every department’s allowance by a fifth. Steyne’s model is adjusted monthly from Survey filings, and its Lantern Gate keystone is a chip of amber. The tide-tables are for Saltcombe and Tollhaven. Steyne’s summons card is gilt-edged, in clove-scented ink.

**Deviations.**
- Wil Ashdown was a first-year (link) in Seedtime 1201, so I added that the College keeps him on the rolls as a chain. This squares Ch 8’s “second-year” with the ledger’s 1201 date.
- Since Ch 15 already establishes that the lines begin at Thaw 1201, the Ch 23 connection is that *the Quiet* began with them. The new paper evidence is the requisition, not the watermark.

## Chapter 24 — “Raking Light” (Deepwinter 15–17) — 3,813 words

**Summary.** Damask darkens his Palimpsest workroom and shows raking light on an 841 H. mill-leat deed. A scraped-out boundary between two brothers rises from the paper, and with it the name “Edwy”: “Oh! Look, *look*.” He confirms that a never-visible ink shows even better than a scraped one. That night Ilse takes the Frost 1 Wren Stair folio, pre-skew and one of Hen’s last, and lays it on the floor by a low candle. The lines from each mark’s knot, which run beneath the rule to the binding, bend together in the blank margin into one channel, ending in a printer’s registration tick. It is a delta reversed. She remembers Quince’s word “conduit” and realizes there is a root sheet somewhere. The line from Hen’s gull-wing is deepest. Hallam invites her to share a seed-cake. In his study (sea-coal fire, books, a sounder-ivory paperweight) he is warm, asks after Hen and Teo, praises Brask, offers to speak for her hand at the Board, and says “Every map is an act of love.” She tells him a portion: lines under the marks that join at the margin, seen by raking light. She does not mention the damp-lens, blind ink or Quince. He gravely acknowledges the seventh Law, thanks her, and promises to investigate personally. He asks whether she has told anyone (she says no) and where the sheet is (she lies: back in its bundle). He asks her to tell no one: “Panic kills more than any illness.” Cass is sceptical (“Big House looks after Big House”); Ilse says “Not him.” Before First Bell, Sorry wakes her in a panic. The wardrobe has drifted nearly three times its usual distance, and the folio is gone from Halloway’s *Elements of Error* although the door was latched. Ilse measures and records it, then wakes the others.

**State at end.** Dawn, Deepwinter 17. The folio is gone. Ilse believes Coyle noticed the missing sheet and the Provost’s people took it. She does not suspect Hallam. Hallam believes no one else knows, and that the sheet is in its bundle. Cass and Teo know Ilse spoke to Hallam. Sorry is distressed.

**Invented details.** The Palimpsest workroom is under the Library of Record, with felt-lined shutters. The 841 deed is from the parish of **Thorne**, with the scraped brother **Edwy**. Quince’s boast: he once ghosted a chapter-house so that a dean’s burden ran into a dozen dead canons, which is where the word “conduit” comes from. Hallam’s seed-cake comes from his sister’s daughter in Lisk. The step into his study “has never moved since the Ninefold.” Wall maps include a faded hand-drawn Sallow coast. Hallam’s story has Brask crossing a standing-rain bog with a lamp, singing to keep hollowings off. The wardrobe measured 2 ft 11½ in from the wall.

**Deviations.** Hallam is never menacing on the surface. His questions (told anyone? Mr. Reyne? where is the sheet?) are kept plausible as care, and the narration draws no attention to them. Ilse’s suspicion at the end is pointed at Coyle and the Provost to preserve the Ch 25 reveal.

## Chapter 25 — “Kasimir’s Ledger” (Candlemonth 20) — 3,825 words

**Summary.** A week after the theft, Hallam sent a kind note (“Inquiries proceed… Burn this”), and Ilse burned it. In the crow-loft at sunset, while Ilse sends her weekly letter to Brask, Kasimir climbs in, hits his head, and is cackled at by Mother Nettle (“*They* duck”). He explains that he has been investigating the voids for his mother since the summer. His notebook pairs seven voids with seven Register travel warrants signed by Hallam, from posting-house ledgers and the bursar’s daybook (with Orla’s help): Hythe Corner, Little Sallow, Marlow, Fennock, the Little Wick lane, Brine End and Corrow. That is seven of eleven tours, and the other four went to deeply held ground. “I was in Hobbet that week. I did not see him. I saw you.” Ilse resists and argues for Steyne. Kasimir answers that the Provost has not travelled on any of those dates and does not sign warrants. In Brask’s padlocked Fieldwork shed (entered by the wedged back door), raking light shows him the ghost knot under his own R and the gathering channel. He tells her about his stilled father, explains his perfectionism, and is horrified by the off-register trick (“That is a lie”), then accepts it with a dry “Recorded.” Ilse assembles the clues: the paper, the Antechamber rule, the untaught seventh Law, the travel, the grey gloves at Hobbet, and Nan’s “grey hands.” She grieves the real kindness and admits she told Hallam everything at tea. Kasimir records it. “He knows I know.”

**State at end.** Night of Candlemonth 20, the Fieldwork shed. Ilse and Kasimir both know Hallam is responsible for the ghosting and the voids, and that a conduit or root sheet exists somewhere. They do not know where it is, about the Zero Sheets, or about the Equinox plan. They are uneasy allies; he still calls her “Miss Marrow,” and the accusation stands. Kasimir is ghosted but shows no grey, and will now sign off-register. He has his party’s Candlemonth 19 folio. Ilse has not yet told Teo and Cass of the Hallam conclusion.

**Invented details.**
- Void list: **Hythe Corner**, Salt Steps (Blossom 1202, harbour wall unknit 9 rods); Little Sallow (Fogmonth 1202, well displaced 22 yds); Marlow (Seedtime 1203, ditch); Fennock (Greening 1203, gatepost and fence); Little Wick lane (Reaping 15); Brine End (Fogmonth 9); Corrow (Frost 7).
- Hallam’s Fallowmarch warrant ran Reaping 8–21, 1203, with stages at Verity Cross, Tessary and Hobbet (two nights). For Brine End, his warrant said the Salt Steps, but the Verity Cross posting house records a gig hired and driven south.
- The Provost has travelled to Tessary three times in two years for the Assembly.
- Mother Nettle’s crow is **Tansy**. The cairn-crows build pebble cairns on the loft sills. A hill has stood under standing rain three miles out since Fogmonth.
- Kasimir holds an assessor’s commission. His notebook includes sample errors from Fogmonth 4 and 11.

**Deviations.** The outline sets scene 2 without naming a place. I used the Fieldwork shed (raking light needs the dark, and Brask’s absence resonates there) after the crow-loft conversation.

## Continuity flags for the line edit (cross-writer)
- **Ch 26** says Kasimir told Ilse about his father “in the crow-loft, in four sentences.” In my Ch 25 he tells it in the Fieldwork shed, in about four sentences. Either change Ch 26 to “in Brask’s shed,” or accept it. Ch 26 also has him visiting his father “every day for a year and then I stopped,” and I removed my conflicting line to match.
- **Ch 26** gives Marlow as a “sheep-drove” and Fennock as a “ford.” Ch 1 and my Ch 25 have a ditch at Marlow and a gatepost at Fennock. Ch 26 should change.
- **Ch 26** has Ilse tell Brask she told Hallam “on Deepwinter 15” and that the sheet was gone “the next morning.” My timeline: Damask class and raking on Deepwinter 15, tea on the 16th, theft discovered at dawn on the 17th. The dates are not stated in my text, so changing Ch 26 to “Deepwinter 16” aligns them.
- **Ch 26** describes Kasimir’s warrants as from “the Register’s day-books.” Mine come from posting-house ledgers plus the bursar’s daybook. These are compatible but could be unified.
- I aligned with Ch 12, Ch 15, Ch 17 and Ch 18 on several points:
  - Dunmore & Sons wheat-sheaf paper gave way to the Bridge Mill bridge at Thaw 1201.
  - Ghost knot under each mark, with lines running beneath the rule to the binding edge.
  - Off-register means “a sixteenth high of the rule, a little sunwise.”
  - Marks: Hen’s gull-wing, Teo’s pothook T, Cass’s zigzag, Kasimir’s R with a level line, Absalom’s cross, Piety’s steeple.
  - Orle’s green ledger, with Benet Lowe as the left-handed case.
