# Notes: Chapters 30–32

## Chapter 30 — “Under the Lantern Gate” (Thaw 22, 1204 H., equinox dawn) — 3,894 words

### Summary
At 4:20 a.m., in the Hall of Instruments, Ilse and Kasimir finish the open charter of the Drift Wing. They drafted it overnight from the merged party’s two chainings of the Wing (outbound on Thaw 21, homeward before dawn on Thaw 22). Every wall is drawn as a near and far limit, and every length is given as a range with its rate and direction. Kasimir leaves to join Cass and Coyle for the Conduit. At a quarter to six the College gathers under the Lantern Gate. Hallam praises the merged party, then gently proposes setting the open charter aside “pending study” and signing his prepared folio “for safety.” Steyne nods, and Calder records an objection. Ilse steps onto the meridian studs and tells the truth: the ghost-lines, the Quiet, the voids, and the seventh Law. She names herself a forger to explain how she knows. Hallam answers with sorrow: “our accused forger … would have you doubt the Register.” Piety and Orla speak up for her, and Steyne wavers. Hallam signs his folio and Ilse feels the ghost wake under it. Before Lisbet Arnaud can sign, Ilse reasons that Precedence asks “truer?” before “older?”, and she attests the open charter, which Teo holds open on the steps. The Wing breathes and Hallam’s folio becomes only a drawing. Fog-damp shows its violet under-drawing to Lisbet and the front rows. Kasimir runs in with the Conduit Sheet, followed by Cass and Coyle. Hallam’s mask drops into grief (Level Seven, the softening, Steyne’s order of silence, and “a few hundred of us … against a million of her children”). Steyne looks away in front of everyone. Ilse knows that tearing the sheet sends every sliver back down the delta to its root. Hallam says, “Do it, then. Someone has to carry her.” She tears it. The court goes still. Teo’s grey recedes, and Hen’s laugh rings from an infirmary window. Hallam’s gloves split, the grey climbs to his throat, and he sits on the Gate steps and stills with his eyes open, facing east toward Tessary. The chapter ends on the lantern and Ilse’s shaking hands.

### State at end
- **Hallam:** stilled (a benchmark), seated on the Lantern Gate steps.
- **Students:** all ghost-links are broken. Hen is awake in the infirmary and Teo’s fingertips are clearing. The Wing is held by the open charter, swaying within its limits.
- **The Conduit:** torn into four pieces on the steps.
- **Hallam’s folio:** superseded. Its blind-ink under-drawing was publicly seen.
- **What Ilse knows:** everything in Ledger section A except why the Zero Sheets fail. The whole College has heard her confess to forgery and heard Hallam say “the center is failing.”
- **Sorry:** awake early after the dog form (see deviations), recoiling from the Conduit’s quicksilver.

### Invented details
- The Ninefold line Hallam quotes: “the Reach is held by many hands or by none.”
- Hallam’s witness-mark: “a neat looped sigil like a coiled rope.”
- There are 44 proctors (Teo counts them).
- The open charter’s figures: Long Gallery 3 ch. 38 l. to 3 ch. 47 l.; Wren Stair turning 4° either hand; Third Range swinging through 2 ch. 6 l., west on the ebb and home on the flood. (The canonical 3 ch. 41 l. stretch total sits inside this range.)
- The Observatory “has been drunk since 1140” (Kasimir’s joke).
- Eleven students sleep in the Third Range (Hallam’s claim).
- Absalom’s broken leg came from the fold. Piety wears a bandaged wrist.
- The infirmary has tall second-floor windows onto the Old Court.

### Deviations and choices
- **“First in time”:** the outline has Hallam sign first and Ilse attest “first in time and higher in fidelity.” I read Precedence by the canonical Law: fidelity decides first and age only breaks ties. Ilse needs to be *truer*, and to act before any co-signer’s pen touches the ghosted folio. The Wing takes Hallam’s folio briefly, then goes to the truer record. This follows Ledger A.8(c).
- **The ghost fails (A.8b):** it fails because the superseded folio no longer holds anything fixed. The College co-signs the open charter (in Ch 31) with no ghost able to take.
- **Hen sitting up and laughing:** close-third POV means Ilse can’t see the infirmary. She *hears* Hen’s laugh through an open infirmary window onto the Old Court, and Ch 31 confirms the scene.
- **Sorry:** by the ossel rules it should have slept a full day after the Ch 29 dog form. It wakes early, groggy, and “points” toward the Register. This is meant as a small further hint of Sorry’s unusual nature.
- **Brask is absent:** she is still barred from the grounds.
- **Piety and Orla:** they speak for Ilse, a small change of heart after the Ch 29 rescue.

## Chapter 31 — “The Board of Errata” (Thaw 23–28) — 3,888 words

### Summary
On Thaw 23 the infirmary is full of recovering students. Hen eats everything, cries and laughs. Ilse confesses that she knew about the lines for two weeks, and Hen gives her “a horrible prune” on Teo’s instructions. Teo shows everyone his pink fingertips. Wil Ashdown speaks for the first time anyone on the Wren Stair has heard: “Is that marmalade?” On the roof, Cass explains the annotated lock and the key hidden in the hollow sounder-ivory paperweight, which Coyle didn’t know he knew about. Cass is not glad about Hallam. At noon on the equinox the whole College had co-signed the open charter. On Thaw 24 Steyne’s Mess address blames “grave misconduct by a single Master” with a burden-disordered mind and declares the Quiet cured. He never mentions the Zero Sheets, which Ilse and Kasimir both notice, and the Walkers’ table answers with a low chant of “Broth.” On Thaw 28 the Board sits in the Hall of Instruments: Calder presiding, with Halloway, Moy, Orle and Damask. Lady Imogen Reyne observes. A Court evidence tin holds 29 wren’s-foot charters that Kasimir gathered by writ over the winter. Brask, reinstated for the morning, testifies. Kasimir testifies without lying, and argues that the law that would expel Ilse would have kept Hallam in office. Ilse confesses fully and names two forgeries the Court missed. With the Board’s leave, she unsigns all 29 with her own hand, the Tull charter last. The Board finds her guilty, but as a Member she cannot be handed to the Court. Under *Errata v. Pell* (1061) she is sentenced to restitution and to “the use the College most needs.” That means probation as the first student of Contrary Charting since 1097, under Brask, whose suspension is lifted. Lady Reyne leaves without a word to her son. Ilse stands on the step where Hallam sat, with no pride but something lighter.

### State at end
- **Ilse:** a Member on probation and a student of Contrary Charting under Brask. All her known forgeries are released, except Fennock and the Marlow road gate, which await their sheets. The Tull hedge will go back two rods east that night.
- **Brask:** reinstated.
- **Hallam:** stilled, carried to the Crypt on the night of Thaw 22 by eight orderlies.
- **Coyle:** has burned all unprinted ghosted folios.
- **Kasimir:** testified, and has not been spoken to by his mother.
- **Steyne:** in office but publicly mocked. Lisbet Arnaud lets the chant run.
- **What Ilse knows:** unchanged, plus Steyne’s deliberate omission.

### Invented details
- **Board composition:** Calder (presiding), Halloway, Moy, Orle and Damask. Steyne does not sit.
- **The forgeries:** 31 wren’s-foot forgeries in all, 29 recovered from eleven parish chests. The two missing are a ditch at Fennock (chest at Saint Ivo’s) and a field gate on the Marlow road.
- **Places and clients:** Lower Hendle (a cowman with three daughters, a ditch, 11 yards, spring 1201); a hedge at Marlow (22 yards); a gatepost at Bell Cross; a well-head at Ashby (4 feet, 1202); a water-meadow at Coldharbour.
- **Precedent:** *Errata v. Pell*, 1061. The last Contrary Charting student was admitted in 1097.
- **Orle** wrote to Tessary eleven times.
- **Unsigning:** shown as one line drawn through the visible mark with intent. Ilse feels a faint drop in the level for each, and Quince’s ghosted thread lets go too.
- **Lady Reyne:** dove-grey travelling coat, black kid gloves, ebony cane.
- **The infirmary** held nine recovering Quiet students.

### Deviations and choices
- **How the release works:** the magic system requires the original sheet and the surveyor’s own hand, so the sheets had to be physically present. I had Kasimir gather them by Court writ as evidence over the winter. This is in character and explains how a 560-mile-distant release happens “at the tribunal.” I distinguish *unsigning your own thread* (possible at a distance) from Hallam’s *redaction of others’ charters* (which needed proximity, per Ledger A.5).
- **Timing of the land’s return:** released land returns overnight. I left this vague, and Ch 32’s letter confirms the hedge moved on the night of Thaw 28.
- **Wil Ashdown:** given as “the first time anyone on the Wren Stair had heard,” rather than “in two years.” The outline’s Seedtime 1201 onset makes the span close to three years.
- **Steyne’s disgrace:** kept light (the Mess chant, and his public look-away in Ch 30). He keeps his office, as the bible requires.

## Chapter 32 — “Until the Term Is Spent” (Seedtime 3–10, 1204 H.) — 3,899 words

### Summary
On Seedtime 3 Ilse visits the Crypt of Benchmarks: 41 stilled surveyors, their grey palms full of centuries of coins. Hallam sits on an oak bench at the end of the eastern row, bare-handed and slate-grey, facing east. Someone has already left two pennies in his hand. She tells him about Hen, Wil and the swaying Wing, and admits that his kindness was real *and* that he would have let her go Quiet. She leaves one of her last six Long Field marks in his empty right hand. On Seedtime 5, walking the gorse-bright Margin at dawn, Brask reports that Halloway and Calder have measured Level Seven again: the softening has sped up from two hairs a season to nearly two a month now that the Conduit is gone. Steyne’s letter to the Assembly says nothing of it. Nobody knows why it is happening. “Next year, we find out.” Brask gives Ilse her unexpurgated 1183 Sallow field-book. On Seedtime 8 Damask leads her through the Unheld Stacks door, which now stays within a range thanks to Ilse and Kasimir’s open charter of the corridor. Inside, a drifted library holds a second-century working copy of an Ammonite Atlas sheet with a margin note in the founders’ script. Sorry, undescribed, takes the shape of a many-jointed grey hand. It retraces the note in spilled pounce-sand stroke by stroke, separating the figures, and Damask recognizes three of his eleven context-guessed signs in order: *until … the term … spent*. That night Cass, told of it, says Nan’s proverb “rent’s coming due,” and for the first time suspects it means something. A letter from Hesta passes on Agnes Tull’s forgiveness. On Seedtime 10 the four-person party does Morning Survey under the open charter. Kasimir catches Ilse’s dropped chain and his fingers stay over hers a little longer than a chain needs. In the Antechamber the folios carry the old wheat-sheaf watermark, and breathing on the sheet shows nothing beneath. Ilse signs her own mark alone. Final line: “The Wing moved at a quarter past six in the morning, and it wanted to, and so did she.”

### State at end
- **Ilse:** knows everything in Ledger A plus the fragment “until the term is spent.” She has Brask’s Sallow field-book (with Hesta’s letter folded inside), Sorry, and five silver marks.
- **Hallam:** a benchmark in the Crypt, holding her mark.
- **The party:** all four active. Hen is recovered, out of the infirmary since Seedtime 1. Absalom is on crutches.
- **The Zero Sheets:** failing at about two hairs a month. The Assembly is uninformed.
- **The Unheld Stacks door:** stable within a range between the third and fifth windows of a Second Range corridor.
- **Cass:** troubled by “rent.”

### Invented details
- **The Crypt:** 41 benchmarks. Among them, a woman in a two-centuries-old coat with a hand raised, and a boy stilled in 1088 holding a sea-wall. Orle’s orderlies dust the benchmarks weekly and talk to them.
- **Halloway’s paper** on the open charter has 41 footnotes. No lost-stair fines in eleven days.
- **Brask’s field-book:** oilcloth, tied with a bootlace, inscribed *O. Brask — Field-Book — Sallow 1183*. It holds drawings, soundings, “some of what I heard,” and last pages written in the dark after she lost the eye.
- **Brask’s first Contrary Charting lesson:** “Everything you know is a lie.” The post has no salary, room or instructions.
- **The Stacks re-survey:** Ilse and Kasimir did it on Seedtime 1–2, chaining the corridor forty times. Kasimir said: “the first thing I have ever charted that I could not have charted alone.”
- **Damask’s lexicon:** eleven signs guessed from context. The traverse-closing sign reads “spent / paid out,” the span-of-years sign reads “term,” and the boundary-stop sign reads “as far as / until.” The folio is a second-century fair copy of the Ammonite margin sheet.
- **Hesta’s letter** (arrived Seedtime 9): the hedge went back on the night of Thaw 28, and Agnes “had her Pearmain one autumn more than she ought.”
- **Watermark:** Coyle has gone back to the old wheat-sheaf mill.

### Deviations and choices
- **Hallam seated:** the outline says “standing among the others.” He stilled sitting (Ch 30), so he is placed seated at the end of a row, still facing east.
- **How the fragment is read:** to make “half-reads” plausible for an unknown script, the reading comes from Damask’s context-lexicon combined with Sorry’s stroke-order tracing, which separates the run-together figures. Nothing more of the script is read. The Vey nature of the script and of Sorry stays hidden, per Ledger E.
- **Hesta’s letter:** added as a short beat to close the Tull and Agnes thread from Ch 31. It is not in the outline.
- **Final line:** it echoes the book’s opening line. The forced hedge that “did not want to” becomes the Wing moving freely, “and it wanted to, and so did she.” This points toward Book Two, the Sallow return foreshadowed by the field-book and “next year.”
- **Hand-touch wording:** written as “a little longer than a chain needs” rather than “a beat too long,” to save the limited uses of “a beat.”
