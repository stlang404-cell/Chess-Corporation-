# THE FORGER OF VERITY — Book One of *The Long Survey*
## Synopsis, act structure and scene-level chapter outline *(SPOILERS)*

### Synopsis
Ilse Marrow, 23, forges boundary charters for the poor of the Fallowmarch. Her own family's field was legally stolen by a lord's surveyor. When Kasimir Reyne, a young Concordance assessor, spots a hedge she has moved, she spends her savings (the money meant to buy back her family's field) on a coach to Cape Verity. There she takes the deadly open trial called the Walk. Members of the Meridian, the College whose surveyors hold the world still, can only be tried by the College. She passes, with the help of new friends Teo and Cass, and one-eyed Field-Surveyor Odile Brask quietly notices her forger's habits.

Inside the College, whose outer wing must be re-surveyed by students every dawn or it wanders, Ilse finds belonging, rivalry and a strange little ossel called Sorry. She also finds a crime only a forger could recognize. Hidden ghost-lines under the Morning Survey sheets are passing the burden of a failing world onto the students who sign them. Students are going Quiet. Margin villages are coming apart, and the Margin-folk are blamed. To report it, Ilse would have to reveal what she is.

As her friend Hen goes Quiet and Kasimir formally exposes Ilse's past, Ilse, her party, her suspended mentor and her reluctant rival trace the scheme to the College's kindest master. Aurek Hallam has been spending students to hold the heart of the Reach together, and he plans to bind the whole College at the spring equinox. During the Long Measure, stranded on collapsing Drift ground, Ilse discovers the *open map*: a record true enough to admit motion, which a ghost cannot cling to. At the Equinox she supersedes Hallam's folio, breaks his Conduit, and frees the students at the cost of her own secret. Hallam stills under his own burden.

Tried before the Board of Errata, Ilse confesses everything, releases her old forgeries (even the good ones) and is admitted on honest terms as the first student of Contrary Charting in a century. But the Zero Sheets are now failing faster. In the Unheld Stacks, Sorry traces a phrase in the founders' unknown script: *until the term is spent.*

### Act structure
- **Part One — The Walk (Ch 1–7).** Inciting incident (Ch 1–2); threshold (Ch 4); admission (Ch 5); first plot point: Ilse enters the College under watch (Ch 7).
- **Part Two — The Ranges (Ch 8–16).** Belonging, training, first clues. Midpoint shift: Ilse discovers the ghost-lines (Ch 15), then Hen goes Quiet (Ch 16), making it personal.
- **Part Three — Errata (Ch 17–24).** Ilse shares her truth with her friends. Investigation and false suspects. Low point: she wins the Moot, is accused, and Brask is suspended (Ch 20–21). Second plot point: she confides in Hallam (Ch 24), which is a mistake.
- **Part Four — The Long Measure (Ch 25–32).** Alliance, confession, competition, climax (Ch 28–30), resolution (Ch 31) and hook (Ch 32).

### Emotional progression
Defiant solitude (1–3), then terror and wonder (4), fragile belonging (5–11), the first trust in a mentor (14), guilt and secrecy (15–16), shared truth (17), public victory and private ruin (20–21), communal confession (22), misplaced trust (24), betrayal recognized (25–27), courage in the open (28–30), and finally honesty, cost and home (31–32).

## PART ONE — THE WALK

### Ch 1 — "A Very Confident Opinion" *(Reaping 14–15, 1203 H.; Wick Fallow)*
- **Opening line:** "The hedge moved at a quarter past two in the morning, and it did not want to."
- **Scene 1:** Night, in Widow Agnes Tull's orchard strip. Ilse finishes the forgery: chain by shuttered lantern, levelglass, blind ink, and Quince's dead mark ghosted under her work. Show the craft in detail and her pride in it. She attests. The hawthorn hedge *moves*: roots whisper through the soil, thorns scrape, blackbirds startle, and the hedge settles two rods west, returning the orchard strip. Ilse is wrung out and exultant, with a headache behind her ears. Agnes, in nightcap and shawl, gives her eggs and three silver marks. Through Agnes we learn that Corwin Fask's survey last spring took the strip.
- **Scene 2:** Morning in Hobbet. Her mother Hesta at the laundry: love expressed as disapproval. Market gossip: a "College gentleman in grey gloves, ever so polite" was at the parish office two days ago, looking at the Little Wick lane charter. Ilse's savings tin is mentioned: 41 marks toward buying back the Long Field. Her memory of the Long Field and her father, kept *brief*.
- **Scene 3:** Dusk, on the Little Wick lane. Ilse is walking home when the lane **unravels**: ruts swim, the cobbles at the ford drift apart like loose knitting, and a milestone sinks to its shoulders. A carter's wagon tips. Ilse and the carter (Tam Brewin) get the horse free. This is *unheard of* in the Fallowmarch, deep in the Reach. Ilse's level screams. She feels the hold *let go*, as if a charter had simply ceased to exist.
- **Scene 4:** Late. Ilse cuts back past Widow Tull's at night to check her hedge (a forger's paranoia). A young man in a Concordance assessor's grey-blue coat is crouched by it with a dark lantern and a notebook, measuring the root-tear. He looks up. **End line:** "This hedge," he said, in the voice of someone who expected to be believed, "has moved."

### Ch 2 — "The Assessor's Notebook" *(Reaping 15–17)*
- **Scene 1:** Continuous. Kasimir introduces himself formally (junior assessor attached to a Concordance inspection of the Fallowmarch voids). Ilse plays a curious local. A sparring dialogue in which they test each other. He notes: fresh root-tear, gall-ink smell, and a hedge cannot move two rods in a season without a charter. She notes his beautiful boots and his assumptions. He asks her name; she gives it truthfully, because lying would be checkable.
- **Scene 2:** Next day. Kasimir and the inspection's senior assessor, Pollard (bored), examine the parish chest. Ilse had filed the forged amendment properly. Kasimir requests a Court "drawing of the mark" inquiry on the Tull charter. That would reveal the ghosted mark of Quince, a dead man, and from there, inevitably, her. The procedure takes about three weeks. She overhears it from the vestry door.
- **Scene 3:** Ilse and Hesta at night. Hesta: "You could run." Ilse thinks it through like a survey. Where could she go that the Court can't follow? Collegiate privilege. The Walk: the autumn equinox, seven days away, 560 miles west. The express mail coach takes four days and costs 38 marks. That is the Long Field money. The choice, and its price: buying her freedom with her father's field. Hesta's farewell is practical (bread, the good coat, "write, you wretch").
- **Scene 4:** Dawn of Reaping 17 in Hobbet coach-yard. Kasimir is at the inn window across the yard, packing to leave for the Meridian himself: he is a new Patent first-year. Neither knows yet that the other is bound for the same place. (He sees her board. She sees him see her.) On the coach roof, the Fallowmarch slides away. **End:** an image of the hedgerows she knows, all of them held by someone else's ink.

### Ch 3 — "Cape Verity" *(Reaping 17–22)*
- **Scene 1:** The journey, compressed in vivid fragments: the Great Meridian Road; Tessary's copper roofs glimpsed and the Zero Stone obelisk; westward the land grows "thinner," the level in her ears changing. Fellow passengers: a hedge-preacher who swears on the Atlas. The coach skirts the Margin's edge on the last day, and the milestones there have benchmark arrows freshly re-cut.
- **Scene 2:** Arrival at Verity Cross, dusk of Reaping 21. First sight of the Meridian on the headland: honey stone on the Held Side, and the Drift Wing trailing out over the dark heath like a sleeping animal, lights in its windows. As she watches, one lit window *slides* a few feet along the dark. A tavern (the Chain & Anchor). She overhears students. Sleeps badly.
- **Scene 3:** Before dawn on Reaping 22, at the Lantern Gate. 19 Walkers. Introduce **Teo** (huge, apologizing, sweating, carrying a pie "for the road, or for my wake") and **Cass** (Margin-folk braids and route-cords, mocking the paper-walls, flicking a sling stone up and down). Also Yara Penhallow (a cheerful farmer's daughter who talks to Ilse) and Oskar Fell. Morning Survey parties are passing in the half-dark, chains clinking: a glimpse of the ritual.
- **Scene 4:** Brask arrives with the Long Lie. A memorable briefing: "Go to the cairn. Come back. Bring a true survey. Don't die; it's paperwork." Rules: one chain, one levelglass, one sightvane, one plane-table board, one water flask, no assistance from non-Walkers; return by dusk tomorrow. Hallam stands beside her with a kindly blessing (gloves). In the gallery above the Gate are the watching Patent students, Kasimir among them. Their eyes meet. He knows why she's here. Brask notes the look. **End:** they walk out under the lantern into fog.

### Ch 4 — "The Walk" *(Reaping 22)*
- **Scene 1:** The Margin: heath, gorse, salt-pools, and ground that "breathes." Ilse discovers that her chain-work is perfect but slow, and the ground shifts between stations. She learns to measure in quick sequence and to close her traverse fast. Cass moves with uncanny ease, reading grain ("the ground's leaning, can't you feel it?"). Ilse can feel it, a little. She is irritated and fascinated.
- **Scene 2:** The widening creek. Teo is stuck where a creek keeps widening each time he looks. He has vertigo and is near panic. Ilse helps: she makes him calculate (his calm) while she chains. They cross together and Cass mocks them both, then helps. A tentative alliance: "No assistance from non-Walkers. We're all Walkers."
- **Scene 3:** Fog and sound. A low song comes up through their feet. **A sounder breaches** out of a hillside 200 yards off. Turf and stones slide from its grey back, an eye the size of a cartwheel turns, and the hill closes behind it like water. Awe. Cass is reverent. Teo weeps. Ilse feels her level go silent. (This is the book's first great wonder image.)
- **Scene 4:** Yara calls out from the fog that she has found a shortcut. Ilse shouts at her to wait. Nothing. She is gone.
- **Scene 5:** The cairn at dusk, with a cairn-crow on top. A night camp with Teo and Cass: Teo's pie, shared. Cass sings a Tenting road-song, a route that changes with the moon: "the road to Corrow goes by the high stone when the moon is new." Ilse thinks of maps that move and dismisses it. Talk of why each came. Ilse lies ("I wanted to be a surveyor"). Cass: "Liar. Nice one, though." **End:** Ilse awake, listening to the ground breathing.

### Ch 5 — "A True Survey" *(Reaping 23)*
- **Scene 1:** The return. The route has changed overnight. Ilse must re-survey half her traverse. At a stream bank, her measurements don't close by 14 links. Under exhaustion she **"corrects" the bank** on her draft, making it consistent and beautiful, the way a forger would, rather than recording the anomaly. (Clue for Brask. Make it vivid, and make it *feel* like the right decision to Ilse.)
- **Scene 2:** They reach the Lantern Gate at four in the afternoon. Hallam welcomes them warmly ("old Sallow frostbite" when Teo asks about his gloves; clue). Ilse attests her survey under the arch.
- **Scene 3:** Judgment in the Hall of Instruments. Brask examines surveys with a lens. She spends a long silent time on Ilse's stream bank. She passes her: "True enough." Teo passes narrowly. Cass's survey is ugly but true. 11 pass. Yara and Oskar Fell are missing. Brask takes the Long Lie and goes back out alone into the dusk to search; Hallam protests mildly. Ilse watches her go.
- **Scene 4:** Ilse is now a Member. The steward hands her a Member's ring, plain iron with the meridian line. **Relief.** Kasimir intercepts her in the Old Court: "Congratulations, Miss Marrow. I have written to the parish of Hobbet asking that the Tull inquiry be forwarded to the Board of Errata." So she is safe from the Court, but not from the College. **End:** she has bought herself a slower trial.

### Ch 6 — "Benchmarking" *(Reaping 24–30)*
- **Scene 1:** Party formation in the Mess. Hen Solberg plonks down: "you looked like you knew what you were doing, and the others looked like they wanted to marry each other." The party is Ilse, Teo, Cass and Hen. Rooms in the Second Range, on the **Wren Stair** (mockery). The room moves slightly at night: a wardrobe that was against the wall is a foot from it by morning.
- **Scene 2:** The first Morning Survey at First Bell. Comic disaster. The Long Gallery is two feet longer and the stair has turned; they argue; Hen counts; Cass says "the ground's leaning"; they fix it with three minutes to spare and sign the folios in the **Register Antechamber** (detail: the clerk Merrin Coyle with ink-stained cuffs, the thick folios, and the rule that "Master Hallam likes every sheet signed here, not in the Ranges"). Other parties' failures: a lost stair, a door opening onto gorse.
- **Scene 3:** The Ossel Fair at Verity Cross gate. Ilse steps on a small grey indeterminate creature ("Sorry!"). It becomes a small grey cat with too-big ears, because she says so ("it's a — a cat? A small grey cat with ridiculous ears"). It follows her. Cass explains ossels, and also the ethics ("you lot describe things into whatever you want"). Ilse buys it for a penny from a Tenting girl. It's named Sorry because that's all it answers to. Teo calls it a ferret and it flickers, which distresses it, so they agree: cat.
- **Scene 4:** Benchmarking. The Old Wall. Brass arrows, hundreds of marks. Each link chooses a witness-mark and cuts it. Ilse hesitates, then registers the **wren’s foot**, the unregistered mark she has signed her forgeries with for three years, because it is the only mark that has ever felt like hers. (Reckless, honest.) She thinks of Quince’s mark ghosted beneath it all those times, and of the dead man carrying her weight. Kasimir, cutting his own mark nearby, looks at hers a moment too long. Her first honest attestation: a paving stone in the Old Court, which she must keep in place for a year. Halloway presides, stammering, and fluent on numbers. Hallam is present, kind, and says she has "a draftswoman's hand." **End:** Ilse's first true signature, and how *light* it feels.

### Ch 7 — "Fieldwork" *(Gleaning 1–2)*
- **Scene 1:** The first Fieldwork class in a leaking shed at the Margin end of the First Range. Brask's teaching style, shown through an exercise: each student must estimate the distance to a gorse bush and then *state how they might be wrong*. Brask: "If you can't say how you might be wrong, you haven't measured anything." Ilse's answer is excellent. Brask is unimpressed out loud and impressed in deed: after class she makes Ilse her Fieldwork assistant, carrying instruments and cleaning chains. Oskar Fell was found by Brask on Reaping 25, half-mad; Yara still lost. Brask is grim and tired.
- **Scene 2:** Charting class (taught by Hallam's adjunct). Kasimir's flawless charter of a cobble, admired by all. Ilse's is competent. Piety Mercer is snide about Walkers. Orla Devane is amused.
- **Scene 3:** Kasimir and Ilse in the Library of Record, the first true confrontation. He has no proof yet: the parish replied that the charter is "in order," and the Court inquiry must now be refiled with the Board. He will be patient. Their philosophies clash: he says law protects the weak from the powerful; she says law *is* the powerful. He: "I do not dislike you, Miss Marrow. I dislike that you are good at something you ought not to be able to do." She doesn't tell him about the Long Field.
- **Scene 4:** Evening on the Wren Stair. The party's first real conversation as friends: Teo's spirit-stove, Hen's laugh, Cass's feet on the table. Sorry eats a bottle of ink. A note under the door, in Brask's hand: "Walk with me tomorrow, Marrow. Before the bell." **End Part One.**

## PART TWO — THE RANGES

### Ch 8 — "The Quiet" *(Gleaning 3)*
- **Scene 1:** A pre-dawn walk in the Margin with Brask. She teaches reading the grain: how gorse leans, how dew pools on high-hold ground, how the level changes pitch. She uses the Long Lie. Ilse notices that the stilled left hand doesn't bend. Brask won't discuss it. She asks Ilse why she *really* came. Ilse gives a half-truth: her family's field. Brask accepts it without comment. Brask on Yara: "The Margin keeps what it keeps."
- **Scene 2:** Back at the College, in the infirmary corridor (Ilse is delivering instruments for Brask), she sees **Wil Ashdown**, a second-year, sitting on a bench and staring. His eyes are flat. The fingers of his *right* (signing) hand are slate-grey. Dr. Sabeth Orle, brisk and frowning. A second Quiet student is in the beds. Orle mutters that it isn't Drift-fatigue, "whatever the Provost likes to call it."
- **Scene 3:** At assembly in the Mess, Provost Steyne gives a smooth announcement: "a seasonal Drift-fatigue among a few students; rest and broth." Students joke about it. Cass: "Broth. Road and rain." Ilse watches Wil being led away. Hallam sits at high table, gloved, eating little.
- **Scene 4:** Night. Ilse can't sleep. She practices her wren's foot on scrap paper and notices how much better her own mark feels than Quince's ever did. Sorry sleeps on her feet. **End:** quiet and warm, with the image of Wil's grey fingers lingering.

### Ch 9 — "Rain on the Register" *(Gleaning 10)*
- **Scene 1:** Morning Survey in driving rain. The Long Gallery drips. Chains are slick and the party is miserable and funny. At the Antechamber, Sorry (smuggled in Ilse's coat) knocks a cup of Coyle's tea across the folios. On the damp sheet, under Ilse's fresh wren's foot, a **faint second line** shows for a moment, pale and violet, then dries invisible. She assumes she smudged it. (A forger's unease she suppresses.)
- **Scene 2:** Hallam's Principles of Hold lecture in the Antechamber. He lets her hold a 400-year-old charter of the Ammonite Hills. Wonder at the weight of old hold in the paper, which hums in her ears. His warmth.
- **Scene 3:** Afterwards Hallam keeps her back: she has a draftswoman's hand, and would she help with copying in the Register two evenings a week? Paid. Access to Levels One to Three. Ilse is flattered. He's the first adult since Quince to praise her openly. He mentions gently that Brask is "a brilliant woman broken by grief; don't let her break you, my dear."
- **Scene 4:** She tells Brask about the Register post. Brask's reaction is a long silence, then "Mind the stairs." **End.**

### Ch 10 — "The Six Laws" *(Gleaning 14–20)*
- **Scene 1:** Hallam lectures the Laws: Witness, Fidelity, Precedence, Attestation, Burden, Latitude. Ilse waits for the seventh. It doesn't come. Kasimir asks about "contrary records." Hallam deflects gracefully: "Forgery, Mr. Reyne, is not a law of nature but a failure of character." Laughter. Ilse burns.
- **Scene 2:** Mensuration with Halloway. Error theory: probable error, least squares in simple form. Ilse, a self-taught genius at the practical, is lost in the mathematics. Humiliation at the board. Halloway is kind: "Error is not sin. *Unrecorded* error is."
- **Scene 3:** The Errata Society in the Mess kitchen after hours. Teo tutors Ilse by explaining error theory with pie crust and Lisk bread arrogance. Friendship deepens. Hen and Teo flirt over a stove (Teo's blush). Cass arrives with smuggled cider and an argument about whether maps are theft.
- **Scene 4:** Ilse, alone, does thirty practice problems and gets twenty-six right. **End:** something earned.

### Ch 11 — "The Ladder" *(Gleaning 28)*
- **Set piece.** The first-term practical. Each party is sealed into an unstable chamber in the Third Range, totally dark, and has two hours to produce an attested survey by instruments alone. The room's latitude means it slowly changes while they work.
- Party strategy: Teo *sounds* the walls by knocking and listening for depth; Cass reads grain by touch on the floor; Hen counts paces and seconds; Ilse drafts by touch with knotted strings (Cass's route-cords adapted) and then transfers the result to paper by feel. Mid-exam crisis: the room *turns*, and their baseline is lost. Ilse realizes they should measure the *rate* of drift and record it rather than fighting it (a seed of the open map that she doesn't recognize). Halloway is delighted.
- Results: Kasimir's party first (flawless). Ilse's party second, the best Walker result in years. Halloway is effusive about "the rate annotation." Kasimir, privately to Ilse: "Interesting." Celebration at the Chain & Anchor in Verity Cross. Teo dances with Hen. Cass with three people. Ilse watches and is happy, and that frightens her. **End.**

### Ch 12 — "Watermarks" *(Fogmonth 2–6)*
- **Scene 1:** Ilse in the Register copying for Hallam: Levels One and Two, lamp-lit stacks, the hum of old hold. Wonder detail: charters stacked by parish, each a held place. Merrin Coyle is nervous and pleasant. She notices that the Morning Survey folios filed since **1201 H.** carry a different watermark (a three-arch bridge rather than the old wheat-sheaf). She asks idly. Coyle: "Master Hallam changed the supplier — cheaper, from Lisk." It's just a detail. She files it in her forger's mind.
- **Scene 2:** In a Second Range corridor at night, a strange door appears that wasn't there: the **Unheld Stacks** door, oak with a brass plate reading FAR RANGE — CLOSED BY ORDER 1105. She reaches for it. Archivist Damask stops her, delighted rather than angry, and tells the history of the Stacks: "no one has re-surveyed the way in ninety-eight years; it comes and goes like a cat." He invites her to his Palimpsest class.
- **Scene 3:** A letter from Hesta: Lord Saffery's surveyor Corwin Fask has been asking about the Tull hedge, and the parish clerk says an inquiry is coming "from the Big College." Also: "your Long Field money — I hope it bought you something." Ilse is wrecked by this privately.
- **Scene 4:** Brask's Fieldwork. A night exercise on the Margin reading wayhorn antlers. Brask about the Drift: "It's not trying to kill you. It isn't trying anything. That's the frightening part, and it's also a lie, but you're not ready for that one." **End.**

### Ch 13 — "Brine End" *(Fogmonth 9–10)*
- **Scene 1:** News: a void at Brine End, a Margin village six miles south. Its well has moved 40 yards and two cottages have slid. The College sends Fieldwork (Brask and assistant). Hallam is away "on inspection" in the Salt Steps that week (clue). A cart ride with Brask. Her silence. Pipe unlit.
- **Scene 2:** Brine End. The unravelling close up: a garden wall unknitting stone by stone, chickens confused, a woman weeping over a doorstep that faces the wrong way. Brask checks the parish copy, which is fine, and then sends a crow to the Register for the master. The reply comes by nightfall: the **master charter for Brine End is missing** from the Register.
- **Scene 3:** Villagers blame "the Tenting," because a Margin-folk wagon (Wainwright clan) is camped nearby. A confrontation. Brask speaks to the Tenting elder with respect and in their idiom. Ilse sees a new Brask. The elder says "rent's coming due" and Brask goes very still.
- **Scene 4:** Dusk. **First hollowing.** A blur on the heath that won't come into focus, sliding toward the freshly re-attested patch where Brask and Ilse worked. Stones slump where it passes. Ilse's level drops into silence. Ilse touches its edge and loses five minutes (un-pinning). Brask drives it off with a low Tenting song, in a voice Ilse has never heard her use. **End:** Brask: "Not a word of that song to the Provost."

### Ch 14 — "What Brask Knew" *(Fogmonth 11)*
- **Scene 1:** Brask's cottage at Verity Cross (she lives outside the College): books, crow feathers, a cold hearth. She makes tea badly. She confronts Ilse: the Walk survey's stream bank. "You corrected it. Forgers correct. Surveyors record." Ilse braces for expulsion.
- **Scene 2:** "Good," says Brask. "So was I, once — every surveyor who ever chose what to leave out." She tells the **Sallow story** in part: seven went, four were lost, *something* tried to speak to them, and she lost her eye to a hollowing. Hallam carried her twenty leagues. The Board called it hallucination. Brask: "The map is a promise, not a cage. Know which one you're making."
- **Scene 3:** The deal. Brask will keep Ilse's secret on one condition: no more lies on any College sheet. Ilse agrees, and means it. Brask also says, as a warning, that Kasimir has asked her about Ilse.
- **Scene 4:** Walking back through the dark, Ilse realizes she has just been *trusted*, and doesn't know where to put it. **End.**

### Ch 15 — "Blind Ink" *(Fogmonth 18–20)*
- **Scene 1:** The violet line keeps nagging at her. Ilse retrieves her forger's kit from the lining of her father's coat. She mixes a damp-lens: a glass with a film of alum-water, the old forger's way to read blind ink.
- **Scene 2:** Night in the Register Antechamber (she has a key as copyist). Under the lens, **every Morning Survey sheet since 1201** has a ghost-line beneath each student's mark: a hidden trace in perfect registration. Her horror, *technically* understood: "Someone is ghosting the whole College." Only a forger would see this. To report it, she must explain how she knows.
- **Scene 3:** A sound. Kasimir is in the Antechamber too, by the far stacks, with a lamp. Tense mutual suspicion: each catches the other where they shouldn't be. She hides the lens. He accuses her of tampering with folios; she accuses him of spying for the Court. Neither yields. He notes that she is "very frightened for someone who is merely copying." **End** on stalemate, and his footsteps going.

### Ch 16 — "Hen" *(Fogmonth 25 – Frost 2)*
- **Scene 1:** Over days, Hen flattens. First she misses jokes; then her laugh goes; then she answers in short declaratives. Teo notices first. Cass fights with her trying to make her angry, and it doesn't work.
- **Scene 2:** Morning Survey. Hen insists on signing extra sheets for the party ("I'm fastest"). Ilse sees the grey on Hen's right fingertips. The realization lands: Hen is the most diligent signer. The ghost.
- **Scene 3:** Hen is taken to the infirmary. Dr. Orle, grim. Ilse at Hen's bedside: Hen says "I can't remember why things were funny." Ilse's guilt: she knew about the lines for two weeks and said nothing to protect herself.
- **Scene 4:** Ilse walks the Drift Wing at night and decides. **End:** she knocks on the Wren Stair door and tells Teo and Cass, "I have to tell you something about myself, and then something about this College, and you'll like neither." **End Part Two.**

## PART THREE — ERRATA

### Ch 17 — "Confession in the Mess Kitchen" *(Frost 3)*
- **Scene 1:** She tells them everything: Quince, the hedges, Quince's ghosted mark, the Tull hedge, Kasimir, and the ghost-lines. Teo is hurt that she lied, then steadfast: "You lied about yourself. You're not lying about this." Cass laughs: "Knew you were Tenting at heart. Paper-wall with a hedge-thief's hands."
- **Scene 2:** The plan. They can't stop signing; refusing attracts attention. Ilse teaches them a forger's trick: sign with the mark *off-register*, a deliberate skew so the ghost-line can't take. It is a small lie on a College sheet, and she breaks her promise to Brask almost immediately. She knows it. Risk: the party's stretch might drift slightly.
- **Scene 3:** Testing with Sorry. Sorry eats ordinary ink eagerly but recoils from Morning Survey folio ink (blind ink has quicksilver, which ossels avoid). Then, startled by a dropped pan, Sorry becomes **a small grey heron-like bird that no one described**. Then it settles back into a cat. Cass is unsettled: "Ossels don't do that."
- **Scene 4:** Next morning the off-register signatures work. Their stretch holds, wobbly. Teo whispers: "We just lied to the building." **End.**

### Ch 18 — "Drift-Fatigue" *(Frost 5)*
- **Scene 1:** Visiting Hen. Dr. Orle takes Ilse aside: the grey always starts on the signing hand. "Fatigue doesn't choose a hand." Steyne visited and told Orle to stop writing to Tessary about it. Orle becomes an ally.
- **Scene 2:** Cass is summoned before the proctors and the Provost about Brine End. Margin-folk sabotage is suspected, and Cass was seen leaving the College that week (to visit kin). Steyne is smooth and insinuating. Cass is furious and dignified. Ilse waits outside. Cass emerges white with rage: "They think we're unmaking the ground. *We.*"
- **Scene 3:** On the roof of the Second Range at night, Cass and Ilse. Their friendship deepens. Cass on the Tenting's view: "You paper-walls think if it isn't written, it isn't there." **End.**

### Ch 19 — "Corrow" *(Frost 7–8)*
- **Scene 1:** A crow brings word to Cass: Corrow, the Tenter wagon's winter camp eight miles southeast in the Margin, is coming apart. Brask authorizes a "field exercise" for Ilse, Cass and Teo.
- **Scene 2:** Corrow: painted wagons, a camp where the ground is sliding gently like a tablecloth being pulled. Nan Ottery Tenter, Cass's grandmother: tiny, ancient, a pipe, and a voice like a gate. Hospitality: stew and argument. Nan sings road-songs that *describe routes as changing*. Ilse hears for the first time that these are **maps**, maps that move. Nan: "Paper says where a thing is. Song says where a thing *goes*."
- **Scene 3:** Nan: "a gentle old man with grey hands came asking the way to the Corrow stone three nights back, polite as a parson, and the next night the ground let go." Ilse goes cold. *Grey hands.* She refuses the thought: Hallam is kind, Hallam was away... Corrow had a College charter (granted 1190 under an old treaty) that is now missing. "Rent's coming due," Nan says, and Cass asks her what it means. Nan: "What it says."
- **Scene 4:** They help the camp move to firmer ground at night by singing the route. Ilse walks a road that moves and finds it doesn't frighten her. **End.**

### Ch 20 — "The Moot" *(Frost 10)*
- **Set piece.** The Concordance Moot in the Old Court. The pairing is drawn: Ilse against Kasimir. Magistra Calder presides with dramatic pauses. The Moot-stone is a basalt block on a sand bed. Each duelist surveys and charts the stone to a position of their choice within its latitude. Both attest at once. Precedence decides, and the stone moves where the higher-fidelity map says.
- Kasimir's strategy: a flawless point charter, to a hair. Ilse's: she reads where the sand *wants* the stone (its natural drift tendency) and charts it there, *exactly true to the latitude*. That makes her map more faithful to the land as it actually is. The tension of simultaneous attestation, the crowd, the stone grinding three inches toward Ilse's mark. **She wins.** Calder: "Fidelity to the ground, Mr. Reyne, not to the page."
- Kasimir's face. Hallam applauds and watches her a little too long. Brask, at the edge of the crowd, says only "Hm."
- Afterwards Kasimir, white: "That was not a survey. That was a forgery that happened to be true." **End.**

### Ch 21 — "The Accusation" *(Frost 12–14)*
- **Scene 1:** Kasimir files a **formal accusation of forgery** with the Board of Errata. He has obtained the Tull charter via the Court: it bears Quince's ghosted mark, dated three years after Quince’s death. It is undeniable. Ilse is summoned. Her tribunal is set for after the spring equinox (the Board sits quarterly). She may continue her studies pending it.
- **Scene 2:** Brask is called and *admits she knew* and kept it from the Board. She is **suspended** from teaching and barred from the College grounds pending the tribunal. Ilse is devastated. Brask, packing the shed: "You'll do the Long Measure without me. Good. You'd have leaned."
- **Scene 3:** Ilse and Kasimir fight in the snowing Old Court, the fiercest scene between them. She tells him about the **Long Field**: her father, sixty yards, the well, the appeal, his death. "You think I don't know what a forged boundary costs? Fask's was legal." He is shaken. He holds: "Then the law must be fixed. Not ignored. If everyone with a grievance and a pen..." "Most people with a grievance don't have a pen, Mr. Reyne." **End:** he walks away, and he doesn't withdraw the accusation.

### Ch 22 — "Errata Night" *(Frost 22)*
- **Set piece.** Midwinter. The whole College in the Mess, candles in hundreds, mulled cider, the Sounders' Choir singing low (Teo among them). The ritual: anyone may stand and confess an error, and the College answers "Recorded."
- Comic and tender confessions: Halloway confesses a miscalculation from 1189 that still bothers him; Orla confesses that she reads Piety's letters; Cass stands and says "I love the Big House, may the Road forgive me," to roars. Teo confesses his fear of heights, which everyone knows.
- Ilse stands. Silence. "I've lied on paper. For years. Well." The College answers, "Recorded." The ritual absorbs it as it absorbs everything. Kasimir, across the hall, doesn't say it. Then he does, quietly, a beat after everyone else.
- After, on the Wren Stair, Teo shows Ilse his fingertips: faintly grey. The off-register trick has slowed it but not stopped it, because his earlier sheets are still ghosted. Stakes rise. **End.**

### Ch 23 — "The Infirmary Ledger" *(Deepwinter 3–5)*
- **Scene 1:** Dr. Orle shows Ilse her ledger: the first Quiet case, Wil Ashdown, was in **Seedtime 1201**. Ilse connects it: the watermark changed in 1201. Someone changed the paper, and the students began going grey.
- **Scene 2:** Steyne summons Ilse to his study (fine carpets, a model of the Meridian). He is kindly menacing: he knows about the accusation and offers to have it "managed" if she stops "upsetting Dr. Orle" and stops asking questions in the Register. "The College's survival depends on calm, Miss Marrow." She leaves certain that Steyne is behind it (red herring).
- **Scene 3:** She tells her party. Cass: "The Provost. Road and rain." Teo isn't sure: "He's a coward, not a — whatever this is." The investigation now points at Steyne. **End.**

### Ch 24 — "Raking Light" *(Deepwinter 15–17)*
- **Scene 1:** Damask's Palimpsest class, which wonderfully shows a different way of looking: raking light, a candle held at a grazing angle, revealing erased layers. The old man's delight: "Oh! Look, *look*." Ilse uses raking light that night on a Morning Survey sheet she filched. The ghost-lines don't all point the same way, and they don't end. They run *toward a single root direction*, like a river delta reversed. They are channelled into one place: a **conduit**.
- **Scene 2:** Hallam invites her to tea in his study behind the Antechamber (fire, books, a sounder-ivory paperweight, his gloved hands around a cup). Conflicted, she almost tells him everything. He is the one person with authority who has been kind. She tells him a *portion*: the ghost-lines exist (not how she knows, and not her forgery). He listens gravely, is grateful, promises to investigate personally, and asks her to tell no one, "for the students' sake; panic kills more than any illness." (Chilling in retrospect.)
- **Scene 3:** She leaves lighter. Next morning, the filched sheet is gone from inside her Mensuration textbook. Sorry is agitated, and the room's wardrobe has drifted in the night. **End:** someone has been in her room. **End Part Three.**

## PART FOUR — THE LONG MEASURE

### Ch 25 — "Kasimir's Ledger" *(Candlemonth 20)*
- **Scene 1:** Kasimir corners Ilse in the crow-loft (Mother Nettle cackles at them). He has been investigating the voids for his mother's Court since before term. In his notebook of errors he has compared **void dates with Register travel warrants**. Brine End, Corrow, Little Wick lane (yes, *her* lane) and four others: every void followed an inspection tour signed by **Hallam**. He came to *her* because she's the only one who would understand forgery evidence. Uneasy, prickly alliance.
- **Scene 2:** She shows him the raking light and a fresh sheet. He sees the ghosting, the crime he hates most, inside the College. His fastidious horror. He also notices his own mark ghosted. The fear of stilling cracks open: his father, the family chapel. A confession of his own.
- **Scene 3:** It's Hallam, not Steyne. Ilse's grief: the kindness was real, and the man is this. She remembers she told him about the lines. **End:** "He knows I know."

### Ch 26 — "What the Sallow Gave Back" *(Candlemonth 22)*
- **Scene 1:** Ilse and Kasimir go to Brask's cottage (Brask is suspended but at home). Brask hears them out. Her memory: Hallam came back from the Sallow "certain. He was never certain before. He was the best doubter I knew." She realizes. Grief and fury, controlled.
- **Scene 2:** Strategy. They lack proof that the Board would accept from a suspended mentor, an accused forger and the Chancellor's son. Hallam has 40 years of trust. Brask: "The Equinox Charter. Everyone signs. If I wanted to bind a whole College at once, that's where." The Long Measure is in four weeks, and the winning party presents its charter at the Gate.
- **Scene 3:** The roof of the Old Court. Ilse and Kasimir, cold stars. An almost-moment. His father stilled at fifty; he writes perfect charters so the land never strains against him; he is terrified of being still. She tells him she's terrified of being hollow. They don't kiss. He calls her "Ilse" once. **End.**

### Ch 27 — "Registration Guides" *(Thaw 12–20)*
- **Scene 1:** Cass and Teo trap Merrin Coyle in the crow-loft with Mother Nettle's cheerful complicity. Coyle breaks. He prints the folios with "registration guides" on Hallam's instructions. Hallam has personally prepared the **Equinox folio**. In Hallam's study there is a master sheet he never lets Coyle touch, the **Conduit Sheet**. Then the scale: Coyle knows from Hallam's mutterings that **the Zero Sheets are softening**. "He's trying to save Tessary. He says the center is coming apart." Moral weight: this isn't greed. It's the world.
- **Scene 2:** The party, Kasimir and Brask (by crow) argue the ethics. If Hallam is right, stopping him endangers a million people. Ilse: "Then we find another way to hold her. We don't pay for it with Hen." The plan: (1) win the Long Measure so their charter is the basis of the Equinox Charter; (2) draft that charter so a ghost can't take, which Ilse doesn't yet know how to do; (3) Cass, Kasimir and Coyle seize the Conduit during the ceremony. A rule problem: Hen is in the infirmary, and a party must have four. Kasimir can't join; he has his own party. They will compete as three under the short-party penalty (an extra hour).
- **Scene 3:** **The Lantern Count**, the eve before the Measure (Thaw 20). Every student walks the Drift Wing with a lantern, counting steps aloud, 300 voices holding the Wing. A beautiful communal scene. Ilse counts and feels the College held by voice, not ink, and something nags at her (the road-songs). **End.**

### Ch 28 — "The Long Measure" *(Thaw 21)*
- **Set piece.** At dawn, twelve parties set out from the Lantern Gate for the Tern Stack, 3 leagues out. Detailed Drift terrain: standing rain, a skein (an airborne river-loop with skein-eels), folded warm air with fold-moths, glasswrack fields ringing. Sorry rides in Ilse's coat.
- Hallam, at the start, wishes all parties well. To Ilse he says "Come home safe, my dear," and she can't tell if it's a threat.
- Hollowings are more numerous than anyone has seen, drawn by the hundreds of fresh attestations along the course. One party turns back.
- The Tern Stack at nightfall: the beacon cairn on top must be re-attested by one party member. **Teo climbs** the 140-foot stack in the dark, terrified, counting aloud, with Cass calling the grain up to him and Ilse holding the line. He does it. (His arc lands.)
- Kasimir's party reaches the Stack second. A wary nod between rivals. **End:** they turn for home, and the ground behind them is moving.

### Ch 29 — "The Open Map" *(Thaw 21–22, night)*
- **Scene 1:** The return at night. A swarm of hollowings and a ridge that's slumping. Kasimir's party is trapped in a fold where the ground is coming loose. Absalom has a broken leg, and Piety is screaming that it's the Walkers' fault. Ilse's party goes to them, losing all chance of winning.
- **Scene 2:** A hollowing attack. Cass, shouting, *describes* Sorry: "the biggest, ugliest, fiercest dog that ever walked the Margin!" Sorry becomes it, briefly enormous, and drives the hollowing off. Then it collapses, back to a tiny grey shape, asleep in Ilse's hands. (It hated being a dog.)
- **Scene 3:** The insight. The ground keeps sliding, and every charter Kasimir attests tears, because the ground's latitude is enormous and his point charters fight it. The hollowings are drawn to the fighting. Ilse puts it together: Cass's road-song (where a thing *goes*), Brask's promise-not-cage, the Ladder's rate annotation, the Moot (fidelity to the ground), and the Law of Fidelity itself. **A record that is true about motion is the truest record there is.** She drafts the ridge as a *range*: "this ground lies between here and here, and moves thus." It needs exact limits, so Kasimir's flawless hand supplies the precision while her latitude-sense supplies the bounds. Both attest. The ground stops fighting. It *settles* into a slow, gentle sway, like a boat at anchor. The hollowings, which flee moving ground, drift away. Silence, then Cass laughing.
- **Scene 4:** Realization: an open charter can't register with a fixed ghost-line. "A ghost needs the lines to sit still." This is the weapon.
- **Scene 5:** They reach the Lantern Gate before dawn as a merged party of seven, the only party to complete the course. The judges argue over whether a merged charter is valid, and whether an "open" charter is a charter at all. Calder rules by the Law of Fidelity: it is true; it holds; it stands. It will be the basis for the Equinox Charter. Hallam looks at the open charter for a long time. **End:** he understands what it does.

### Ch 30 — "Under the Lantern Gate" *(Thaw 22, equinox dawn)*
- **The climax.** The whole College gathers under the Lantern Gate. Hallam, Master of the Register, presents the Equinox folio. He proposes, reasonably and gently, to set the open charter aside "pending study" and use his prepared folio "for safety; the Wing must hold." Steyne nods.
- Ilse objects publicly and tells the truth: the ghost-lines, the Quiet, the voids. She *names herself a forger* to explain how she knows. Murmurs. Hallam is calm and sorrowful: "Our accused forger, my dear friends, would have you doubt the Register on the eve of her tribunal." Many believe him. Steyne wavers.
- Meanwhile (Ilse knows only from glimpses and Sorry's agitation), Cass, Kasimir and Coyle go for the Conduit in the study. It's locked with an annotated lock that can't be forced.
- Hallam signs his folio first, then calls the proctors to sign. Ilse, knowing the Law of Precedence, steps forward and **attests the open charter of the Drift Wing first in time and higher in fidelity.** The Drift Wing *breathes* in the dawn, rooms swaying gently and settling, and Hallam's folio loses registration. His ghost won't take. Gasps.
- Kasimir runs in with the Conduit Sheet (Coyle knew where the key was kept: inside Hallam's sounder-ivory paperweight). Hallam, desperate, finally drops the mask, and it isn't villainy but grief: "You'll kill her. The center is failing. I measured it. Do you know what it is to *measure* the end of the world and be told to keep it quiet?" He looks at Steyne. Steyne looks away.
- Ilse holds the Conduit. She knows that tearing it will release the students and send the burden back up the chain to its root. She hesitates; she knows what it costs him. Hallam, quietly: "Do it, then. Someone has to carry her."
- She tears it. Light doesn't flash. Things go *still*. All across the College, Quiet students stir. In the infirmary, Hen sits up and laughs. Hallam's gloves split. He is grey to the elbow, then the throat. He sits down on the Gate steps and stills, his eyes still open, looking toward Tessary. **End:** the lantern on the keystone burning, and Ilse's hands shaking.

### Ch 31 — "The Board of Errata" *(Thaw 23–28)*
- **Scene 1:** Aftermath. Hen awake and laughing, crying, eating everything. Teo's fingertips pink again. Wil Ashdown speaks for the first time in two years. The College is in shock. Steyne makes a statement: "grave misconduct by a single Master." The fraying of the Zero Sheets is *not mentioned*. Ilse notes the omission.
- **Scene 2:** The tribunal before the Board of Errata (five Masters, Calder presiding). Lady Imogen Reyne attends as Court observer: cold, elegant, terrifying. The charge: forgery (true). Brask testifies, reinstated for the purpose. Kasimir testifies, and *does not lie*. He states that she forged, and that the law which would expel her would have kept Hallam in office, because Hallam's crime was invisible to every honest instrument but hers. He looks at his mother as he says it.
- **Scene 3:** Ilse speaks for herself. She confesses fully and **releases every forgery she ever made**, including the Tull hedge (the widow's orchard strip goes back to Saffery's land) and the forgeries done under Quince's mark. It is the costliest honest thing she has done. She'll write to Agnes Tull.
- **Scene 4:** The verdict. The Board finds her guilty of forgery committed *before* her membership, a matter for the Court except that she is now a Member. The Board's sentence: she is to be put to the use the College most needs. She remains, on probation, as the first student of **Contrary Charting** in a century, under Brask (reinstated). Lady Reyne leaves without speaking to her son. **End:** Ilse outside in the thin spring sun, legitimate and not proud of it. She is something better than proud.

### Ch 32 — "Until the Term Is Spent" *(Seedtime 3–10, 1204 H.)*
- **Scene 1:** The Crypt of Benchmarks. Ilse visits Hallam, a new benchmark, gloves gone and hands grey, standing among the others. She leaves a coin in his hand. She talks to him a little. Grief for the kindness that was real.
- **Scene 2:** Brask and Ilse walk the Margin at dawn. Brask: the Zero Sheets are failing faster now that the conduit is gone. Steyne has told the Assembly nothing. Nobody knows *why* the oldest bindings fail. Brask: "Next year we find out." She gives Ilse something: Brask's own old field-book from the Sallow.
- **Scene 3:** Damask invites Ilse into the **Unheld Stacks**. Its route has been re-surveyed with an open charter (Ilse's and Kasimir's), so the door now stays reachable, swaying. Dust, wonder, a library that has drifted for 98 years. In a Ninefold-era folio, a margin note in the unknown founders' script. Sorry, *undescribed*, shapes itself into something like a small hand and traces the script in spilled sand. Damask, trembling, half-reads with Ilse a phrase from the pattern: *"...until the term is spent..."* Cass, when told that night: "Rent's coming due."
- **Scene 4:** Morning Survey at First Bell, all four of the party. Hen's laugh. Teo's chain. Cass leaning with the grain. Kasimir's party passing; his hand on Ilse's a beat too long as he passes a chain. The Drift Wing breathing in the dawn under its open charter, held and moving. Final image and line, worth returning for: Ilse signs her true mark and understands that the world is not finished, and neither is she.
