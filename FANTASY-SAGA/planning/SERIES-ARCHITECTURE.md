# THE LONG SURVEY — SEVEN-BOOK MASTER ARCHITECTURE *(SPOILERS)*

**Series spine question:** *What did the Ninefold promise, and what is owed?*
**Thematic spine:** truth versus fixity; home as a boundary versus home as a relationship; the ethics of describing other beings.
**Emotional progression:** Discovery → Belonging (B1) → Learning → Mastery (B2–3) → Exploration (B4) → Revelation (B3–4) → Transformation (B5–7).
**Ultimate ending (fixed):** Ilse forges the release of the First Atlas, signing the Ninefold's long-dead marks: the most consequential forgery in history, and a lie that tells the truth. The Held Reach becomes an **Open Atlas**, co-attested by humans and Vey. Land moves within agreed ranges. Homes are held by *relationship* (people and Vey walking the bounds together) rather than by burden. Ilse loses her own fixity: she becomes partly unheld and cannot be pinned by any record, so she can wend. She lives at the Margin, teaching. The final line of Book Seven echoes the first line of Book One.

## Escalation logic
- **Knowledge:** from one corrupt man (B1), to a lost expedition's truth (B2), to the legal foundation of the world (B3), to the founders' betrayal (B4), to institutional tyranny (B5), to the bill coming due (B6), to a new compact (B7).
- **Scope:** from a College (B1), to the Margin and near Drift (B2), to Tessary (B3), to the deep Drift (B4), to the whole Reach (B5), to humans and Vey (B6), to the world (B7).
- **Institution:** corrupted (B1), then sponsoring (B2), under siege (B3), damaged (B4), split (B5), lost (B6), re-founded (B7).
- **Moral complexity:** a villain who is a frightened protector (B1); Vey grievance (B2–4); the heroes' own founders as the original wrongdoers (B4); the Stillists as reasonable people who are terribly wrong (B5); the heroes forced to give up what they love (B6).
