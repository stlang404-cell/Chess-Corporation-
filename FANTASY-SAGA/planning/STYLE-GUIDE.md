# PROSE STYLE GUIDE — *The Forger of Verity*

## Narrative stance
- **Close third person, past tense, Ilse Marrow only.** No other POV and no head-hopping. We know only what Ilse perceives, infers or remembers.
- Ilse's interior voice: wry, exact and observant. She sees *how a thing is made* (joinery, ink, stitches, lies). Her emotions show through the body and through what she chooses to notice, rarely through named feelings.
- Register: literary, accessible, adult. Sentences vary between short percussive ones and long accumulating ones. Humor is frequent and arises from character, never from winking at the reader.

## Technique
- **Show the craft.** Surveying scenes show instruments, numbers, method, error and correction. Use real units (links, chains, rods). Numbers make the magic feel true.
- **Scene over summary.** Summary is allowed only for transitions of time and travel, and only briefly.
- **Exposition:** never more than a short paragraph of world explanation at once. Fold lore into argument, jokes, instruction, objects and disagreements.
- **Wonder:** at least one image per chapter that a reader could paint. The Drift should be strange in specific ways (standing rain, folded air, a stair that turns the other way), never in generic ones ("eldritch," "otherworldly").
- **Dialogue:** follow `characters/VOICE-GUIDE.md`. Use "said" and action beats, and avoid adverb tags.
- **Chapter endings:** vary them. Some end on a turn, some on a quiet image, some mid-motion, some on a line of dialogue. **Never** end with a one-line ominous paragraph more than once every five chapters.
- **Subtext:** characters rarely say exactly what they feel. Brask especially.

## Banned or limited phrases (generated-fiction tics)
Do not use: "a testament to," "tapestry," "palpable," "the weight of [abstract noun]" (at most once in the book), "a dance of," "little did she know," "in that moment," "something shifted," "the air was thick with," "electric" (for tension), "a breath she didn't know she was holding," "eyes widened" (at most 3 in the book), "smirk" (at most 3), "padded" (walked), "orbs," "ministrations," "unshed tears," "jaw clenched" (at most 3), "the silence stretched," "a beat" (as a noun, at most 3), "it was as if," "she couldn't help but," "despite herself" (at most 2), "her heart hammered" (at most 2), "let out a breath," "searing," "visceral," "liminal," "Somewhere, a [noun] [verb]ed." Avoid the triplet-adjective habit and the "Not X. Not Y. Z." construction (at most once in the book).

## Consistency rules
- Spelling: British-flavored where natural (colour, grey, metres not used; use feet and chains). Use **"grey"** throughout.
- Terms are capitalized as in `continuity/CANON-LOCK.md` (Drift, Margin, Held Reach, the Meridian, Morning Survey, Drift Wing, Lantern Gate, Register).
- The ossel is "Sorry." Pronoun: "it" in Book One.
- Ilse's mark is a **wren's foot**.
- No profanity stronger than "bloody," "hell," "bastard," and occasional "shit" (Cass).
- Romance: restrained. No sex scenes. A held gaze or a touch carries weight.
- Violence: clear and brief, with consequence.

## Chapter format (Markdown)
```
# Chapter N
## Title

Text...

* * *

Text...
```
Scene breaks use a line containing only `* * *`. Use real em dashes (—). Use curly quotes (“ ” ‘ ’) consistently, with no straight quotes in prose.

## Length
Each chapter **3,200–3,900 words**. Book target: about 112,000 words over 32 chapters.
