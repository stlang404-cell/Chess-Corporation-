# How to Use This Companion

This guide collects what a careful reader, or a first-year student at the Meridian, might know about the world of *The Long Survey*. It reveals **nothing that isn’t established in Book One**, and it doesn’t reveal Book One’s mysteries or ending. Where in-world scholars disagree, the guide says so. Where the College says one thing and the Margin-folk say another, both are given.

![The Held Reach, with the Margin and the near Drift](ARTDIR/map-world.png)

# I. The Shape of the World

## The Held Reach
A great, roughly circular country about six hundred miles across. Its land stays where it is put. Its farms, rivers, roads and cities are stable because they are **held**: measured, recorded and attested by trained surveyors. At its heart is **Tessary**, the capital, built in rings around the **Zero Stone**, the first point ever held.

**Regions:**

- **The Fallowmarch** (east): hedge-and-barley farm country of smallholders and great estates. Market town: Hobbet.
- **Lisk** (south): a canal city on the river Ambry, famous for eel pie, chandlers, and a conviction that it makes the only real bread in the Reach.
- **The Salt Steps** (northwest): terraced fishing coast and salt pans.
- **The Ammonite Hills** (north): quarries and mines, home of masons who “feel true.”
- **Cape Verity** (far west): the granite headland where the Meridian stands, with the market town of **Verity Cross** two miles inland.

## The Margin
A band of country, five to thirty miles wide, that rings the Reach. Here the ground is only *half* held. Paths shift by a few yards a week, streams wander, and a heath may be a little larger on Tuesday than it was on Monday. The Margin is home to small villages such as **Brine End** and **Corrow**, and to the wagon-clans of the **Margin-folk**.

## The Drift
Everything beyond. The Drift is not chaos. Surveyors describe it as *slow weather made of land*. Hills migrate like dunes. Forests travel. Rivers change their minds, and sometimes rise into the air in loops called **skeins**. Rain may fall on a single hill for a week (**standing rain**), and pockets of warm air called **folds** linger in valleys. The horizon doesn’t sit still, which makes distances hard to judge.

Notable features known to the College:

- **The Tern Stack**, a sea-stack three leagues west of the Cape, crowned by a beacon cairn.
- **The Sallow**, a distant region of grey reeds, where an expedition was lost in 1183 H.

# II. Hold: How the World Stays Put

## The level
About one person in four hundred can *feel* how firmly matter is held: a faint pressure behind the ears that changes pitch near loose or firm ground. Surveyors call this **having the level**, and such people are **levelled**. Anyone can measure, but only the levelled can **attest**.

## Charters
A surveyor walks the land with chain, sightvane and levelglass, draws an exact record of it, and signs it with a personal **witness-mark**. The signed record is a **charter**, and it holds the land to what it says.

## The Six Laws (as taught at the Meridian)
1. **Witness.** A record binds only what its maker has personally measured. You can’t hold land by copying someone else’s map.
2. **Fidelity.** The more accurate and complete a record, the more strongly it holds.
3. **Precedence.** When two charters disagree, the more faithful one prevails. When they are equally faithful, the older prevails.
4. **Attestation.** Only a signed record binds. An unsigned map is only a picture.
5. **Burden.** A surveyor carries the strain of everything they have signed. If the land pulls against the record, the surveyor feels it.
6. **Latitude.** A charter can only fix land into a state it could plausibly be in. You can’t chart a mountain into a lake. You *might* chart a hedge a few yards over.

## Burden and the grey
Burden builds over a lifetime. Older surveyors suffer stiff joints, then grey patches of skin, then a hardening of the mind into certainty. The most heavily burdened become **benchmarks**: living, fixed and still. Several stand in the Meridian’s crypt, and students leave coins in their hands for luck.

## Drift-sickness
Time on unheld ground causes vertigo, nausea and the sense of being “un-pinned.” Trained wayfinders learn to bear it.

# III. The Disciplines of Cartology

| Discipline | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mensuration** | Precise measurement and the mathematics of error. The foundation of everything. |
| **Charting** | Binding land to records. |
| **Fieldwork / Wayfinding** | Surviving and navigating the Margin and the Drift by reading the “grain” of the ground. |
| **Soundings** | Measuring hidden depth: wells, walls, buried things. |
| **Concordance** | The law of conflicting maps, and the art of map duels. |
| **Palimpsest** | Reading older records hidden in reused sheets and in the land itself. |
| **Annotation** | Making small “true tools,” such as a rule that can’t warp. |
| **Bone-charting** | The surgeon-surveyor’s costly art of charting an injured body back into its true shape. |
| **Skycharting** | Briefly fixing the weather. Dangerous and mostly abandoned. |
| **Redaction** | The unmaking of records. Strictly controlled. |
| **Contrary Charting** | Binding land to an untrue but plausible state. In the Reach this is called **forgery**, and it is a crime. |
| **Tallying** | Charting *people* to a record. Outlawed since the Tally Tyranny of about 640 H. |
| **Wending** | A Margin-folk practice of *moving with* loose ground rather than fixing it. Most scholars consider it folk custom rather than a discipline. |

# IV. The Meridian

![The Meridian at Cape Verity](ARTDIR/map-academy.png)

The College of the Fixed Meridian was founded in 4 H. to keep the First Atlas, the founding map of the Reach. Over its Lantern Gate are carved the words **WE RECORD WHAT IS.**

## The two halves
- **The Held Side:** the Old Court, the Hall of Instruments, the Library of Record, the Mess, the chapel and crypt, the infirmary, and the **Register**, a vault seven levels deep holding the master charters of the Reach.
- **The Drift Wing:** a rambling extension built out across the Margin on a causeway. Because it stands on loose ground, *it moves*. Corridors lengthen, stairs turn, and rooms wander. It is divided into the **First, Second and Third Ranges**, with the closed **Far Range** beyond, home of the **Unheld Stacks**, a library annex whose door turns up in different corridors.

## Students
Students are called **links** in their first year, **chains** in their second, **furlongs** in their third, and **leagues** after that. They live and work in **survey parties** of four, which they choose themselves.

## Admission
Most students enter by examination in Tessary (the **Patent route**). An ancient rule also allows anyone with the level to take **the Walk**: on the autumn equinox, a candidate walks out into the Margin to a marked cairn three miles off and returns with a true survey of the route. Not all who walk return.

## The College calendar
- **Morning Survey** (daily, at First Bell): every party re-measures its stretch of the Drift Wing and signs the sheets, so the Wing stays where it is. A party that “loses its stair” is mocked for days.
- **Benchmarking** (first week): each newcomer cuts their witness-mark into the Old Wall and makes their first attestation, holding a single paving stone in place for a year.
- **The Ladder** (end of first term): a practical examination in a dark, unstable chamber.
- **The Concordance Moot** (early winter): first-years duel with maps over the position of the Moot-stone.
- **Errata Night** (midwinter): anyone may stand and confess an error, and the whole College answers, “*Recorded.*”
- **The Lantern Count** (eve of the spring equinox): every student walks the Drift Wing with a lantern, counting steps aloud.
- **The Long Measure** (spring equinox): the great survey race to the Tern Stack and back.

**The College toast:** “*To what is,*” answered by “*and what isn’t, yet.*”

# V. Creatures of the Margin and the Drift

![A sounder breaching](ARTDIR/illus-part1-sounder.png)

- **Sounders:** vast whale-like beings sixty to a hundred and twenty feet long that *swim through loose land*, breaching out of hillsides while turf and stones slide from their backs. Their songs are felt more than heard. Margin-folk law forbids killing them, and their shed **sounder-ivory** is prized.
- **Ossels:** small, soft, mutable creatures that take the shape of whatever is most confidently described aloud near them. Undescribed, they are mostly ears and eyes. Their eyes are always pale amber. They eat insects, seeds and, notoriously, ink. They are sold at the **Ossel Fair** in Verity Cross and are technically forbidden in the dormitories.
- **Wayhorns:** deer-like grazers whose antlers branch toward firm ground. Wayfinders read them like compasses.
- **Cairn-crows:** grey-necked crows that build tiny cairns at firm points. They make reliable messengers. The Meridian keeps a crow-loft.
- **Glasswrack:** a crystalline spiral plant that rings faintly in the wind.
- **Hollowings:** predatory *absences*. A hollowing looks like a patch of air or heath that won’t come into focus. Where one passes, ground loosens and memory slips. The Margin-folk drive them off with song.
- **Skein-eels and fold-moths:** small creatures of the skeins and the warm folds.

# VI. Peoples and Powers

- **The Concordance Court** at Tessary: the high court of land. It licenses surveyors and prosecutes forgery.
- **The Reach Assembly:** an elected chamber of parish delegates.
- **Collegiate privilege:** members of the Meridian are tried for charting offenses by the College’s own **Board of Errata**, not by the Court. It is an ancient right, and politically resented.
- **The Margin-folk**, who call themselves **the Tenting**: wagon-clans of the Margin named for their founding wagons (Tenter, Wainwright, Coachey, Hollin). They keep **road-songs**, sung routes for a country that moves, and sayings such as “*don’t sign what you can’t carry.*” They call Reach people **paper-walls**.
- **The Ninefold:** the nine surveyors who made the First Atlas and ended **the Wandering**, the age when settlements were lost to moving ground. They are honored in chapels across the Reach.

**Years** are counted from the Holding (H.). **Months:** Thaw, Seedtime, Blossom, Greening, Highsun, Haysel, Reaping, Gleaning, Fogmonth, Frost, Deepwinter, Candlemonth.

# VIII. Glossary

**Attest:** to sign a record with intent, binding the land to it. **Benchmark:** a fixed reference mark, and also a surveyor who has stilled. **Blind ink:** a forger’s ink that dries invisible. **Burden:** the strain a surveyor carries from their charters. **Chain:** the surveyor’s measuring chain (66 feet), and also a second-year student. **Charter:** an attested record. **Drift-fatigue:** a listlessness that the College says sometimes troubles students. **First Bell:** the bell an hour before dawn. **Folio:** a linen-rag survey sheet. **Grain:** the lean of loose ground, readable by wayfinders. **Latitude:** the range of states land could plausibly be in. **Levelglass:** a spirit level with a drop of quicksilver that responds to hold. **Link:** 7.92 inches, and also a first-year student. **Paper-wall:** a Margin-folk term for Reach people. **Register:** the Meridian’s vault of master charters. **Sightvane:** a sighting rule for taking angles. **Witness-mark:** a surveyor’s personal sigil.
