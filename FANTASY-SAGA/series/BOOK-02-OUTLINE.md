# BOOK TWO — *THE SOUNDING SEA*
*The Long Survey, Book Two. Spoilers for Book One.*

## Premise
Ilse's second year (she is now a "chain"). The sounders, the vast land-whales, are migrating toward the Held Reach in unprecedented numbers. Their songs now reach Cape Verity at night, rattling the Drift Wing's windows. With the Zero Sheets failing faster after Hallam's fall, the Meridian sponsors the first expedition to the Sallow in twenty years. Brask leads it, and her student party goes with her. Their task: follow the sounders to their singing-grounds and find out what draws them, and whether anything of the 1183 expedition survives.

## Themes
Grief and listening. Hearing what the world is saying rather than telling it what to be. Second chances for a mentor's failure.

## Major acts
1. **The Summons (Seedtime–Highsun 1204).** Ilse's probationary year in Contrary Charting under Brask. Brask's curriculum is brutal and strange: how to lie well enough that the lie becomes true, and how to know when not to. The Court sends observers. Lady Reyne lets it be known that she considers the open charter "the most dangerous document since the Tally Rolls." Tessary has its first public void: a street in the Copper Ward unravels during a festival. Steyne, weakened, has to approve the Sallow expedition when the Assembly demands answers. The party is Brask, Ilse, Teo, Cass, Hen (recovered, insisting on going), Kasimir, Orla Devane, and Oskar Fell, the half-mad Walker found in the Margin, who claims he followed "a voice like a girl's" and may know where Yara went.
2. **The Deep Drift (Haysel–Gleaning).** A long journey. Standing-rain valleys, the skein-forest where rivers loop overhead, and the first Ninefold cairn (impossibly still after 1,200 years). Expedition life: rationing, rivalries, a broken sightvane, Kasimir learning to wend badly, Teo sounding the ground like a doctor with a stethoscope. Hollowings follow them, and Cass learns that singing only works when she *means* it. Ilse and Kasimir become lovers-in-waiting. Brask grows lighter the farther they go.
3. **The Singing-Grounds (Fogmonth).** In the Sallow, the sounders gather in a "sea" of loose land, and the ground there is liquid. They are not migrating *toward* the Reach. They are migrating toward the *softening*, the way whales follow warm currents. The first true **Vey contact**: a figure that appears to each of them differently (to Ilse it is her father; to Kasimir, a statue that breathes; to Cass, her grandmother as a girl). It communicates by moving the ground around their camp into patterns. Ilse, using what she learned from Sorry's strange shapes, recognizes the patterns as a *script*.
4. **Ansel Corrow (Frost).** They find **Ansel Corrow**, one of the four lost in 1183, alive. After twenty years in the Sallow he is partly unheld: his face shifts, he forgets his name, and he is happy. The other three (Mira Tey, Jory Paske, Benedek Vass) chose to "go on" with the Vey, and Ansel stayed near the edge "to wait for Odile." Brask's grief and joy: she was right, and it cost her twenty years. Ansel tells them that the Vey call the Reach *"the Borrowed Country."*
5. **Return (Deepwinter–Thaw 1205).** Ansel comes back with them, though the Reach makes him ill. On the return, a hollowing nest. The party discovers that hollowings *avoid Ansel*. Yara Penhallow is found living with Margin-folk on the far side of the Margin, having wended her way there. At the Meridian, they find the Court has used their absence to petition the Assembly against collegiate privilege.

## Central conflict
Brask's expedition against the hostile Drift, the expedition's internal tensions (Orla's recklessness, Oskar's unreliability), and the Court, which wants the expedition to fail.

## Character arcs
- **Ilse:** from a talented probationer to a field leader. She has to make a lethal call (to abandon the expedition's supply sledge to save Oskar) and live with it.
- **Brask:** resolves the grief of the Sallow, and sees vindication arrive too late to restore her name.
- **Kasimir:** learns that his perfect charters can't survive the Drift. Humbled, he asks Ilse to teach him range-charting.
- **Teo:** becomes the expedition's essential ear. He finds he's braver on the ground than he is in the air.
- **Cass:** her songs work in the Sallow as they never have. She begins to suspect the Tenting know more than they say.
- **Hen:** proves to herself she isn't fragile.

## Relationships
Ilse and Kasimir's first kiss, in the Sallow, under sounder-song. They decide not to tell anyone and everyone knows. Cass and Orla flirt and quarrel. Teo and Hen become engaged. Brask and Ansel: old love, transformed.

## Discoveries
The Vey are people. The sounders follow the softening. The Vey call the Reach "the Borrowed Country." The Vey communicate in a script of movement, the same script as the Ninefold marginalia. Hollowings avoid the partly unheld.

## Mystery progression
The *why* of the failing Zero Sheets is now clearly tied to the Vey ("borrowed"). Nobody yet knows the terms of the borrowing.

## Climax
A sounder stampede through the singing-grounds as a Court-sponsored rival party, led by Lisbet Arnaud on Lady Reyne's orders, tries to *chart* the singing-grounds fixed. That would trap and kill the sounders. Ilse's party opens the ground with range-charters, and the sounders pass. The Court party's leader is lost, wending, and found alive. The Vey figure leaves a gift: a stone that holds a pattern of movement.

## Resolution
A bittersweet return. Brask is publicly vindicated; the Board restores her full title. Ansel is too unheld to stay in the Reach and returns to the Margin. The Assembly debate over privilege is scheduled.

## Series consequences
The Court attacks the College directly (Book Three). The script stone and the Stacks fragment can now be cross-read. Sorry becomes visibly agitated near the script stone.
