# BOOK FOUR — *THE WENDING ROAD*
*The Long Survey, Book Four. Spoilers for Books One–Three.*

## Premise
Ilse's fourth year, though she is no longer at the College. A small expedition sets out across the deep Drift to Ordinal, the lost first city of the Ninefold, to find the original lease. The party: Brask, Ilse, Kasimir, Cass, Teo, Sorry, Ansel Corrow (as guide) and Nan Ottery Tenter, who insists, aged eighty, on coming because "the road-songs go there." It is the great journey book: the wildest landscapes of the series, and its darkest midpoint.

## Themes
Wandering as home. What it means to belong to a place that moves. The sins of founders.

## Major acts
1. **Learning to Wend (Thaw–Greening 1206).** They can't chart their way across the deep Drift, because its latitude is too vast. They have to *wend*: move with the ground, as the Tenting do. Nan teaches road-singing as navigation. Kasimir is terrible at it. Ilse finds it natural, which unnerves her. Sorry chooses its own shape for the first time: a long-legged grey wader with a crest, the shape it took at the Ossel Fair once when no one was looking.
2. **The Lost Wing (Highsun).** On the way, they find the **Lost Wing of 902 H.**, the Meridian's outermost wing that drifted away three centuries ago. It has been sailing the Drift ever since, held by the descendants of the eleven students, who have become a strange, scholarly, entirely mobile society: the **Wingfolk**. They keep Morning Survey in a form that has evolved into liturgy. A wonderful, funny, melancholy interlude, and a mirror of what the Meridian might become.
3. **The Ninth Surveyor (Haysel–Reaping).** Cass discovers from the Wingfolk's archive that the Tenting descend from **Ysolde Tenter**, the ninth of the Ninefold, who refused to sign the lease's final clause and walked away into the Margin with her people, singing routes instead of charting them. The Tenting have kept the founders' conscience for 1,200 years without knowing it.
4. **The Fall of the Road (Gleaning).** Crossing a vast slumping escarpment, the expedition is caught in a Drift-quake. Brask attests the road beneath them with every ounce of her remaining capacity. She holds it long enough for everyone to cross, and **she stills completely**, standing on the road. She becomes a benchmark in the Drift, a fixed point that will stand for a thousand years. She is not dead, but she is gone. Her last words to Ilse: "Know which one you're making." It is the series' great loss.
5. **Ordinal (Fogmonth–Frost).** The first city: a ruin that still holds, half-swallowed by Drift, with hollowings everywhere. In its archive they find the original lease. It is not a fair contract. **The Ninefold never meant to return the land.** They wrote a secret second clause binding the Vey's own homelands, the places the Vey drew their selves from, into the Atlas, so that when the term ended the Vey would be too weak to reclaim it. The **hollowings are starved Vey**, those whose lands were fixed, slowly unmade over 1,200 years. The monsters are the victims.

## Central conflict
The expedition against the deep Drift. Ilse against her own faith in the founders, and in herself as their heir.

## Character arcs
- **Ilse:** loses her mentor and becomes a teacher. She has to lead the expedition home.
- **Kasimir and Ilse:** become lovers in the Lost Wing, in a quiet and tender chapter.
- **Cass:** discovers her lineage and is shaken. Her people were the ones who were right.
- **Teo:** holds the camp together after Brask's loss, the steady center.
- **Sorry:** chooses itself.
- **Nan Ottery:** sings at Brask's benchmark. It is her last journey.

## Climax
Ordinal's hollowings, which are the starved Vey, gather around the party. Ilse's choice is to fight them or feed them. She draws a range-charter that *releases* a single square league of Ordinal's fixed Vey homeland, unbinding it. The hollowings nearest it re-form into Vey. The first restitution. It costs Ilse her own capacity: her hands begin to go strange. They are not grey but *soft*, unheld.

## Resolution
They return with the lease, the truth about the founders, a dozen restored Vey as envoys, and without Brask. The Meridian mourns. Steyne, terrified by the truth, suppresses it, and the Stillists are born.

## Series consequences
The schism (Book Five). Ilse's partial unholding has begun.
