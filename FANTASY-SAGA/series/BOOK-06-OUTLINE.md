# BOOK SIX — *THE RENT OF AGES*
*The Long Survey, Book Six. Spoilers for Books One–Five.*

## Premise
The Drift advances. The Margin is swallowed in months, and Lisk's outer canals wander. The Held Reach shrinks by miles each season. The Vey have come for what they are owed, and the restored envoys from Ordinal say that the elder Vey will not negotiate with the heirs of cheats. Ilse, now the person in the Reach whom both humans and Vey call by name, must negotiate the debt of twelve centuries.

## Themes
Paying what's owed. Grief for what must be given up. Restitution versus revenge.

## Major acts
1. **The Advance (Thaw–Greening 1208).** Catastrophe and refugees. The Meridian, which reunited after the Stillist fall under Halloway as acting Provost, becomes a refuge. Ilse's partial unholding is worse: she sometimes forgets where her body ends. Sorry, now nearly adult, speaks the movement-script fluently and serves as interpreter.
2. **The Embassy (Highsun–Reaping).** Ilse, Sorry, Cass (for the Tenting), Kasimir (for the Court, now under his mother's sponsorship) and Teo travel to the **Gathering**, the place where the Vey meet: a landscape that is a parliament. The Vey demand the return of everything: the whole Reach, unwritten.
3. **Steyne's Last Stand (Gleaning).** Steyne and the last Stillists attempt to hold the western Margin by mass stilling. They make themselves into a wall of benchmarks. Lady Reyne leads Court surveyors to stop him, and **dies holding a district** while it evacuates. Kasimir's grief.
4. **The Price (Fogmonth–Frost).** Ilse negotiates. The Vey will accept a new compact on one condition: an act of restitution great enough to show that the heirs mean it. They ask for the Ninefold's own first holding. That is Cape Verity. They ask for **the Meridian itself**.
5. **The Drifting College (Deepwinter–Candlemonth).** The College debates. The vote is close. Ilse casts the deciding argument. The Meridian is unchartered, released, returned. On a winter dawn the whole College walks out through the Lantern Gate for the last time. Behind them the Old Court, the Drift Wing, the Register and the Crypt of Benchmarks, Hallam among them, all drift slowly away into the Drift, lights burning.

## Central conflict
Ilse against the Vey's justified anger and the humans' justified fear. Inside it, Ilse against her own desire to *keep*.

## Character arcs
- **Ilse:** gives up the home she earned, and learns that home can survive its walls.
- **Kasimir:** inherits his mother's seat and her responsibility, and bears burden for the evacuation.
- **Teo:** marries Hen in the refugee camp on the cape, with a wedding feast cooked for four hundred.
- **Cass:** her people are the only humans the Vey trust, which makes her the hinge of the treaty.
- **Sorry:** speaks for its people, and stands against its elders for Ilse.
- **Steyne:** fixed in his own wall, unrepentant.

## Climax
The release of the Meridian. Ilse unsigns the College's founding charter with her own hand while hundreds sing the Lantern Count backwards. It is the saddest and most beautiful scene of the series.

## Resolution
A truce. The Drift halts its advance at the new Margin. The Vey will hear a proposal for a new compact, the **Open Atlas**, but only if it is attested by the Ninefold, the parties to the old one. And the Ninefold have been dead for twelve hundred years.

## Series consequences
To release the First Atlas formally, every Ninefold mark must be unsigned. The marks can't be unsigned by anyone but their owners. They can only be *forged*.
