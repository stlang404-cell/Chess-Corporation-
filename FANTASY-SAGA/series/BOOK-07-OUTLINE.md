# BOOK SEVEN — *THE LAST ATLAS*
*The Long Survey, Book Seven. Spoilers for the entire series.*

## Premise
The world must be rewritten, or returned. The only person in the Reach who can forge nine dead founders' marks well enough to release the First Atlas is the forger of Verity. And the Open Atlas, a co-authored map in which humans and Vey share the land within agreed ranges, requires every human in the Held Reach to *unsign* their personal charters and walk their own bounds again, together with the Vey. It is the largest act of collective trust ever attempted.

## Themes
Home as relationship. The last lie that tells the truth. Endings as beginnings.

## Major acts
1. **The Nine Marks (Thaw–Greening 1209).** Ilse studies the Ninefold marks from the Zero Sheets and the lease. To forge a mark she must *understand its maker*, so she has to read the founders' lives, guilt and fear. Ysolde Tenter's mark is the last. Cass teaches it to her through a road-song.
2. **The Unsigning (Highsun–Reaping).** Across the Reach, the Open Atlas campaign: town by town, people unsign their parish charters and walk the bounds with Vey envoys. Some refuse. Lisk embraces it with festivals. Tessary is divided. The Fallowmarch: Ilse returns to Wick Fallow. Her mother, Hesta, Agnes Tull (very old), and Lord Saffery, who is now frightened and smaller. The Long Field is unsigned along with everything else. Ilse walks it with Saffery's grandson and a Vey envoy and sets its bounds as a range: shared, moving, held by use. She doesn't win the field back. She *unwins* it, and that is better.
3. **Brask (Gleaning).** On the way to the Zero, Ilse, Sorry and Kasimir travel to the benchmark on the Wending Road and release Brask. It is a range-charter of the human body, the first ever. Brask wakes for one day. They walk together one last time, at dawn, on the moving ground. Brask: "Knew which one you were making, then." She dies at sunset, smiling, unheld.
4. **The Last Atlas (Fogmonth).** At the Zero Stone, before the Assembly, the Tenting, the Vey Gathering, the Wingfolk and the scattered Meridian, Ilse forges the nine marks of release. The Zero Stone moves, and keeps moving. The First Atlas lets go. For one terrible hour the Reach is unheld and everything slides. Then people begin to walk their bounds. Millions of voices, human and Vey, in the largest Lantern Count in history, hold the world *open*. Ilse's own fixity goes with the Atlas, and she becomes unpinnable, partly Drift.
5. **After (Frost 1209–Thaw 1210).** The new world. Land moves within agreed ranges. Roads are sung and charted together. Hollowings re-form as Vey. The Meridian, found drifting in the Drift with its lights still burning, is re-founded by Teo as Provost, as a College of both holding and wending. Hallam is released from the Crypt, and Ilse forgives him. Sorry, adult, chooses a shape that is partly a description Ilse gave it years ago, a small grey cat with too-big ears, as a gift, and partly its own.

## Character endings
- **Ilse:** lives at the Margin, teaching range-charting. She is unpinnable and happy, with Kasimir.
- **Kasimir:** Chancellor of a reformed Concordance, which judges disputes between ranges, not points.
- **Teo:** Provost of the re-founded Meridian, with Hen and their three children.
- **Cass:** voice of the Tenting in the Open Atlas, with Orla.
- **Sorry:** ambassador of the Vey to the humans, and Ilse's oldest friend.
- **Brask:** her benchmark released. A road on the Wending Road is named for her.
- **Hallam:** released and forgiven. He teaches palimpsest quietly in the new Meridian, chastened.
- **Steyne:** remains a stilled wall in the western Margin, by his own choice.

## Final image and line
The last chapter is Ilse's first Morning Survey at the new Meridian. Students chain the moving ground at dawn, and nothing is fixed. The final line echoes the first line of Book One: *"The hedge moved at a quarter past two in the morning, and this time it wanted to."*
