# THE MERIDIAN — College of the Fixed Meridian at Cape Verity

## Founding purpose and history
- Founded in **4 H.** by the surviving members of the Ninefold, to train surveyors who would maintain the First Atlas. Its charter phrase, carved over the Lantern Gate: **WE RECORD WHAT IS.**
- The founding meridian line, the College's zero longitude, runs from the Zero Stone at Tessary (center of the Reach) to Cape Verity at the western rim. Brass studs set into the floor mark it through the Old Court. Students step over it for luck. Stepping *on* it is considered presumptuous.
- **Historical failures:** the **Tally Tyranny** (c. 640 H.), when a Provost-Chancellor used Tallying to bind dissidents to their parishes and the practice was outlawed; the **Lost Wing** (902 H.), when the outermost Drift Wing drifted away one stormy night with eleven students inside, never recovered (it is the reason for the Morning Survey); and the **Sallow Expedition** (1183 H.).

## Location and architecture
- **Cape Verity**: a granite headland on the western edge of the Held Reach. The sea lies to the north. The **Margin** lies to the south and west, a band of heath and salt-marsh where the ground is only half-certain. Beyond it lies the Drift.
- **The Old Court (the Held Side):** built on bedrock, inside the First Atlas. It holds the honey-colored stone quadrangle, the Hall of Instruments, the Refectory ("the Mess"), the Library of Record, the infirmary, faculty houses, the Bell of Hours, and the **Register**, a vault cut seven levels down into the cape.
- **The Lantern Gate:** a great arch between the Held Side and the Drift Wing, with a lantern on its keystone that is never allowed to go out. The ceremonial Equinox Charter is signed beneath it.
- **The Drift Wing:** built out onto the Margin and beyond, along a causeway. It is a rambling extension of dormitories, lecture halls, workshops, observatory domes and greenhouses. *Because it stands on low-hold ground, it moves.* Corridors lengthen. A stair may turn the other way. A room may wander a few yards and leave a doorway opening onto heath. It stays where it is only because students re-survey and re-attest it every dawn.
  - **First Range:** nearest the Gate, nearly stable. Lecture halls, the Fieldwork shed.
  - **Second Range:** the dormitories ("the Ranges"). Each party's rooms move a little every night.
  - **Third Range:** workshops and the Observatory of the Tern (a glass dome).
  - **The Far Range / the Unheld Stacks:** the outermost library annex. Its route has not been re-surveyed in 98 years, after a renovation dispute. Officially it is closed. Its door is sometimes found in different corridors. Restricted.
- **The Register:** the master charters of the Held Reach, organized by parish. Level Seven holds the **Zero Sheets**, the oldest First Atlas originals, which no student may enter. The **Antechamber** on Level One is where Morning Survey sheets are filed and where students sign them.
- **The Crypt of Benchmarks:** under the chapel. Former surveyors who stilled stand there, alive in a sense that no one fully understands, maintained by the infirmary. Students leave coins in their hands.

## Admissions
- **The Patent Route:** sponsored candidates sit written examinations in Tessary. Mostly the wealthy.
- **The Walk (open route):** any levelled person may present themselves at the Lantern Gate on the autumn equinox, walk out into the Margin to a marked cairn three miles off, and return with an attested survey of their route. A true survey earns admission. The Walk is old, legal and rarely used. Some walkers don't return. About one in three return with a passable survey.
- An intake is about **sixty students a year**. In Ilse's year: 48 Patent, 11 Walkers. Ilse, Teo and Cass are among the Walkers.

## Student demographics
- Ages 18–30. Most are 19–22. Ilse, at 23, is older than average. Teo is 24. Cass is 22. Kasimir is 21.
- Tuition: Walkers are admitted *free* under the ancient rule, which is resented by Patent families.
- Social strata: Tessary gentry, merchant families from Lisk, provincial scholarship students, and a handful of Margin-folk (Cass is the first in eleven years).

## Academic structure
- **Provost:** Aurelian Steyne. Polished, worried about the College's standing with the Court and the Assembly.
- **Masters** (heads of discipline): Master of the Register (Aurek Hallam); Master of Mensuration (Pim Halloway); Master of Concordance (Ines Calder); Master of Palimpsest (Archivist Ruel Damask); Master of Soundings (Gethin Moy); Master of Infirmary (Dr. Sabeth Orle); Field-Surveyor Emerita (Odile Brask; nominally of Fieldwork, a minor post).
- **Proctors:** fourth- and fifth-year students who keep order. Senior proctor: **Lisbet Arnaud**.
- **Years:** five. First-years are **"links."** Second-years are **"chains."** Third-years are **"furlongs."** Fourth- and fifth-years are **"leagues."**
- **Survey parties:** students form parties of four after the Walk. A party rooms together, does Morning Survey together and competes together. Ilse's party: **Ilse, Teo, Cass and Hen Solberg**. Kasimir's party: **Kasimir, Orla Devane, Piety Mercer, Absalom Grice**.

## Recurring traditions and ceremonies
- **Morning Survey:** at the First Bell (an hour before dawn), every party re-measures its assigned stretch of the Drift Wing. They walk with chain and levelglass, draft, and then file and sign the sheets in the Register Antechamber before breakfast. A party whose stretch drifts overnight is fined and mocked ("lost your stair, links?"). It is the most hated and most beloved ritual of the College.
- **Benchmarking:** in a newcomer's first week, each student cuts their witness-mark into the Old Wall under a brass benchmark arrow. They then make their first true attestation: of a single paving stone, which they must keep in place for a year.
- **The Ladder:** the first-term practical examination, in a sealed and unstable room of the Third Range. Students must survey it by instruments alone in total darkness.
- **Errata Night:** midwinter. The whole College gathers in the Mess. Anyone may stand and confess an error, and the College answers "Recorded." Confessions are not punished. Drink is involved.
- **The Concordance Moot:** late winter. First-years duel in pairs. Each charts the same object, a loose **Moot-stone** in the Old Court, to a different position, and the stone goes where the higher-fidelity map says.
- **The Lantern Count:** the eve of the spring equinox. Every student walks the Drift Wing with a lantern, counting steps aloud, so that the whole Wing is held by many voices.
- **The Long Measure:** the spring equinox competition. Parties of four survey a route from the Drift Wing out into the Margin and Drift to the **Tern Stack**, a sea-stack beacon three leagues west, and return. Judged on accuracy and time. The winning charter becomes the basis of the **Equinox Charter**, which every participant co-signs under the Lantern Gate to re-hold the Drift Wing for the year. Victory confers prestige and first choice of second-year placements.
- **Coins for the Benchmarks:** before examinations.
- **Unofficial:** "lost-stair" pranks; the **Ossel Fair** (Margin-folk sell ossels at the cape gate on market days; students keep them illicitly); the College toast "*To what is*," answered "*and what isn't, yet.*"

## Student organizations
- **The Chainmen's Club:** genteel, Patent-dominated, drinks in the Observatory.
- **The Errata Society:** an informal debating society that meets in the Mess kitchen after hours.
- **The Sounders' Choir:** Teo joins. They sing low.

## Institutional politics (light touch)
- The **Concordance Court** (Tessary) envies collegiate privilege. Its Chancellor, **Lady Imogen Reyne**, wants the Meridian's Register moved under Court authority.
- Provost Steyne's policy: avoid scandal at all costs. It leads him to cover up the Quiet as "Drift-fatigue."
- The College's relationship to the Margin-folk is paternalistic and suspicious.

## Hidden knowledge
- The Law of Transfer (ghosting).
- The Unheld Stacks contain Ninefold-era marginalia in an unknown script.
- The Register's Level Seven shows softening in the Zero Sheets (known to Hallam and Steyne).
- The Lost Wing of 902 H. is still out there in the Drift (Book Four).

## How the institution changes across the series
- **Book 1:** a corruption is exposed. Contrary Charting is recognized as a field of study.
- **Book 2:** the College sponsors the second Sallow expedition.
- **Book 3:** the Court attempts a takeover. Collegiate privilege is defended.
- **Book 4:** the Meridian's Drift Wing is damaged. Brask is lost.
- **Book 5:** schism. Stillists against Errants.
- **Book 6:** the Meridian is returned to the Drift. The College becomes a migrating school.
- **Book 7:** it is re-founded as a College of both holding and wending.
