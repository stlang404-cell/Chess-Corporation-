# CREATURE ECOLOGY

**General principle:** a creature's *hold* governs how fixed its form is. Held-Reach animals are ordinary. Margin animals vary slightly over generations. Drift fauna are mutable within a lifetime. Mutability is a spectrum, not magic in the casual sense.

## Ossels *(Book One major)*
- Small Drift mammals, cat- to fox-sized by default. They have very low hold, so their form follows **the most confident recent description spoken aloud near them.** If no one describes them, they drift into a soft, grey-brown, indeterminate shape that is mostly ears and eyes.
- **Rules:** (1) The description must be *spoken* and *confident*. Hesitant words don't take. (2) Conflicting confident descriptions cause "flicker," which distresses the animal. (3) Large shapes cost energy: an ossel can hold a large form only for minutes and then sleeps for a day. (4) Mass can't increase more than about fourfold. (5) They keep their eyes in every form: always the same pale amber.
- **Ecology:** they live in Margin heath and eat insects, seeds and, oddly, ink. They are social, curious and attracted to voices.
- **Human relations:** Margin-folk sell them at the Ossel Fair. Reach law classes them as vermin. Students keep them illicitly in the Ranges. There is an ethical debate (Cass raises it): "you're describing a living thing into whatever you want."
- **Sorry** (Ilse's ossel): acquired in Chapter 6. Unusually clever. Hates being described as a dog. Twice in Book One it takes a shape *no one described*: once a small heron-like bird (Ch 17), once a shape like a hand tracing letters (Ch 32). *Secret: Sorry is a juvenile Vey.* (Revealed Book Three.)

## Wayhorns
- Large deer-like grazers of the Margin and near Drift. Their antlers branch toward the nearest high-hold ground, so wayfinders read them like compasses. Herds migrate along the Margin in autumn.

## Sounders *(Book One wonder image)*
- Vast whale-like beings, 60–120 feet long, that *swim through low-hold land* as whales swim through water. They breach out of hillsides in slow arcs. Turf and stones slide from their backs, and the ground closes behind them like water. They sing very low, and the song is felt more than heard.
- They are harmless unless you are in the ground they're moving through. Recently they've been migrating **toward** the Held Reach, which is unprecedented. *(Secret: they follow the softening.)*
- They shed **sounder-ivory** plates. Margin-folk law forbids killing them.

## Cairn-crows
- Grey-necked corvids that build tiny stone cairns at points of high hold. Surveyors trust cairn-crow cairns. Crows carry messages because they always fly to fixed points. The Meridian keeps a crow-loft. **Mother Nettle** is the elderly crow-mistress (human).

## Glasswrack
- A crystalline Drift plant that grows in spirals. It rings faintly in wind and is valuable for lenses.

## Hollowings *(Book One threat)*
- Predatory *absences*. A hollowing looks like a place where things blur: a smudge in the air, a patch of heath that won't come into focus. It *eats hold*. Where it passes, ground loosens, stones slump and a person's sense of direction and memory slips. Touching one causes **un-pinning**: short-term memory loss, vertigo, and in the worst cases a "slackening" of the flesh.
- **Behavior:** drawn to *recently attested* ground, like flies to sugar. They are repelled by moving ground and by song (the Margin-folk sing at them).
- **College belief:** mindless Drift vermin.
- **Secret (Book Four):** hollowings are starved Vey whose own lands were fixed by the First Atlas.
- In Book One they appear more often near the Cape than in living memory, drawn by Hallam's heavy re-attestations.

## Standing-weed, skein-eels, fold-moths
Minor color fauna and flora. Skein-eels swim in the airborne river-loops called skeins. Fold-moths gather in warm air pockets.

## The Vey *(rumored; Book One hints only)*
- The intelligent people of the Drift. They have no fixed form. To a human observer they appear differently to each viewer. They communicate by *altering the land*, and their script is a notation of movement. The Margin-folk call them "the Landlords." The College considers them legend, apart from Brask's testimony from the Sallow Expedition, which was officially recorded as "hallucination under Drift-sickness."
- **Bond potential (later books):** Ilse and Sorry's relationship becomes the first mutual *co-description* between a human and a Vey. Each lets the other partly define them, and either may withdraw. That is a two-sided, consent-based relationship, deliberately unlike any rider-mount or master-familiar hierarchy.

## Ethical and conservation themes
- Sounder-ivory: shed only, never hunted.
- Ossel-keeping: the ethics of describing a creature into shape.
- Hollowings: are they vermin or victims?
- Bearing for hire: is it exploitation to pay poor levelled people to carry burden?
