# MAGIC SYSTEM SPECIFICATION — HOLD AND CARTOLOGY
*Canon-locked. Changes require an entry in DECISIONS.md.*

## 1. Origin (objective truth; most of this is hidden from characters in Book One)
The world's matter has a property called **hold**: its tendency to stay as it is. Left alone, the world is mostly *unheld*. Land shifts, migrates and reshapes slowly, like weather moving through stone. That natural state is called **the Drift**. Before history there was only Drift, and its native intelligent people, the **Vey**, lived *with* the motion. They have no fixed bodies and no fixed homes.

Some 1,203 years ago (Year 0 of the **Holding** calendar, written "H."), nine human surveyors, **the Ninefold**, ended the age humans call **the Wandering**. In it, human settlements were constantly lost to moving ground. The Ninefold measured and attested a region of land so thoroughly that it *stayed*. That record is **the First Atlas**. The land it holds is **the Held Reach**.

**Hidden truth (series spine):** the First Atlas held only because the Vey *consented*. The Ninefold negotiated a **lease** for 1,200 years. In exchange, the land would be returned, "unwritten," at term. The Ninefold buried the lease. The term expired in 1200 H., three years before Book One opens (1203 H.). Since then the oldest bindings have been weakening from the center outward. This is *not* known to anyone in Book One except, in fragments, the Margin-folk's idioms ("rent's coming due") and one marginal note in the Unheld Stacks.

## 2. Access and aptitude
- About **1 in 400** people can *attest*. They feel **the level**: a faint pressure behind the ears that changes with the hold of nearby matter. Surveyors call it "having the level," or "levelled."
- Aptitude varies in sensitivity (how fine a difference you feel) and in **capacity** (how much burden you can carry before stilling begins).
- Anyone can *measure*. Only levelled people can *attest*.
- Aptitude usually becomes apparent in adolescence. Many levelled people never train. They tend to make excellent carpenters, masons and sailors because they "feel true."

## 3. The Seven Laws of Hold (as taught at the Meridian)
1. **Law of Witness.** A record binds only what its maker has personally measured. Copying another's map gives no hold. *(This is why surveyors must walk the land. It drives the fieldwork and the expeditions.)*
2. **Law of Fidelity.** A record's hold is proportional to its accuracy and completeness. A sloppy map holds weakly. A map that is precise to the inch holds like iron.
3. **Law of Precedence.** Where two attested records conflict, the record of greater fidelity prevails. At equal fidelity, the *older* record prevails. *(Hence the First Atlas's supremacy, and the Concordance Court's legal authority to judge competing maps.)*
4. **Law of Attestation.** A record binds only when its maker sets their **witness-mark** on it with intent. A witness-mark is a personal sigil, unique and learned at the Meridian's Benchmarking. An unsigned map is only a picture.
5. **Law of Burden.** An attesting surveyor carries the strain of each record they have signed. If the land pulls against the record (Drift pressure, contrary maps, weather), the surveyor feels it. Burden accumulates over a lifetime.
6. **Law of Latitude.** A record can fix land only into a state *within its latitude*: the range of states the land could plausibly be in. Held land has narrow latitude (inches or yards). The Margin has moderate latitude (rods to furlongs). The deep Drift has enormous latitude. *You cannot chart a mountain into a lake.* You **can** chart a hedge two rods west, if the ground there could bear a hedge and the hedge has recently been less than certain.
7. **Law of Transfer** *(not taught; known to forgers and to the Register's oldest procedures).* A witness-mark can be **ghosted**: traced in a hidden under-layer beneath another person's mark. The burden of the record then flows to the ghosted signer, partly or wholly, without their knowledge. Forgers use small ghostings to pass a client's burden onto a dead person's mark. Done at scale it is monstrous.

## 4. Burden and stilling (costs and failure modes)
- **Early burden:** headaches, stiffness in the joints (often the writing hand first), insomnia, and a loss of interest in novelty.
- **Moderate burden ("going grey"):** patches of skin take on a slate or pewter cast and feel cool. Joints lock. The mind grows certain and rigid: sufferers find it physically difficult to change their opinions.
- **Severe burden ("stilling"):** the body slows toward immobility. The stilled are alive but fixed, like a held hill. They are called **benchmarks**, and there are several in the Meridian's crypt.
- **Shedding burden:** a surveyor may **release** a record by unsigning it, which requires the original sheet and the surveyor's own hand. The land loses that hold. Most senior surveyors cannot afford to release, because the land depends on them.
- **Drift-sickness:** this is different from burden. Exposure to unheld ground causes vertigo, nausea, time-slippage and the sense of being "un-pinned." Trained wayfinders tolerate it.
- **The Quiet** *(Book One phenomenon):* students carrying burden they never knowingly took on, through ghosting. They are listless and slow, their eyes go flat, and their hands go grey. Doctors misdiagnose it as "Drift-fatigue."

## 5. The craft (how surveying is done)
- **Instruments:** the chain (66 feet, in 100 links), the rod (16½ feet), the sightvane (an alidade-like sighting rule), the plane-table, the lodestone compass (unreliable in the Drift), the **levelglass** (a spirit level with a drop of quicksilver that responds to hold; a bubble that "swims" means low hold), plumb bobs, the sounding-line, and ink and paper (any paper; the Meridian uses linen-rag sheets called "folios").
- **Process:** measure, then draft, then verify, then attest. An attested sheet is called a **charter**.
- **Units:** inch, foot, link (7.92 inches), rod, chain, furlong (10 chains), league. The Meridian uses chains and links.
- **The Register:** the Meridian's vault of master charters. It includes copies of the First Atlas sheets, though the originals are in the Register's lowest level. The Register is the legal "spine" of the Held Reach.

## 7. Social, economic and political consequences
- **Property** is literal. A charter can move a boundary within latitude. The **Concordance Court** at Tessary adjudicates disputes. Rich landowners hire College-trained surveyors. Poor farmers cannot.
- **Forgery** (unlicensed contrary charting) is a felony: imprisonment, plus the "drawing of the mark" (a magistrate-surveyor cuts a line through the forger's witness-mark on every charter, releasing them all at once).
- **Collegiate privilege:** members of the Meridian (students included) are tried by the College's own **Board of Errata** for offenses involving charting, not by the Court. The privilege is ancient and politically resented. Ilse relies on it.
- **The Margin-folk** live in semi-held land, legally unowned because the Court will not recognize charters of land with wide latitude. They are poor, mobile, proud and distrustful.
- **Assessors** are tax-surveyors who are hated in the countryside.
- **Ethical dilemmas:** the ethics of moving boundaries, of charting another person's home, of tallying, of burden-sharing (rich surveyors pay poor levelled workers to co-sign and carry burden, a legal but ugly practice called **"bearing for hire"**), and of fixing the Drift at all.

## 8. Unknown phenomena (open questions in-world)
- Why ossels obey description.
- Why the hold of the Zero Stone has never varied by a hair, while some ancient First Atlas sheets show "softening."
- What the Margin-folk mean by "rent."
- Why the sounders (land-whales) migrate *toward* the Held Reach in recent years.
- The **Ninefold script**: the founders wrote marginalia in a script no scholar reads. *(It is the Vey's, adapted.)*

## 9. The "open map" (Book One climax innovation, consistent with the laws)
Ilse realizes in the climax that a record may attest **a range** rather than a point: "*this ridge lies between here and here, and moves.*" A record whose content *is* the land's latitude has perfect fidelity, because it is *true*. So under the Law of Precedence it beats a narrower, slightly false record, and under the Law of Fidelity it holds with almost no strain, so almost no burden flows anywhere. This is the conceptual seed of Wending and of the series' final "Open Atlas." Brask's "the map is a promise, not a cage" and the Margin-folk's songs that describe *routes that move* are its foreshadowing. It is a *deduction* from established laws, not a new power.
