# THE WORLD — The Held Reach, the Margin and the Drift

## Geography
- **The Held Reach:** a roughly circular region about **600 miles across**. Its center is the **Zero Stone** at **Tessary**, the capital. Within it lies ordinary, stable land: farms, rivers, cities. Its stability is uneven, highest near Tessary.
  - **Tessary** (center): capital, built in rings around the Zero Stone plaza. Seat of the Reach Assembly and the Concordance Court. Grey stone and copper roofs.
  - **The Fallowmarch** (east): flat farm country of hedges, barley and smallholdings. Ilse's home parish is **Wick Fallow**, near the market town of **Hobbet**. The landowning power there is **Lord Edric Saffery** of Saffery Hall.
  - **Lisk** (south): a river-port of canals and bridges on the **Ambry** river. Merchants, chandlers, food. Teo's home. Famous for eel pie and an arrogance about bread.
  - **The Salt Steps** (northwest): terraced coast, fishing villages and salt pans.
  - **The Ammonite Hills** (north): quarries, levelled masons, old mines.
  - **Cape Verity** (far west): the Meridian. Two miles inland lies the cape town **Verity Cross**, with its market, pubs and the Ossel Fair.
- **The Margin:** a band 5–30 miles wide around the whole Reach. Here the ground is half-held. Heath, salt-marsh, stony downs, wandering streams. Paths shift by a few yards a week. The settlements are Margin-folk camps and a few villages (**Corrow**, **Little Sallow**, **Thessaly Ford**, **Brine End**).
- **The Drift:** beyond the Margin. It is not chaos. It is *slow weather made of land.* Hills drift like dunes. Rivers wander and occasionally rise into the air in loops (the **skeins**). Forests migrate. Light is strange at long distances because the horizon doesn't sit still. Notable features known from expeditions:
  - **The Tern Stack:** a sea-stack three leagues west of the Cape, in the shallows where Drift meets sea. A beacon cairn sits on top of it, re-attested yearly. It is the target of the Long Measure.
  - **The Sallow:** a region about 30 leagues southwest where the Drift is "deep." Grey reeds grow there, the sounders sing, and the 1183 expedition was lost.
  - **The Ninefold cairns:** ancient stone markers that still hold in the deep Drift.
  - **Ordinal:** the legendary first city of the Ninefold. Believed to be a myth. *(Book Four.)*

## Climate
Temperate maritime in the west, with fogs, sea-winds and squally rain. Cold winters with occasional snow. The Drift has its own microclimates: "folds" of warm air, "standing rain" (rain that falls on one hill for a week).

## Calendar
- Years are counted **H.** (from the Holding). **Book One opens in 1203 H.**
- Twelve months are named for the seasons of survey. Characters mostly refer to seasons, the equinoxes and feast days. The months are: Thaw, Seedtime, Blossom, Greening, Highsun, Haysel, Reaping, Gleaning, Fogmonth, Frost, Deepwinter, Candlemonth.
- **Autumn Equinox:** the Walk. **Midwinter:** Errata Night. **Spring Equinox:** the Long Measure.

## Governments and law
- **The Reach Assembly:** an elected chamber of parish delegates, with property qualifications. Mostly dull and mostly in the background.
- **The Concordance Court:** the highest court of land. Chancellor **Lady Imogen Reyne**. The Court licenses surveyors (the **Patent of Charting**) and prosecutes forgery.
- **Parish magistrates** and **Assessors** handle local matters.
- **Collegiate privilege:** Meridian members are tried by the Board of Errata for charting offenses.

## Religion and philosophy
- The dominant civic philosophy is **Fixity**: the belief that order, home and truth are what holds. Chapels honor the **Ninefold** as saint-like founders. People "swear on the Atlas."
- The Margin-folk keep the **Road-songs**: oral songs that describe routes as *changing* ("the road to Corrow goes by the high stone when the moon is new and the low ford when it's old"). Their proverbs include "*rent's coming due*" (meaning: a reckoning approaches) and "*don't sign what you can't carry.*" They tell of the **Landlords**, the people who own the moving country. College scholars treat this as folklore.
- Some philosophers (the **Errant** school, 900s H., now unfashionable) argued that the Drift is the world's natural state and that holding is a kind of theft.

## Trade, economy and transport
- Charters are the backbone of property. Surveying is a lucrative profession for those licensed.
- Main trade routes are canals (Lisk), the **Great Meridian Road** from Tessary to the Cape, and coastal shipping.
- **Margin trade:** Margin-folk sell Drift goods such as glasswrack, sounder-ivory (shed, never hunted, by their law), ossels, wayhorn velvet and salt.
- **Technology:** early industrial in feel but not modern. Clocks, lenses, printing presses, spirit levels, canal locks, gaslight in Tessary (oil lamps elsewhere), steam pumps in the Ammonite mines. No firearms of note. Crossbows and blades for wardens.

## Cultures
- **Reach people:** varied. City manners in Tessary, plain speech in the Fallowmarch, theatrical friendliness in Lisk.
- **Margin-folk** (who call themselves **"the Tenting"**): wagon-clans named for their founding wagons (Tenter, Wainwright, Coachey, Hollin). They practice matrilineal property in goods, communal property in routes, and fierce hospitality. They distrust charters ("paper walls"). They practice *wending*, the craft of walking with shifting ground, which they consider common sense rather than magic. Cass is from the **Tenter** wagon.

## Architecture and art
- Reach buildings are squared, honey or grey stone, with **benchmark arrows** cut at corners to show the building is held.
- Margin wagons are painted with route-diagrams that change. Artists repaint them seasonally.
- The **Meridian style:** brass inlay lines, map-tile floors, ceilings painted with star-charts.

## Political factions (background in Book One)
- **The Court party (Reyne):** wants the Register under Court control.
- **The College:** wants its privilege.
- **The Margin question:** whether Margin-folk land can ever be chartered.
- **The Stillists** (emerging in Book 5): believe that total charting is the only answer to the fraying.

## Historical controversies
- Was the Tally Tyranny an aberration, or the logical end of Fixity?
- Did the Ninefold have a tenth member? (Some old carvings show ten figures, with one chiselled away. That is the Vey envoy. Book Three.)
- The 902 Lost Wing. Could it have been recovered?
