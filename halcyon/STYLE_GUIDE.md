# STYLE GUIDE — *What the Salt Keeps*

## Narrative conventions (locked)
- **POV:** close third, Hester Vail only. Past tense. We never enter another head.
- **Chapter heading in source:** `# Chapter N` then `## Title`, then an epigraph block, then the text.
- **Epigraph:** a markdown blockquote (`> ...`) of 1–4 lines, plus an attribution line `> — *Source*`. Keep it short, in-world and dryly informative. It may be funny.
- **Scene breaks:** a line containing only `* * *`.
- **Dialogue:** double quotes. "Said" by default. Action beats sparingly. No adverb-laden tags.
- **Numbers:** spell out in dialogue and narration up to one hundred, except dates ("6 Russet"), fathom numbers ("the Fifth Fathom"), casing numbers ("casing two hundred and eleven" in speech, "211" on a label), and money in figures when written ("6 sols") but words in speech.
- **Capitalisation:** the Halcyon; the White; the Hollow Sea; the Rim; the Ebb; the Fifth Fathom; the Hush; the Stay Choir / the Choir; a stay / the Stays (as a place); the Stair; the Line; the Lantern; the Lantern Hour; Lantern Night; the Assay; the Long Table; the Brinestacks; the Quiet Rooms; the Cold House; the Cistern; the Settle; the Roar; the Charter; Reader, Warden, Chancellor, Proctor (as titles); aspirant (lower case); grain, stillsalt, live brine, keeping, casing, the hold, the release, lag, arrears, quickening (lower case).
- **Spelling:** British-inflected spelling (colour, grey, centre, practise as a verb). Use "towards" or "toward" consistently: **toward**.
- **Time:** bells. The Halcyon rings twelve bells a day, from first bell at dawn to twelfth bell near midnight (about ninety minutes apart). "The half" or "the half bell" is midway between two bells. Weekdays use ordinary names (Monday … Sunday).

## The voice
- **Dry, exact, observant.** Hester notices weights, textures, prices, lies and the tare of things (the weight of the container versus its contents).
- Humour comes from understatement and from characters' collisions, never from narratorial winking.
- **Sensory anchor:** taste and weight first (Hester's sense), then cold, sound, light.
- **Wonder** is delivered by precision, not adjectives. Don't call things "breathtaking"; say exactly what they did.
- **Emotion** is shown through behaviour and the body. When Hester feels something large, she usually does something small: counts coins, straightens a spoon, tastes the air.
- **Metaphor discipline:** prefer metaphors from Hester's world (assay, scales, brine-boiling, customs ledgers, laundry, Lowside streets). Never use the same metaphor twice in a chapter. No more than one striking simile per paragraph.
- **Sentences:** vary them hard. Short declaratives for impact. Longer periodic sentences for wonder. Fragments are allowed and earned.

## Character voice crib
- **Hester:** short. Lowside turns sparingly ("I'll not," "wants doing," "ta"). Never says "I feel."
- **Teo:** warm, digressive, self-mocking; food; scripture with irony; long sentences that land softly.
- **Saskia:** clipped, ironic, numbers and money; Quellish bluntness; tender only with Pip.
- **Lucan:** formal, Latinate, ironic, precise; when he's sincere, his sentences get shorter.
- **Sorle:** baroque periods with a sting at the end; calls her "Miss Vail"; insults as affection.
- **Orsolya:** Lowside warmth; "chick"; practical; the steel shows in plain short sentences.
- **Aske:** delighted, fast, digressive; "Oh, that's *wrong*."
- **Aldous:** monosyllables, proverbs, then a sudden long true sentence.
- **Agathe:** gentle, Tider phrases ("tide willing"), practical.
- **Gage:** procedural, clipped, soldier's diction.
- **Crisp:** terse, technical.
- **Bassano:** florid, gossipy, kind.
- **Callow:** extremely slow, few words, often with ellipses of time ("He took the length of a held breath to answer").

## Banned / restricted phrases (generic generated-fiction tics)
Never use:
- "a testament to"
- "the weight of [abstract]" (literal weight is fine and encouraged)
- "something shifted" / "the air shifted"
- "let out a breath she didn't know she'd been holding" (doubly banned in a world where holding is magic)
- "a dance of", "tapestry", "symphony of"
- "palpable"
- "electric" (for tension)
- "her heart hammered/pounded" (max once in the whole book)
- "eyes widened" (max 3 in the book)
- "a beat of silence" / "a heartbeat later"
- "the silence stretched"
- "little did she know"
- "in that moment"
- "it was as if time stood still" (ironic in this book; banned)
- "she couldn't help but"
- "ministrations"
- "the ghost of a smile"
- "barely above a whisper"
- "a mixture of X and Y"
- "deafening silence"
- "orbs"
- "unspoken understanding"
- "Somewhere, a dog barked."
- "Her breath hitched."
- "a wave of [emotion] washed over"

Restricted:
- "suddenly" (rare)
- "very" (rare)
- "began to" / "started to" (cut where possible)
- rhetorical-question strings in narration (max 2 in a row)
- one-line paragraph chapter endings (allowed at most every third chapter)

## Exposition rules
- No lore paragraph longer than about 120 words without a character doing or wanting something.
- Teach magic through use, mistakes and argument. Lectures are allowed (Sorle, Crisp) if they are *dramatic*, with someone humiliated, defied or surprised.
- Do not repeat a fact the reader has already been given, unless a character is *using* it.

## Chapter endings
Vary them: an image; a line of dialogue; an action; a quiet thought; a turn. **Do not** end every chapter on a cliffhanger. Do not end chapters with summary-sentiment ("And she knew nothing would ever be the same").

## Content
Adult: grief, death, class, money, sex alluded to but not depicted, swearing in moderation (Lowside "tide take it," "salt and sorrow," real swearing sparingly: "bloody," "hell," occasional "shit").
