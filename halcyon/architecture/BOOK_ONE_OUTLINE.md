# BOOK ONE — WHAT THE SALT KEEPS — Scene-Level Architecture

**Viewpoint:** close third person, Hester Vail only, past tense. Never leave Hester's head. Never withhold from the reader what Hester consciously knows. Where Hester avoids a conclusion, show the avoidance.
**Chapter format:** number + title. A short in-world **epigraph** (1–4 lines) under each title, cited to a fictional source. Scene breaks are marked by a centred ornament (`* * *` in source).
**Target:** 26 chapters, about 3,700–4,400 words each, ≈ 100,000–110,000 words.
**Time span:** 1 Russet – 30 Rime, 903 Y.E. (four months: Russet, Sere, Cinder, Rime).

---

## Synopsis (complete)

**Act I.**
- Hester Vail, 33, a customs assayer, visits her brother Tib, who has been kept in salt at the Cold House charity brinery for eleven years. His casing is weeping, and the beads taste wrong: quick.
- At the customs house she impounds a cargo labelled Shelf grain that is in fact quickened Hush grain, which is felony salt. In it she hears a sound she cannot name.
- **Reader Corvin Aske**, the Halcyon's trade inspector, arrives. He recognises her extraordinary sense and offers her his Reader's Privilege: a fee-free place.
- Her supervisor makes the cargo vanish and suspends her. Her father tells her to let Tib go. She accepts.
- At the Halcyon she enters a world of wonder and finds friends and rivals:
  - **Teo**, an ex-Tider;
  - **Saskia**, a widowed glazier;
  - **Lucan Ferrers**, the Lord Exchequer's son.
- Aske promises to show her what he has found. The next morning he is found kept in a casing of Hush grain in his workroom, ruled a self-experiment, and legally dead.
- Without a sponsor, Hester must find a new Bench by the Midwinter Assay or be sent down.

**Act II.**
- Hester investigates.
- Aske's surviving note reads: *"Cold House 14 weeping / Harl St 0 / Varenne 0 — a draw? Still runs to quick. Cistern? Ask O."*
- Warden **Orsolya Thane**, Lowside-born like Hester, takes her into the **Stay Choir**, which holds the deep pressure-pockets called quickenings. There Hester hears the sea.
- With Teo she finds that Aske requested the founders' Cistern maps. On Lantern Night she bonds with Saskia, catches Lucan at the Cold House and watches a fourth sleeper begin to fail.
- On a field survey a sink opens. Hester keeps Lucan mid-fall, returns him and lags.
- **Reader Sorle**, the disgraced theorist, suspects Aske did not die by accident. At the midpoint Hester, Teo and Lucan descend into the Cistern beneath the Cold House and find new brass **draw-rods** in the founders' brine-lines, pulling the sleepers' stillness east toward the Halcyon.
- The Chief Proctor "loses" the rod Hester brings him. Sorle sponsors her.
- Lucan finds a contract between his father's firm and "W.L.F." for quickened grain.
- Saskia confesses she carried the boxes for money.
- At the Silent Supper a quickening breaks in the Fourth Fathom. During the stay Hester feels stillness arriving from the east through the stay-lines: *the stays are fed by the sleepers.*
- Bett Linnow, kept twenty years, comes back and dies. Hester's father threatens to sign Tib's release himself. Hester confesses to Teo that Tib asked her to let him sleep, and she didn't.
- In the archive she and the under-archivist Callow find Aske's hidden notebook: he confronted "O." The spiral grain of Aske's casing is Orsolya's hand. And Hush grain *does* bloom grey around the dead, so Aske's clear casing means he is alive.
- Hester confronts Orsolya alone. Orsolya confesses everything:
  - the deep is failing;
  - the Chancellor gave her nothing;
  - the sleepers are "already gone";
  - she kept Aske to stop him, intending to return him later.
- Orsolya offers to route the draw around Tib, and does so unasked.

**Act III.**
- Hester refuses the bargain. With Sorle, Teo, Saskia and Lucan she builds an alternative to the stay: **venting**, assembled from:
  - her father's brine-pan craft;
  - Saskia's glass annealing;
  - Sorle's arrears calculus;
  - Aske's notes.
- They plan for **Assay Night**, when the lower Fathoms are emptiest.
- Hester passes her Assay with distinction.
- That night Lucan and Teo cut the draw-rods in the Cistern with Mother Agathe's help. Hester, Saskia and Sorle open the Hush with a key Saskia stole.
- Orsolya arrives as the quickening surges. Seeing the vent working, she chooses to hold the Fifth Fathom's stays while Hester does the thing she is worst at: she lets go.
- The sea rises up the Founders' Shaft and pours out onto the White under the winter stars.
- At dawn a lake steams on the salt. In it are living fish and weed, and a gull lifts from the water and flies.
- Orsolya is arrested; Pell, the Cold House director, too. Aske is returned by venting over six days and wakes mid-sentence.
- Hester tells Tib the truth and promises that when she releases him it will be for him, not for her.
- On the Ebbtide Vigil the Tiders walk out with candles to meet the tide, and this year there is water. At its edge Teo reads the new salt and hears one word in a human voice: *Aelith.*

---

## Act structure
- **Act I** (Ch. 1–6, ~24k): Ordinary world → call → threshold → inciting death.
- **Act IIA** (Ch. 7–13, ~28k): Investigation and the wonder of the Halcyon → midpoint discovery of the draw (Ch. 13).
- **Act IIB** (Ch. 14–20, ~28k): Complications, betrayals, the Silent Supper crisis → dark moment (Ch. 18) → revelation (Ch. 19–20).
- **Act III** (Ch. 21–26, ~24k): Plan → Assay → climax → aftermath → resolution.

## Chapter and scene outline

### Ch. 1 — The Cold House (1 Russet)
*Epigraph:* from the Cold House admissions board: "The living keep clear. Visiting hours: second bell to sixth. Do not touch the casings."
1. Opening. Hester walks through Lowside at dawn, with the Sere wind carrying salt dust, to the Ambry Charitable Brinery on Coldwater Street ("the Cold House"). Visiting a brother "who has been fourteen for eleven years."
2. The charity ward: 300 casings in rows, clear as glass, the sleepers inside like insects in amber but *alive*. Tib in casing 211: a pencil-curl in his hand; the faint flush of the bloom fever. She talks to him: news, a joke about Daisy, the landships.
3. The weeping: beads of brine on the casing, running in *lines*, all toward the floor at the casing's eastern foot. She touches one and tastes it. *Sweet-metallic, wet, quick: the taste of something leaving.*
4. Mother Agathe. A dozen casings in the charity ward weep. "Damp season," says the director, Mr. Pell. Agathe doesn't believe it. Agathe's line: "The living keep clear, chick, and the dead keep grey. Yours is clear. Hold on to that." Hester pays the 6-sol fee and her purse is thin. (Plant: Ebbtide Vigil is coming; Agathe is a Tider.)
5. Brief, controlled memory, not the full truth: the night of the keeping. Aldous drunk; Tib's fever; "he said something to me, and I signed." Hester refuses the rest of the memory.
6. Exit. Across Lowside's roofs, the Halcyon's Lantern burning on its dome out on the White.
*Purpose:* the hook; protagonist; world; personal stakes; first clue (directional weeping, quick taste); plants (clear/grey).
*Ends:* she goes to work.

### Ch. 2 — The Dry Harbour (1 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Customs Schedule of Ambry: "Grain shall be declared by grade. False declaration is fraud; declaration of Hush grain is impossible, since none may be sold."
1. The dry harbour: landships moored at quays above salt, runners chocked, sails furled; the old sea-wall with its barnacle ghosts. The Customs House assay room: scales, tasting spoons, brine basins. **Daisy Fenn** (warm, gossipy, five children). **Comptroller Mallory**.
2. The cargo: the freighter *Constant Wren*, chartered by **Ferrers & Lisk**, with 40 crates of "Shelf grain for curing, Exchequer-certified." Hester tastes a sample and knows at once that it's wrong. It is warm. One crystal holds a live bead of liquid water that trembles. Deep, deeper than anything legal: Hush grain, and quickened.
3. She reads it (a brief hold, tongue to crystal): a white roar, then within it *a sound she doesn't know*, a high falling cry, like a hinge, like a child. (It is a gull. She doesn't know; there are no gulls.)
4. She impounds the cargo. Mallory blusters: "Ferrers & Lisk; the Exchequer's own contractor; you'll release it." She refuses and writes the seizure in the ledger in ink.
5. Evening. **Reader Corvin Aske** arrives (the Halcyon's Inspector of Trade Grain, sent for under the Charter). He is myopic, quick, humming. He tastes and goes pale with delight and fear: "Oh, that's *wrong*. That's beautifully wrong."
6. He tests her blind with twelve samples from his case. She grades all twelve, dates three to the decade, and names one as "kept, then returned, then kept again." He is stunned.
7. The offer: his Reader's Privilege. She refuses (Tib's fee; her wage). He says, quietly: "Have you seen weeping, Miss Vail? In keepings?" She has. He says: "So have I. Come and find out why." He leaves his card.
*Clues:* quickened Hush grain in trade via Ferrers & Lisk; the gull sound; Aske knows of weeping.

### Ch. 3 — Spitting Pans (2–4 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Lowside brine-boilers' saying: "A pan that spits is a pan that's talking. Listen before you lid her."
1. 2 Russet: Hester arrives at work. The bond-shed is empty: the cargo is gone, and the ledger page has been cut out. Mallory suspends her without pay for "insubordination and false seizure." Daisy, furious, slips her a copy of the bill of lading: consignee *Ferrers & Lisk*; shipper's mark *"W.L.F. per E.D."* (Hester doesn't know what it means. Plant.)
2. 3 Russet: the Lowside Pans. Aldous Vail at the boiling pans in steam and the smell of the Weeps. Their relationship: terse. She asks for nothing. He asks about Tib; she tells him of the weeping; he says, "Let the lad go, Hes. Eleven years." A fight, held under control. Then his pan begins to spit. He doesn't lid it; he draws the fire and lets the pan "talk" down slowly, explaining in six words. (**Venting seed.**) "You lid a spitting pan, she'll blow the lid through the roof."
3. That night she counts money. Without wages she has two months of Tib's fee. The Halcyon pays a **Stay Choir stipend** to strong holds (she has heard). The privilege covers fees. Ambry has no work for a suspended assayer.
4. 4 Russet: she goes to Aske's Ambry lodging (above a bookbinder's) and accepts. He is delighted and talks too much. He shows her the Halcyon's Entry Assay format. She asks about the weeping; he goes careful. "Not here. When you're below the Line, I'll show you. There is something wrong in the deep, and I think it's reaching up." He mentions the Matriculation on 6 Russet.
5. She goes to the Cold House one more time to tell Tib. Agathe promises to write if anything changes.
*Clue:* "W.L.F. per E.D." (Warden of the Lower Fathoms, per Edwin Dunmore).

### Ch. 4 — Below the Line (6 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Halcyon Matriculation formula: "Step over, and be below the sea."
1. Dawn. The Causeway: the White pink at sunrise, the night-chime of the tiles, the kept-light posts going out. Hester walks with a carpetbag. **Plant: a figure coming *from* Ambry at dawn**: Warden Orsolya Thane in iron-grey, tired, cold-handed, with salt-mud on her boots. She greets Hester in Lowside voice ("You're Aske's privilege. Well done, chick"). Hester likes her instantly.
2. The Gatehouse: the brass Line in the floor. The Entry Assay (a formality for her; she passes with eerie ease, and the examining Reader, Crisp, notes it coldly). Stepping the Line.
3. The intake, about 110 aspirants. **Teo Anselm** (eating, chattering, ex-Tider). **Saskia Orme** (sharp, measuring Hester's darned blue). **Lucan Ferrers** (elegant, bored), who remarks that "Customs sends its sweepers now"; Hester replies in kind. Cell allocation: Hester with Teo in the Rookery (he asks, she agrees: "I snore." "I'll hum").
4. The descent tour with a Proctor-guide: the Stair and the chalk-names; the Benches; the Lantern Well; the Brinestacks (a glimpse of Callow, "the furniture"); the Third Fathom Cells; the Quiet Rooms (the kept rose, the kept lightning); the Fourth Fathom Choir hall; the sealed doors to the Fifth, and below them a hum Hester feels in her teeth.
5. The Lantern Hour and the **First Taste** at the Long Table: kept noon light pours down the Well into the dark hall, and five dishes of grain from five Fathoms. The fifth (Fifth Fathom grain) makes nearly everyone weep salt tears. Hester tastes it and recognises the taste of the customs cargo and of Tib's beads. Quick.
6. Night in the Rookery: Teo and Hester talk; his faith story in brief; her brother mentioned, not explained.
*Clues:* Orsolya on the Causeway from Ambry at dawn; Fifth-Fathom taste equals the weeping taste.

### Ch. 5 — First Principles (7–15 Russet)
*Epigraph:* The Halcyon Primer, Law the Fourth: "What is kept is owed."
1. Crisp's Keeping class at the Pans. The hold taught as posture. Hester's hold is crude, deep and square: she keeps a falling drop of brine. Then the *return*: she can't. The drop stays kept. Crisp: "You hold like a miser, Vail." (**Flaw planted.**) Lucan's hold is precise and shallow. Saskia's is fluid.
2. Sorle's Horology lecture in the Black Bench. He is acid, brilliant and cruel. He teaches the Law of Arrears with the arrears calculus on a slate and humiliates Lucan. He teaches *still runs to quick* with a demonstration: two keepings joined by a brine thread, where one weeps into the other. The beads run *in lines toward the quicker.* (**Clue: Hester recognises Tib's weeping pattern. Draw-weeping.**) Students mutter "Sorle's Folly." He says the sea is "not gone, only paused" as a provocation, and is laughed at.
3. Montage of days: Bassano's Lumen (light kept in jars); the Brinestacks; the aching cold; the thirst; Teo baking contraband saltcake. Saskia snipes at Hester ("charity case") and Hester snipes back ("rent collector").
4. 15 Russet: Hester meets Aske in his Cell 3-17. He shows her a crystal from a Fifth-Fathom stay that holds a live bead *beating* slowly, like a pulse. "The deep is quickening. They're staying it as fast as they can, and something is making the stays easier than they should be." He's agitated. "Come tomorrow night at the tenth bell. I'll have it all laid out. Bring your tongue." He hums, distracted, writing in his shorthand.
5. She walks back up the Stair and passes Sorle coming down. They exchange no words.
*Clue:* draw-weeping pattern (Ch. 1 ↔ Ch. 5); stays "easier than they should be."

### Ch. 6 — Kept (16–17 Russet)
*Epigraph:* The Stillborn Clause, Charter of the Grain, §41: "A person kept beyond hope of return shall be accounted dead in law."
1. 16 Russet, tenth bell. Hester at Cell 3-17. Locked; dark; no answer. She waits a half-hour in the cold corridor. She hears footsteps on the Stair below, someone *coming up from the Fourth/Fifth*, heavy, dragging something on a trolley (she assumes a porter with brine casks). She leaves, annoyed.
2. 17 Russet, dawn. Uproar on the Third Fathom. Aske was found by a porter at first bell: kept, standing, in a casing in his Cell, one arm reaching, mouth open mid-word.
3. Hester pushes through. **Chief Proctor Gage** presides. The verdict-in-advance: a self-keeping experiment (Aske was notorious for testing on himself). Hush grain was found in a basin. "Kept beyond return: Hush arrears would kill him. In law, dead."
4. Hester observes as an assayer:
   - The casing's grain is **wrong for a self-keeping**. Self-keepings grow outward from the worker's skin, and this one grew *inward* from outside, poured.
   - The grain shows a fine **spiral, turning widdershins**. She notes it and doesn't understand it.
   - The casing is **clear, not grey**. She remembers Agathe: "the living keep clear." Gage: "Hush grain keeps everything clear." She has no reason to doubt him.
   - She sneaks a tongue-touch on the casing's foot. The taste is Hush grain, the cargo's taste.
   - His notebooks are gone; only a loose sheet is left in the blotter, in shorthand.
   - She pockets the sheet. (A transgression: she withholds evidence.)
5. Gage questions her. She was at the door last night, and the porter saw her. Suspicion falls on her, and she shows a bristling innocence. Sorle is questioned too; he was on the Third Fathom at the tenth bell. Lucan is mentioned: he argued with Aske the day before.
6. Orsolya arrives, kind and grieving: "Corvin. Oh, you daft man." She puts a cold hand on Hester's shoulder. "You're without a Bench now, chick. Come and see me."
7. The Registrar: without a sponsor by the Midwinter Assay, she'll be sent down.
*Ends:* in the Rookery, Hester unfolds the sheet, which she can't read; Teo looks over her shoulder: "That's brine-script shorthand. I can read that."
*Clues:* inward-poured casing; widdershins spiral; clear casing; the heavy trolley on the Stair from below (Aske carried up).

### Ch. 7 — Ask O. (17–19 Russet)
*Epigraph:* a Tider hymn: "Ask, and the tide shall answer; ask not, and the tide shall still answer."
1. Teo deciphers: *"Cold House — 14 weeping. Harl St Brinery — 0. Varenne Private — 0. Charity only? A draw? Still runs to quick. Cistern?? Old lines. Ask O."*
2. Discussion: Hester decides she will find out. She tells Teo about Tib properly (the keeping, the fee; not the wish).
3. Who is O? Teo: the Order? Hester: Orme? (Saskia is Aske's courier for Bassano's light samples, a convenient suspicion.) Orsolya isn't raised; it doesn't occur to her.
4. Hester confronts Lucan about his argument with Aske, in the Brinestacks Reading Well. Lucan, cold: Aske accused his father's firm of buying deep grain. "My father buys what the Halcyon sells him. Ask *your* Halcyon." She mentions the *Constant Wren*. Lucan doesn't know of it. A flicker.
5. Aske's casing is moved to the Quiet Rooms. Hester watches the porters, and Orsolya directs them gently. Hester stays a minute by the casing.
6. Saskia appears to have been crying (Hester misreads it). Saskia snaps.
*Ends:* Hester lists suspects in her ledger like a customs schedule.

### Ch. 8 — The Stay Choir (20–23 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Stay Choir work-song, refrain: "Hold the line, hold the line, so the others can sleep."
1. The sponsor hunt. Crisp refuses honestly ("I'm full and you're unfinished"). Bassano is kind but full. Two Readers refuse for class reasons. Sorle refuses insultingly ("I don't take charity cases from the dead").
2. Orsolya's office in the Fifth-Fathom antechamber (the Warden's Gallery). Warmth, tea, Lowside talk; Orsolya's dead father, a delver crushed at Coldbank. She can't formally sponsor until her current aspirants assay out, but she offers a Choir place: "I need holds like yours." Stipend: 3 sols a week. Hester accepts (money for Tib).
   - **Plant:** Orsolya says, tired, "The Stays have been kind this season. Kinder than they've any right to be." (Unreadable to Hester at this point.)
   - The clerk **Dunmore**: bland and efficient.
3. First descent with the Choir. Sixty workers in grey wool; the slow singing to lock breath; descending into the Fifth Fathom's galleries; brass stay-lines; the warm wet salt of a quickening.
4. The stay. Hester holds with the Choir and her stillness flows into the line. In the hold she hears it, through her teeth: **the sea**. Waves. Wind. A roar with *shape*. She weeps salt and doesn't know why.
5. After: lag, cold, the thirst. **Ama Tessaly**, a Choir elder, wraps her in a blanket. Orsolya is proud.
*Clue:* "the stays have been kind"; the stay-lines run east (a passing detail: "the eastern trunk").

### Ch. 9 — The Brinestacks (24–27 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Brinestacks notice: "Readers are reminded that the tablets remember being touched."
1. Hester and Teo research in the Brinestacks. They look for the Cold House's records in the Exchequer's brinery returns: three charity sleepers "returned by casing failure" and died in the past ten months. Normal rate: one in three years.
2. "Cistern" in the catalogue. The founders'-era Cistern plans were requested on 2 Russet by C. Aske, and **before him, a year ago, by "W.L.F. (office)."** Hester doesn't decode W.L.F. She connects it to "W.L.F. per E.D." on the bill of lading. Something to find out. (She asks a clerk, who is out; the lead hangs.)
3. The plans: the Great Cistern beneath Coldwater Street, and a main line running east under the White *to the Halcyon*. Teo: "The Cold House is sitting on the old reservoir."
4. Teo reads an old Brinescript tablet (230 Y.E., pre-fire) and in the Roar beneath it hears a falling cry. Hester: "That. I heard that in the cargo." Teo, astonished, finds it in a hymnal woodcut caption: *"the gull, a bird of the sea, crying."* "Hester. There are no gulls."
5. Wynn Callow, "the furniture," speaks one sentence as they leave: "Corvin sat there too. On the first of the month." (Aske came to read the same.)
*Clues:* the W.L.F. requisition; the Cistern route; the gull.

### Ch. 10 — Lantern Night (30 Russet)
*Epigraph:* Ambry street-song: "Summer in a jar, summer in a jar, pour it on the rooftops where the cold folk are."
1. Afternoon: Hester, Teo and Saskia in Ambry. Saskia is warmer outside the Halcyon. They go to the Glassmarket: Saskia's mother Hennie and daughter Pip. Saskia's husband drowned in a sink. A turn in their relationship.
2. Saskia explains **annealing** while they watch the lantern-jars being loaded for the release: "Glass that cools fast keeps its stress inside and one day it just bursts. You cool it slow and the stress walks out." (**Venting seed #2.**)
3. The Cold House at dusk. Tib's casing weeps more. Agathe shows them a sleeper, **Bett Linnow**, whose casing is thinning badly. Agathe is frightened. Lucan Ferrers is in the ward, in an expensive coat, looking at the casings and the eastern floor drains. A confrontation in whispers. He admits he came because of the *Constant Wren*: he asked his father, who told him to drop it. He saw the Cold House's name on a Ferrers supply invoice, for brine delivered "for cistern maintenance." Grudging truce.
4. Lantern Night itself. At the ninth bell the Halcyon pours a summer's kept light over Ambry from jars on the rooftops and the Lantern. The city is lit gold; birdsong of the summer days is in it; the warmth on the face; people cry and laugh. Hester stands with Teo, Saskia, Pip on Saskia's hip, and Lucan a few feet off. Beauty.
5. Lucan, quietly, at the end: "I don't think it was an accident." Hester: "No."
*Clues:* annealing; the "cistern maintenance" invoice; Bett Linnow failing.

### Ch. 11 — The Sink (6 Sere)
*Epigraph:* Weathering field manual: "The White does not forgive. The White does not hate. The White is a floor with a hole in it."
1. Fathoming field practical: Orsolya leads twenty first-years by landship (the skiff *Gannet*) to a surface "bloom-site" 5 miles out: a quickening near the surface on the open White. Sailing the White: speed, wind, runners hissing, glare-goggles, the Pull. Hester has never sailed. Exhilaration.
2. The bloom-site: wet salt, warm, a smell of weed, and a faint sound of surf from underfoot.
3. **Plant:** Orsolya, chatting with Hester: "How's your brother? Still at the Cold House? Coldwater Street. They're good there." Hester never told her the street, or even the brinery. Hester thinks Aske must have told her.
4. The sink opens. Lucan falls. Hester throws her brine-flask across him as he drops and keeps him: a man-shaped casing hanging in the rim of the sink, one hand up. Then she has to return him before the casing's weight drags it down. She can't release. Orsolya talks her through it ("Don't push it out, chick. Open your hand"). She returns him; the arrears snap cracks two of his ribs; he lives.
5. Hester lags for **four minutes**. She comes to on the salt with the sky changed, the others around her, Teo holding her hand. Terror at lost time.
6. Back at the Halcyon, Sorle appears at the Rookery door. "You kept a man. Badly. Interesting. My study, Thursday."
*Clues:* Orsolya knows the Cold House street. The release flaw is shown.

### Ch. 12 — Sorle's Folly (8–9 Sere)
*Epigraph:* Minutes of the Conclave, 871 Y.E.: "Reader Sorle's hypothesis was found to be without foundation and an embarrassment to the Bench."
1. Sorle's study: books, slates, a kept moth on his desk, his dead wife's portrait. He interrogates her keeping of Lucan, then Aske.
2. Sorle admits he was invited to Aske's Cell on 16 Russet at the tenth-and-a-half bell (half an hour after her). He arrived and found the corridor empty, saw nothing, and left. He believes Aske was killed. "Corvin couldn't keep himself in a pickle jar. His hold was *radial*. A child could see that casing was poured."
3. The Held Moment Hypothesis, explained by Sorle as Hester's teacher: the sea is not gone but held, and grain is its stillness. Hester thinks it's mad; she can't dismiss the gull. Sorle on 871: "I was demolished by a young Fathomer with a gift for sums. Orsolya Thane. She was right about my maths and wrong about the world."
4. He won't sponsor her yet: "Bring me evidence. Not tastes. Evidence I can put on a slate."
5. Lucan in the Physic ward with cracked ribs. He thanks her badly; she accepts badly. A small honesty: he has never owed anyone anything. Begins.
*Ends:* Hester resolves to go into the Cistern.

### Ch. 13 — The Cistern (14 Sere) — MIDPOINT
*Epigraph:* Ambry Water Board, 400 Y.E.: "The founders' cistern is bricked and shall not be reopened."
1. Planning with Teo and Lucan. Lucan supplies Exchequer plans and money. Agathe lets them in through the Cold House cellar at night (Pell is away).
2. The descent: old brick, founders' brass, a vaulted reservoir (the Great Cistern) dry but for a channel of live brine. The smell is quick.
3. They find **the draw**: new brass rods with Halcyon Lattice-mason marks, sleeved in live brine, running from drains under the charity ward's eastern floor into the founders' main line *east*. Hester tastes the brine in the line: it is stiller than anything she has ever tasted. "This is them. This is Tib."
4. Teo reads the line: the Roar, and under it *children breathing.* The sleepers' moments, flowing.
5. Pell surprises them with a lamp and a cudgel, terrified. A struggle. Lucan's name stops him ("I am Lucan Ferrers. Put that down"). Pell blurts out "the Halcyon's maintenance," the "gentleman from the Warden's office," "ten sols a month." They escape with **one draw-rod** sawn free.
6. Outside at dawn: the realisation. The draw runs to the Halcyon. Someone inside is drawing stillness from the sleepers. "Warden's office": which Warden? There are four.
*Midpoint reversal:* not smuggling but a draw from inside.

### Ch. 14 — What Is Owed (15–20 Sere)
*Epigraph:* Sorle, *Lectures on Arrears*: "Every keeping is a lie we tell time, and time collects."
1. Hester takes the rod to Chief Proctor Gage, against Sorle's advice. He listens and is grave. He takes the rod and promises inquiry.
2. Two days later the rod is "lost." Gage warns her off: "There are matters of Halcyon infrastructure above both our heads." She suspects Gage and wonders whom he answers to. (Deputy Chancellor Vey is glimpsed leaving Gage's office.)
3. Hester goes to Sorle with the full account: the rod lost, Pell's words. Sorle believes her. **He sponsors her**: "I have nothing left to lose and you have even less. That's a Bench." He gives her his black cord. A key emotional turn.
4. Lessons with Sorle: the arrears calculus; quickenings as moments under pressure; still runs to quick. "Stays saturate. Pour enough still into a quickening and it quiets — and grows hungrier." Hester asks: "What if you let it out?" Sorle: "Then the moment *proceeds*." He refuses the thought as madness for now. (**Venting seed #3.**)
5. Hester writes to Agathe and gets a reply: Tib's weeping is worse, Bett is worse.

### Ch. 15 — Ferrers (22–28 Sere)
*Epigraph:* a Ferrers & Lisk contract clause: "The purchaser shall make no enquiry as to provenance."
1. Lucan has gone to his father's house in the Upper Ward and found the contract: Ferrers & Lisk buys "residual stay grain" from **W.L.F.** (the office of the Warden of the Lower Fathoms), signed by **E. Dunmore, clerk.** His father told him to stay out of it ("the Halcyon's dirty laundry keeps our lamps lit"). Lucan tells Hester; he is shaken.
2. Hester decodes W.L.F. at last: the Warden of the Lower Fathoms. Orsolya's office. She refuses the conclusion. *Dunmore.* The clerk. Orsolya can't know: she's Lowside; she's the Choir's mother. Hester reasons it's Dunmore, perhaps for Vey. (**Self-deception shown.**)
3. Orsolya's private lessons in the hold (she has offered to help Hester's release). Warmth, and Orsolya's story of Coldbank. **Plant:** Orsolya demonstrates keeping a candle flame and the casing shows a widdershins **spiral**. "Every worker leaves a hand, chick. Mine's a corkscrew. My mam said I came out twisting." Hester sees it. Something tugs; she doesn't let it surface.
4. Hester sounds Orsolya out about Dunmore. Orsolya, smooth: "Edwin handles the residue contracts; it's all above board, Conclave-approved." Hester is relieved and not quite relieved.
5. Choir shift: the stays still draw easier than they should.

### Ch. 16 — Annealing (2–4 Cinder)
*Epigraph:* a Quell glassmakers' proverb: "Glass remembers how it cooled."
1. Saskia is short of money. Hester, ledger-minded, notices her new boots and her late returns.
2. Saskia confesses after Hester confronts her in the Lumen kilns. Since summer she has been doing evening courier work for "the Warden's clerk": carrying sealed grain boxes from the Fifth-Fathom lift to the Ferrers landship at the dry harbour, 2 sols a run. She carried the *Constant Wren* boxes. "I didn't ask. I've a daughter. I don't do causes, Vail. I do rent."
3. A real fight: ugly, adult, both right.
4. Reconciliation (later, in the Rookery with Teo's saltcake). Saskia offers what she knows: the boxes came up from the Fifth; Dunmore was always the hand; she never saw the Warden. She also mentions that Dunmore keeps the Warden's **duplicate keys** in his desk, including the Hush key. (**Plant for the Act III key.**)
5. Hester tells Saskia everything. The group of four (Hester, Teo, Saskia, Lucan) forms.
*Clue:* "O." might be Orme? Hester rules it out now. Reader suspicion is redirected.

### Ch. 17 — The Silent Supper (9 Cinder)
*Epigraph:* Halcyon custom: "For one minute the whole Halcyon is still, and the Settled are named."
1. The Silent Supper: the names on the Stair re-chalked by candle. The Minute: six hundred people utterly still at the Long Table, and Hester can feel the whole Halcyon holding.
2. The alarm bell in the middle of the Minute. A quickening has broken in the Fourth Fathom's east gallery, *above* the Stays. Water is running out of salt.
3. The Choir goes down. Chaos and beauty: seawater (cold, smelling of weed) running across the gallery floor, archives in danger, the sound of surf in the walls.
4. The stay. Orsolya leads, magnificent. Hester holds in the Choir line. Mid-hold she feels **stillness arriving through the stay-line from the east**, not from the Choir: a cold, old, vast stillness. *She knows its taste. It is the taste of the line under the Cold House. It is the taste of Tib.* The stays are fed by the sleepers.
5. **Ama Tessaly** holds too long and Settles to death on the gallery floor, still sitting, still singing.
6. After: Orsolya kneels by Ama and weeps. Hester watches her, and *cannot* make herself think what she has to think. "The stays have been kind this season."
*Ends:* Hester goes up the Stair and chalks Ama's name.

### Ch. 18 — The Fourth Sleeper (16–18 Cinder) — DARK MOMENT
*Epigraph:* from a Brinery release form: "Signature of next of kin. Relationship. Date. The house accepts no responsibility for what returns."
1. Agathe sends for her. **Bett Linnow** is coming back: the casing is failing beyond recasting. Hester goes. Bett's mother Ruth, 79, holds her hand. Bett returns, gasping in the last instant of her twenty-year-old haemorrhage, and dies in the arrears-heat. Unbearable.
2. Tib's casing: thin at the eastern foot. Agathe: "Weeks, chick. Not months."
3. Hester goes to Aldous drunk-adjacent and tells him. He says that as next of kin he'll sign Tib's release himself, now, "before he goes like that lass." The worst fight of their lives. He says the thing: "You didn't keep him for him. You kept him for you." She leaves.
4. The Rookery. Teo. She tells him the truth: "He asked me to let him sleep. He knew what he meant. I signed." The confession: eleven years of it. Teo does not absolve her. He sits with her. "Then you've been keeping two people, Hes. Him and the girl who signed."
5. Low point: she considers stealing Hush grain to recast Tib herself. She doesn't. Barely.

### Ch. 19 — The Notebook (25–28 Cinder)
*Epigraph:* Wynn Callow's only published sentence, 851 Y.E.: "The archive is a keeping like any other, and like any other it is owed."
1. Callow's hint returns to her: "Corvin sat there too." Hester sits at Callow's desk for an afternoon. Callow speaks in his slowness: Aske filed something "where the founders sleep." In the Founders' Gallery, behind a catalogue drawer of 230 Y.E. fire-salvage, they find **Aske's notebooks**.
2. Teo deciphers through the night. Aske traced it all: the quickened grain in trade; the Cold House draw; the founders' Cistern line to the Old Tap in the Fifth; W.L.F.; Dunmore; Pell. His last entry (15 Russet): *"She is right that it will flood. She is wrong that it must be held. The pressure wants a road, not a lid. Vent? Need Sorle's arrears for the shaft. I'll ask O. tomorrow, before I tell Gage. I owe her that. — If anything — tell Hester to taste it."*
3. Hester returns alone to the Quiet Rooms and tastes Aske's casing again, reading it now with full knowledge. The hand is a **widdershins spiral**. Orsolya's corkscrew.
4. And the clearness. She asks Callow. He takes a long time: "Hush grain keeps clear quickly. Then it blooms like any other. A month. Corvin's been there two." Aske's casing is still clear. **Aske is alive.** Kept, not killed.
5. Hester sits in the Quiet Room with the truth. Orsolya. And Aske alive.

### Ch. 20 — The Warden (2 Rime)
*Epigraph:* Orsolya Thane, Stay Choir address, 899 Y.E.: "I have buried eleven of mine. I'll not bury more."
1. Against everyone's advice in advance (she tells no one; her flaw), Hester goes to Orsolya's office at night.
2. The confrontation: the spiral; the notebook; Dunmore; the draw.
3. Orsolya confesses.
   - Quickenings have tripled since 896. Eleven choristers buried in five years; Ama makes twelve.
   - The Chancellor and Vey told her to manage quietly and gave her nothing: "no inquiry, no money, no noise."
   - If the Hush breaches, the Halcyon floods, and perhaps the crust between here and Ambry with it.
   - She found the founders' lines. "They built the Cistern for this, chick. The founders. To feed the stays. It was always meant." (A saga hint.)
   - The sleepers: "Their families are bled white for keeping. They're gone, Hester. We keep them because we can't bear the bill of letting go. I'm letting them go *usefully*."
   - Aske: he tried to shut the Old Tap and the line would have backflowed into the stays. "I kept him, God help me, I didn't kill him. I kept him till it's over. I'll vent him myself when we're through."
4. The offer: "Your lad's line — I can route round him. He'll hold till spring and past. Nobody need know." Hester says nothing.
5. Orsolya lets her go: "Think about it. You're Lowside. You know what it is to choose who drowns."
6. Next day, a note from Agathe: Tib's casing has stopped weeping. *Orsolya did it without being asked.* Hester's complicity.
*Ends:* Hester sits on the Stair below Ama's chalked name.

### Ch. 21 — Still Runs to Quick (3–19 Rime)
*Epigraph:* Halcyon Primer, Law the Fifth: "Still runs to quick."
1. Hester tells the four and Sorle everything, including the offer and the fact that Tib is now safe *because of* Orsolya. Debate:
   - Go to the Chancellor? (Sorle: the Seal will bury it; Gage already lost the rod.)
   - Go to the Assize? (The Halcyon will close ranks, the draw will continue, and the Hush will still be failing.)
   - Cut the draw? (The Hush breaches and kills thousands.)
2. Hester's turn: refusing the binary. "The pressure wants a road, not a lid." Aske's notes, her father's pans, Saskia's annealing, Sorle's arrears.
3. Sorle works the calculus over four days on every slate in his study. Venting is possible *if* the pressure has a road to somewhere harmless, and if the release is paced (Saskia: "annealed") over hours, in stages. **The Founders' Shaft** runs from the Hush to the open White. Let the sea come up it.
4. The cost: the moment *proceeds*. What's held in that pocket will move. Water on the White. And the founders' records in the Fourth Fathom's east gallery may flood. Sorle: "If I am right about the sea, we are about to find out in public."
5. The plan for **Assay Night**:
   - after the Assay, the whole Halcyon feasts and the lower Fathoms are emptiest;
   - Lucan and Teo cut the draw in the Cistern with Agathe's help;
   - Saskia steals the Hush key from Dunmore's desk;
   - Hester, Saskia and Sorle open the Hush and the Founders' Shaft and vent.
   - Hester must be the release, the thing she can't do.
6. Hester visits Aldous and they don't reconcile. She asks him how to let a pan down slow and he tells her, in detail, the whole craft. Unspoken things.
7. Training the release with Sorle and Orsolya's own technique ("Open your hand").

### Ch. 22 — The Midwinter Assay (21 Rime)
*Epigraph:* the Assay formula: "Discern. Keep. Return."
1. The Hall of Assay: the salt chandeliers lit, the galleries full of Ambry. Aldous is *there*, at the back, in a clean coat. (He came.)
2. Teo's Assay: brilliant discernment, a wobbly keep, and "Assayed." Saskia's: "Assayed with distinction." Lucan's: "Assayed with distinction."
3. Hester's Assay before Crisp, Bassano, Vey, Orsolya and Chancellor Holm (frail, motionless).
   - **Discernment:** perfect. She also names a sample as "quickened Hush, from the Fifth, east gallery" and sees Orsolya's face.
   - **Keeping:** a moth in flight.
   - **Return:** she must release the moth, and she does: she opens her hand. The moth flies. Crisp: "With distinction."
4. Orsolya's eyes meet hers across the hall. Orsolya knows that Hester has decided. Neither moves.
5. The Assay Night feast begins. The four slip away.

### Ch. 23 — The Old Tap (21 Rime, night)
*Epigraph:* Lowside saying: "Take her off the heat slow."
1. Interleaved through Hester's knowledge and timing, never leaving her POV:
   - Saskia gets the key.
   - Hester and Sorle descend through the empty Fifth to the Hush door.
   - At the agreed bell, the cut: Hester feels it happen as a change in the stay-line's taste. The sleepers' stillness stops arriving.
2. The Hush: the founders' working floor, humming, with ancient brass and a vast smooth floor of clear deep salt. Under it, something dark and enormous. (Not explained; saga.)
3. The quickening at the Hush's east end is already warm, wet and loud with surf. With the draw cut, it surges at once.
4. They find the Founders' Shaft and dissolve its salt plug with hot brine and crowbars. It's harder than planned. Sorle is old.
5. Alarms above. The skeleton Choir is called. Orsolya is coming.

### Ch. 24 — The Hush (21–22 Rime, night) — CLIMAX
*Epigraph:* none, or the founders' motto: "What is kept is not lost."
1. Orsolya arrives with Dunmore and two Fathomers. Confrontation in the roaring dark: "You've killed us all, chick."
2. The quickening breaches. Seawater bursts from the salt, black and cold and alive with the smell of weed.
3. The vent: Hester at the boundary, in brine to her knees, hands on the quickening's skin of salt. She has to *release*: give the moment its road up the shaft. She can't. She holds, her flaw, and the pressure climbs.
4. Orsolya sees the shaft taking water and understands that the vent could work. Her choice: she orders the Fathomers and Dunmore to hold the gallery stays above, keeping the Fifth from flooding. Dunmore flees. She holds alone with her two Fathomers.
5. Hester's release. She thinks of Tib: "let me sleep." She thinks of her father's hands drawing the fire. *Open your hand.* She lets go.
6. Saskia counts the stages: "Hold. Now. Hold. Now." Sorle times the arrears on a slate by lantern.
7. The sea goes up the shaft in pulses, roaring.
8. Hester's release-cost: the rush (heat, a racing heart, time flaring). She nearly dies. Sorle holds her.
9. Silence. The quickening is spent. The Hush hums lower.
*Ends:* Orsolya sitting on the Fifth-Fathom floor, Settled for hours, her hands open.

### Ch. 25 — The Gull (22–28 Rime)
*Epigraph:* a Rim ballad: "And the grey birds cried over Ostrine water / where no grey bird has cried since."
1. Dawn. Hester is carried up. A quarter-mile east of the Lantern, where the Well-Cap was, a **lake** steams in the frost: black water on the white salt, its edges already freezing. Half the Halcyon is on the shore, and Ambry's people are coming over the Causeway.
2. In the shallows: weed, green and living; small silver fish moving; a crab.
3. **The gull**: a grey-and-white bird struggling out of the water onto the ice, shaking, and then crying, *the sound*, and lifting, and flying over the White toward Ambry. The first in nine centuries.
4. Chancellor Holm stands at the Lantern's foot and watches it. Hester sees her face: grief, not surprise. Holm: "It was always going to come back, Miss Vail. We only hoped to be dead first." (Ambiguous.)
5. Consequences in quick, adult strokes:
   - Orsolya confesses to Gage and is arrested;
   - Pell is arrested in the city;
   - Dunmore has fled;
   - the Ferrers contract is leaked by Lucan to the Assize;
   - Vey's statement calls it "a natural quickening event";
   - the Fourth Fathom's east archive is flooded;
   - the Cold House sleepers are secure.
6. **Aske's return.** Hester and Sorle, with Physic, vent Aske's casing over six days in a warm bath, in stages, annealed.
   - On the sixth day his hand moves. He wakes mid-sentence: "—you can't, Orsolya, it'll backflow—" Then, blinking at Hester: "Oh. You tasted it." Then: "How long?" "Three months." "Have I missed the Assay?" "You have." "Did you pass?"
   - His right hand is stiff (partial Settle) and permanent.

### Ch. 26 — What the Salt Keeps (29–30 Rime)
*Epigraph:* The Halcyon motto: "What is kept is not lost."
1. Hester visits Orsolya in the Proctory cells. Two Lowside women. No forgiveness, but understanding. Orsolya: "Did it hurt? Letting go?" "Yes." "Good. Means you did it right."
2. The Cold House. Tib's casing is clear and dry. Aldous comes with her: his first visit in nine years, at the door, unable, then able. He touches the casing. Hester tells Tib the truth aloud, the whole thing, with her father listening. Her promise: "When I know how to bring you back properly, or when I know how to let you go, I'll do it. For you. Not for me. And I'll tell you first." Agathe's line returns: "The living keep clear."
3. Sorle formally enters her on his Bench as an Assayed aspirant. Lucan, ribs healed, and Hester: one moment of charged honesty, unresolved, adult. ("I find I don't mind owing you." "That's a start.")
4. The Ebbtide Vigil (night of 30 Rime). The Tiders walk out onto the White with candles to "meet the tide," and *this year there is water*. The whole city walks, a river of candles on the salt to the new lake. Teo in his old Tider shawl. Saskia with Pip. Lucan. Aldous walks a little way.
5. At the water's edge Hester and Teo kneel. The new crust forming at the shore. Teo reads it; Hester tastes it (the sea, tears, something living). Teo goes still and says: "There's someone." A voice in the salt, human, one word, a question: ***Aelith?*** Neither knows the language.
6. Final image: Hester looks out over the black water toward the Deep, the first real sea-wind of nine hundred years in her face, and doesn't know the answer, and finds she can bear not knowing. Close.

## Climactic payoff structure
1. **Plot:** the draw cut + the vent = the sleepers saved and the Halcyon saved.
2. **Mystery:** Aske returned and testifying; Orsolya's confession; Dunmore/Pell/Ferrers exposed.
3. **Character:** Hester's release (the flaw overcome in action, not speech). Orsolya's right choice. Saskia's loyalty (the key). Lucan defies his father (the cut). Teo listens (*Aelith*). Sorle vindicated privately.
4. **Theme:** keeping is not loving; let it move.
5. **Saga:** living water; a gull; a voice.
