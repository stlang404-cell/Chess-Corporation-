# THE HALCYON — Academy Architecture

## 1. Identity
- **Full name:** The Halcyon Collegium of the Grain. Commonly **the Halcyon**.
- **Emblem:** a halcyon, a kingfisher-like bird of the old sea, sitting on a single wave drawn as a straight line. The legend says the halcyon nested on the waves and stilled the sea while it brooded.
- **Motto:** *What is kept is not lost.*
- **Mission** (the Charter's wording): "to study, regulate, and preserve the gift of the Ebb for the good of the Rim."
- **Why it exists** (the real reasons):
  - It is the only institution that can *train* the sense. Self-taught workers like Hester are rare and limited.
  - It licenses grain-work, which makes it a regulator.
  - It holds the only access to the deep grain under the Shelf, which makes it a mine.
  - It is the Rim's great centre of research.
  - **[SPOILER]** It guards the Keeping.

## 2. Physical plan
The Halcyon is carved downward into the **Shelf**, a salt dome rising some 80 feet above the White, 2 miles east of Ambry. Depth is measured in **fathoms below the old waterline**, marked by a brass line set into the Gatehouse floor.

**Surface (the Crown)**
- **The Gatehouse**: the Causeway's end. It has a brass waterline in its floor ("the Line") that new aspirants step over on Matriculation Day.
- **The Lantern**: a 200-foot tower of salt-glass and iron. Its rotating mirrors gather sunlight into the **Lantern Well** and the light-shafts.
  - At dusk each day, during the **Lantern Hour**, Glaziers release a measure of kept noon light down the Well, so the refectory fills with daylight at nightfall.
- **The Rookery**: the student lodging. Terraced cells cut into the dome's western flank, each with a salt-glass window looking over the White toward Ambry. Two to a cell for aspirants. It is cold.
- **The Readers' Close**: faculty houses, built of stone, on the dome's lee side.
- **The Pans**: practice evaporation ponds coloured rose and green, used for Keeping and Lumen practicals.
- **The Choir Steps**: an outdoor amphitheatre of salt where the Stay Choir rehearses in summer.

**First Fathom: Teaching**
- **The Benches**: lecture rooms. The tiered salt benches are worn smooth and slightly hollowed by nine centuries of seated scholars.
- **The Long Table** (refectory): a single table, 300 feet long, carved from the living salt and directly under the Lantern Well.
- **The Hall of Assay**: a great domed hall with **salt chandeliers** (lattice-grown and lit from within by kept light). The Midwinter Assay is held here, with public galleries.
- **The Proctory**: the Chief Proctor's office and cells.

**Second Fathom: The Brinestacks (library)**
- Some 300,000 paper volumes, plus about 40,000 **brinescripts**: salt tablets carrying both text and kept impressions.
- The **Reading Wells** are small chambers with brine basins for reading tablets.
- **The Callow Desk**: the under-archivist's station, where Wynn Callow sits so still that people take him for furniture.
- The **Founders' Gallery** (restricted): what survived the 230 Y.E. fire.
- Readers' studies line the outer galleries.

**Third Fathom: The Cells (workrooms and laboratories)**
- Private Readers' workrooms.
- **Corvin Aske's workroom**: Cell 3-17.
- Glaziers' kilns and light-presses.
- **The Physic wards**: the Halcyon's own small brinery for staff and students.
- **The Lattice Yards**, where masons grow salt arches.

**Fourth Fathom: The Quiet Rooms and the Choir**
- **The Quiet Rooms**: the Halcyon's own collection of kept things. Among them:
  - a kept rose of 304 Y.E.;
  - the "Last Breath" of First Reader Ysolde, a puff of kept breath in a glass sphere;
  - a kept lightning bolt (frozen white fork, 712 Y.E.);
  - kept staff and students who await cures, plus the kept-beyond-return under the Stillborn clause. **Aske's casing is moved here in Ch. 7.**
- **The Choir**: a vast oval chamber where the **Stay Choir** practises and from which it descends to the Stays.

**Fifth Fathom: The Stays (restricted, Fathomers only)**
- A labyrinth of galleries where quickenings are held.
- Each Stay is a re-keeping supplied from the Choir through **stay-lines**: brass-sheathed brine channels.
- The **Warden's Gallery** is Orsolya's command post.
- **[SPOILER] The Cistern main line enters here, at the "Old Tap," a founders'-era brass valve-head.**

**Below the Fifth: The Hush**
- Sealed by a lattice door bearing the founders' emblem.
- The Hush is the founders' original working floor. It **hums**: a sub-audible pressure felt in the teeth.
- **The Founders' Shaft** is a sealed vertical shaft running from the Hush to the surface. It comes out on the open White a quarter-mile east of the Lantern, capped by a salt-crusted iron lid known as "the Well-Cap."

**Movement**
- **The Stair**: a great spiral running from the Crown to the Fifth Fathom.
  - Its walls are chalked with the names of the Settled, rewritten every year at the Silent Supper.
- **Brine-lifts**: counterweighted cages using brine tanks. They are slow, and they creak.

**Demographics.**
- About half the aspirants are Exchequer- or guild-sponsored.
- About a third pay their own fees, mostly from wealthy families.
- About a sixth are on scholarships, Reader's Privilege or Delvers' Guild bursaries.
- Roughly 40% are women. Many are married, some are parents, and some hold jobs in Ambry.

## 4. Hierarchy and governance
- **Aspirant** (three years, with an Assay each midwinter) → **Fellow** (after passing the third Assay) → **Reader** (by Conclave election) → **Warden** (by Chancellor's appointment) → **Chancellor** (elected for life by the Conclave).
- **The Conclave of Readers** governs academic matters. **[SPOILER] The Seal**: an inner circle of the Chancellor and two or three trusted Readers who know the founding secret. In 903 its members are **Chancellor Perpetua Holm** and **Deputy Chancellor Ilario Vey**.
- **Bench:** every aspirant must be attached to a **Reader's Bench**. A Reader sponsors at most six aspirants. An aspirant without a Bench at the Midwinter Assay is **sent down**.
- **Reader's Privilege:** once a decade a Reader may admit one candidate without fees, subject to the Entry Assay. Aske used his for Hester.

## 5. Admission
- The **Entry Assay** is a blind test of the sense: identify grain by grade and depth, detect a kept object among unkept ones, and attempt a first hold.
- Candidates need fees or a sponsor.
- Two intakes a year: the **Turning Intake** (autumn, the larger) and the **Thaw Intake** (spring).

## 6. The Midwinter Assay
- A public examination held on the shortest day (**21 Rime**) in the Hall of Assay. Families and the city watch from the galleries, and it is something between an exam and a theatre.
- Each aspirant performs three tasks before a panel of Readers:
  1. **Discernment**: identify unknown grains.
  2. **Keeping**: keep a set object, such as a falling drop, a flame or a moth.
  3. **Return**: return their own keeping cleanly.
- Grades are announced aloud: "Assayed," "Assayed with distinction," "Wanting" (resit allowed) or "Sent down."

## 7. Traditions and ceremonies
- **Stepping the Line**: on Matriculation Day, aspirants step over the brass waterline in the Gatehouse floor, "going below the sea."
- **The First Taste**: on the first night, each aspirant tastes a grain from each Fathom, laid on the Long Table in five dishes, to the cheers of second-years. The deepest one makes most people weep, involuntarily: salt tears.
- **The Lantern Hour**: the daily release of kept noon light at dusk in the refectory.
- **Lantern Night** (end of Russet): the Halcyon releases a summer's worth of kept light over Ambry on the first night longer than day. It is the city's great festival.
- **The Silent Supper** (in Cinder): the whole Halcyon holds still for one minute (the "Minute") in memory of the Settled, and the names on the Stair are rechalked.
- **The Midwinter Assay** (21 Rime).
- **The Ebbtide Vigil** (end of Rime): a Tider festival, observed by many aspirants informally.
- **Chalk-names**: the names of the Settled on the Stair walls.
- **Benchcords**: coloured cords on the grey gown that mark the Bench. Assay is white; Keeping, blue; Horology, black; Fathoming, iron-grey; Lumen, gold.

## 8. Student organisations
- **The Thirsty Society**: a drinking and debating club, founded 600 Y.E. Its motto: "Drink, for tomorrow we settle."
- **The Lowside Bench**: a mutual-aid society for scholarship and Lowside students. It meets in a disused brine-lift engine room.
- **The Stay Choir**: semi-professional. Aspirants with strong holds are recruited and paid a stipend, which is dangerous work and popular with poor students.
- **The Glaziers' Guildlet**: lumen students, rich and showy.
- **The Open Grain Circle**: illicit, pro-Quell.

## 9. Political factions within
- **Charterists** (majority; Chancellor Holm, Deputy Vey): defend the monopoly and secrecy and work closely with the Exchequer.
- **Reformers** (Crisp, Bassano and others): want an open Charter and free keeping for the poor.
- **Fathomers** (Orsolya's people): practical guardians of the Stays. They care about safety, not politics.
- **The heterodox** (Sorle, Aske privately): the Held Moment thinkers.

## 10. Institutional secrets
1. Quickenings have increased threefold since 896 (known to the Fathomers, the Chancellor and Vey). It has been hidden to avoid Exchequer panic and Delver strikes.
2. **[SPOILER]** The founding truth: the Seal.
3. The Founders' Gallery holds an unburned fragment of Ysolde's journal (Book 2).
4. The Cistern lines still connect the Halcyon to Ambry's oldest districts.
5. The Stillborn clause has been used to retain valuable kept persons as Halcyon property.

## 11. Relationship with the city
- The Halcyon is needed, resented and admired. Ambry depends on it for medicine, light and revenue.
- Lowside sees it as the place that takes its children and returns them Settled at fifty.
- The Exchequer treats it as an indispensable partner and a rival.
- Proctors are disliked in the city.

## 13. Faculty in Book One (teaching philosophies)
- **Maudelaine Crisp** (Keeping, 52). "The hold is a posture, not a feeling." Exacting, fair, Reformist, and secretly kind. She drills endlessly and disdains talent without discipline. She refuses to sponsor Hester (Ch. 8) because she's full, and says so honestly. In Ch. 22 she grades Hester "with distinction."
- **Benedek Sorle** (Horology, 64). "Every keeping is a lie we tell time, and time collects." Socratic, abusive and brilliant. He lectures by humiliation and secretly marks generously. He has been unsponsoring for nine years and has two aspirants.
- **Ottavio Bassano** (Lumen, 44). "Light wants to be spent." Theatrical, generous and gossipy, with a ring on every finger. Saskia's sponsor.
- **Corvin Aske** (Assay, 47). "The salt doesn't lie; people label it." An enthusiast, short-sighted, with ink-stained fingers. He hums. He keeps notebooks in his own shorthand.
- **Orsolya Thane** (Fathoming, Warden of the Lower Fathoms, 58). "Hold the line so the others can sleep." Teaches through doing; Lowside-warm, motherly, steel underneath.
- **Ilario Vey** (Deputy Chancellor, Brinescript, 51). Smooth and administrative. Lucan's family friend.
- **Perpetua Holm** (Chancellor, 78). Appears rarely; her voice is a whisper. Heavily Settled: she moves very little.
- **Halloran Gage** (Chief Proctor, 56). An ex-Concord soldier who wants order and closed files. Honest within narrow limits.
- **Wynn Callow** (Under-Archivist, 84). Advanced Settle. He speaks rarely and slowly and remembers everything.
