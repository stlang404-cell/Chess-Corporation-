# MAGIC SYSTEM SPECIFICATION — "Grain-work" (authoritative)

> CANON-LOCKED. Any manuscript passage contradicting this document is an error unless this document is formally amended in DECISIONS.md.

## 0. Summary in one breath
Salt from the Hollow Sea ("stillsalt", "grain") can hold a thing still in time.
- A trained worker dissolves grain into **live brine**, enters **the hold** (a deliberate bodily stillness), and lends that stillness to the brine. The brine crystallises around a target and **keeps** it: paused, unchanging, alive if it was alive.
- Kept salt can be **read**, giving the impression of the kept moment, and **returned**: dissolved, so that the kept thing resumes exactly where it stopped.
- Nothing kept is free. Every keeping accrues **arrears**, a pressure to resume that must be paid when released.
- Workers pay with their own body-time.

## 1. Origins
- **Public belief (Reader canon, Book 1):** In the Ebb, nine centuries ago, the Ostrine Sea receded in one night. Its salt, "touched by the catastrophe," carries held stillness. Why it does is officially unknown and philosophically debated. The orthodox view is that stillness is a mineral property formed under unique pressure. The heterodox view, "Sorle's Folly," is that the salt is a held moment.
- **Objective truth (SPOILER; Series Bible only):** The sea was kept. In 0 Y.E. the Quiet Company performed the Great Keeping, stilling the Ostrine Sea, the fleet of Ilisse and the island cities in a single instant. The water was held in crystalline form, so the "salt" is the sea's moment made solid. Stillsalt carries stillness because it *is* stillness: a fragment of one held instant. Mining and dissolving grain spends that instant; each dissolved dram is a sliver of the sea's moment allowed to pass. The art of keeping was **Ostrin** in origin (they kept fish, medicine, the dead). The Marrane stole it in war.

## 2. Fundamental laws (as taught in the Halcyon Primer)
1. **Salt keeps.** Live brine, worked in the hold, crystallises around a target and keeps it in its present state.
2. **Salt remembers.** Kept salt holds an impression of what it kept. In the hold, a worker in contact with it perceives that impression: images, sounds, sensations, temperature.
3. **Salt returns.** Kept salt dissolved by a worker in the *release* (the reversed hold) gives its moment back. The kept thing resumes from exactly where it paused.
4. **What is kept is owed.** (The Law of Arrears.) A keeping accrues pressure in proportion to the *mass* kept, the *duration*, and the *liveliness* of what is held. Living things accrue faster than stone; motion faster than rest. Arrears discharge on return as heat, sound, a pressure shock (the "snap"), and in great keepings as violent motion.
5. **Still runs to quick.** (Taught in Year One, Horology and Fathoming.) Stillness moves through *physically continuous live brine* from the stiller to the quicker, the way heat moves from hot to cold through metal. It does not jump gaps. No symbolic or sympathetic links exist. Dry salt, glass and air insulate.

**Corollary taught by Sorle (Horology):** Old keepings grow *stiller* over time; they saturate. Old human keepings are among the stillest things in the world.
*Objective reason:* the casing draws on the held life inside it, and on the deep grain's own held moment.

## 3. Materials
- **Stillsalt / grain.** Hollow-Sea salt only. Salt from elsewhere (rock-salt from the Varrow, salt from evaporated foreign seawater) is **dead salt** and keeps nothing.
- **Grades by depth** (the Exchequer schedule):
  - **Shelf** (surface to ~3 fathoms; weak): food, curing, cheap keepings of hours to weeks.
  - **Middling** (~3–40 fathoms): trade keepings, records, ordinary kept light.
  - **Deep** (~40–300 fathoms; costly): medical keepings of people, long archival keepings, fine lumen.
  - **Hush grain** (below the Fifth Fathom; restricted, never sold): enormously still and enormously pressured. It keeps anything clear, almost instantly, but its arrears are monstrous. **Its sale is a felony under the Charter.**
- **Quickened grain:** grain from near a quickening.
  - Signatures: warmth to the touch; inclusions of liquid water inside crystals ("live beads"); readings carry vivid sea-sounds; it tastes *sweet-metallic and wet*, like a bitten tongue (Hester's description).
  - Highly unstable. Prone to "spitting" (small shocks) when dissolved.
- **Live brine:** grain dissolved in water. It must be worked within minutes or it "dies": the stillness disperses into the water and leaks off at the surface.
- **Kept light / lumen salt:** grain crystallised in sunlight keeps the light. Dissolved, it releases that light.
  - A jar's worth makes an amber lantern that lasts hours.
  - Released summer light is warmer and gold; winter light is white-blue.
  - Kept light carries a *faint* heat (its arrears) and sometimes the sounds of its day: birdsong from a summer noon.

## 4. Access and aptitude
- **Grain-sense:** the perception of stillness.
  - Roughly 1 in 300 people have a usable sense; perhaps 1 in 5,000 a strong one.
  - It is heritable only loosely, and its strength varies with training and attention.
  - **Modality varies by person:** pitch (most common), pressure, temperature, taste. Hester senses it as **taste and weight**: sweetness at the back of the tongue, heaviness behind the eyes. Teo hears it as **voice-like sound**. Orsolya feels **cold in the hands**. Saskia sees **colour fringes** at the edges of light. Lucan feels **rhythm**, as if counting.
- **The hold:** learned by anyone with the sense.
  - Steps: slow breath; drop the heart rate; quiet the musculature; still the attention.
  - Depth of hold determines strength. Steadiness determines precision. *Shape* (what the worker intends to keep) determines the casing.
  - **Every worker leaves a characteristic grain-pattern in casings, a "hand,"** like handwriting. Hester's hand is dense and squared, assayer-tidy. **Orsolya's is a widdershins spiral from her left-wrist counter-turn.** Crisp's is laminar. Aske's is fine and radial. Skilled readers can identify a hand by touch or taste.
- **The release:** the reverse of the hold. The worker *gives up* stillness, letting go of intent while in contact with the keeping in brine. It is harder for most and is temperamentally linked: controlling people find release difficult. Hester is **excellent at keeping and poor at release** (Book One arc).

## 5. Costs (the worker's account)
- **Lag.** Lending deep stillness costs the worker their own momentum. After a significant hold the worker "lags": lost seconds to minutes during which they are effectively paused, unaware, unmoving.
  - A strong worker keeping a person for the first time might lag 2–5 minutes.
  - Lag is dangerous: a lagged worker cannot react.
- **Cold and thirst.** Holds chill the body and dry it. Workers drink constantly ("the Halcyon thirst") and wear wool year-round.
- **The Settle** (chronic). Over a career, lent stillness does not fully return.
  - Symptoms: joints stiffen; the heart slows permanently; tears and sweat leave salt crusts; hair whitens early; the worker begins to sit for long intervals without noticing ("drifting").
  - Advanced Settle: a worker sits unmoving for hours or days. Final Settle is death by stillness: the heart simply slows to a stop.
  - A heavy career lasts about 25–35 years. Stay Choir workers settle fastest.
- **The rush (cost of release).** Releasing a large keeping hands the worker back a surge of motion: heat, a racing heart, sweat that will not salt, a sensation of time flaring *forward* (sounds too fast, light too bright). Minor releases produce a flush. A major release (a person, a quickening) can stop the heart by overdriving it. Lag is the cost of keeping; the rush is the cost of letting go.
- **Arrears shock.** Returns discharge arrears. A returned day-old keeping of an apple: a faint warmth. A returned decade-old human keeping: heat, a cracking sound and a pressure that can break bones unless *vented* (dissolved gradually). Medical returns of people are therefore done slowly over hours in warm brine baths by Physic.

## 6. Constraints and failure modes
- **Keeping does not heal. Returning does not heal.** A kept dying man returns dying. This is the cruelty of the Brineries: keeping waits for cures that may never come.
- **No keeping without brine contact.** The target must be wetted with or immersed in live brine. Spray, pour, immersion.
- **Partial keepings are possible but fragile:** a wound, a limb, a sound in a jar. Boundaries tend to fracture.
- **Living keepings stay clear; dead keepings cloud grey ("death-bloom").** A casing around a body that was dead or dying at the moment of keeping develops a grey bloom within days. *[Clue in Book One: Aske's casing stays clear. He is alive.]*
  - The Proctor's excuse: Hush grain keeps everything clear. **This is false. Hush grain keeps clear more quickly, but death-bloom still appears within a month.** Callow knows this; Ch. 19.
- **Weeping.** Brine beading on a casing's surface means the casing is losing stillness faster than it holds it. Causes: age, damp, cracking, or *a draw* (stillness pulled off through a brine contact). Natural weeping is slow and evenly distributed. **Draw-weeping beads in lines toward the draw point and tastes quick.** *[Clue, Ch. 1.]*
- **Recasting:** adding fresh brine and stillness to a failing casing. It works against natural weeping. Against a draw it fails within weeks.
- **Spitting:** unstable or quickened grain shocking when dissolved.
- **Quickenings:** spots in deep grain where the held moment pushes through.
  - Signs in rising order: warmth → live beads → sea-sounds → smell of weed and brine → wet salt → running water → jets.
  - Orthodox handling: the **Stay**, a re-keeping that adds stillness to the pocket, supplied by teams of workers holding in synchrony (the **Stay Choir**, who sing slow to lock their breathing).
  - Stays saturate the pocket temporarily. Quickenings recur and *grow*.
- **Venting** (Hester's innovation, Book One climax): instead of adding stillness, allow the held moment to *complete* a small increment by giving the pressure a path to a place where its motion is harmless. Dissolve the pocket's boundary in controlled steps, let water flow up a shaft, release in staged pulses.
  - It draws on: brine-pan practice ("take her off the heat slow and let her talk," Ch. 3), glass annealing (Saskia, Ch. 10/16) and Sorle's arrears mathematics (Ch. 14). Aske's notes reached the same idea (Ch. 19).
  - Its cost is that the moment *does proceed*: the sea is actually let through, and whatever was kept in that pocket moves again.

## 7. Specialisations and interactions
- Keeping + Lumen → kept light.
- Reading + Brinescript → salt archives: text cut into salt tablets, plus impressions of the scribe's moment.
- Keeping + Physic → medical keepings, haemorrhage holds, anaesthetic part-holds ("numbing"), keeping organs for transplant (rare, Book 4+).
- Fathoming + Choir → stays.
- Sonance → kept sound: recordings of song, speeches, the "Roar." The **Roar** is the white noise heard when reading old Hollow-Sea grain, and it is the sea's moment: waves, wind, gulls, and very faintly voices. Most readers hear only roar. Deep listeners (Teo) catch fragments.
- Sanguine Grain (forbidden): the salt of human tears and blood, worked with grain, can *key* a keeping to a person, so that it opens only for them. Forbidden after the Red Grain murders of 611 Y.E. Book 5.

## 8. Social and economic consequences
- **Medicine:** the Brineries keep the dying (bloom fever, cancers, wounds) until cures exist. There are private brineries for the rich and charity houses for the poor.
- **The fee economy:** families pay monthly keeping fees for decades. Debt bondage is common.
- **Trade:** kept fruit, kept fish (inland!), kept milk, so preservation underwrites long-distance trade.
- **Light:** the kept-light trade lights the rich; the Lantern Tithe (Book 2) taxes it.
- **Records:** salt archives. Courts accept "kept testimony": salt-read impressions of an event, admissible under strict rules.
- **Labour:** the Delvers' Guild mines grain under Halcyon licence. Collapses and sinks kill delvers. The Lowside district is built on this labour.
- **Mortality of workers:** the Settle makes grain-work a sacrificial profession. The city honours its Settled; the Halcyon chalks their names on the Stair.

## 9. Ethical dilemmas (in-world debates)
- Is a kept person alive? (Legally yes, while their casing is clear. Kept-beyond-return is legally dead under the "Stillborn clause": a person kept in grain so deep that return would kill them by arrears.)
- Who may sign a keeping? (Next of kin; the kept person's own wish is not legally required if they are incapacitated. This is a Hester wound.)
- Should the poor be kept at all, when keeping means decades of fees?
- Deep mining versus the Tider prohibition.
- Is kept testimony truthful? Impressions are partial.

## 10. Unknown phenomena (open questions, in-world)
- Why only Hollow-Sea salt? (Truth: it is the sea's moment.)
- Why does the Roar sometimes sound like speech? (Truth: it is speech.)
- The **Halcyon bird** of the founders' emblem: extinct? (Truth: kept in the moment, Book 7.)
- Why do quickenings smell of *living* weed, not rotten? (Truth: it is living weed, paused.)
- Why are the oldest records of the Ebb so few? (Truth: destroyed by the Seal.)
- Why does the Hush hum? (Truth: the Founders' working is still active.)

## 11. Rules for writers (hard limits)
- No magic in Book One that the reader has not seen at least once at smaller scale.
- A worker can keep only what live brine touches.
- Return is never instant for large or living keepings without consequences.
- Stillness never travels without continuous live brine.
- No healing by magic.
- No keeping of thoughts or feelings directly; only bodies, matter, light, sound, heat.
- Lag must be shown whenever a major hold occurs.
