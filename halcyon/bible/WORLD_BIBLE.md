# WORLD BIBLE — The Rim and the Hollow Sea

> Internal author reference. Sections marked **[SPOILER]** contain objective truths not yet revealed to readers.

## 1. Geography

**The Hollow Sea ("the White").**
- An elongated basin roughly 300 miles east–west and 120 miles north–south, floored by a salt crust.
- The crust runs from a few feet thick at the margins (the Shelf) to an unknown depth in the centre (**the Deep**). Deep borings have reached 900 fathoms without finding rock.
- Its surface is white, glazed and polygonally cracked into plates ("tiles") a few yards across.
- Hazards:
  - **sinks:** soft brine pockets under crust, which swallow carts and people;
  - **whiteouts:** salt storms;
  - **glare-blindness;**
  - **the Pull:** a mirage effect that makes distances lie.
- Brine pools at the margins are tinted **rose-red and copper-green** by halophile algae and brine shrimp ("pinkwater", "greenwater").
- At night, in the dry season, the White **sings**: thermal contraction makes the plates tick and chime.

**The Rim** (the habitable band around the White):
- **Western Rim.**
  - **Ambry** (pop. ~180,000), the largest free city. It is built on the old western shore, and its lower districts sit on the former seabed.
  - The **Halcyon** stands 2 miles east of Ambry on a salt dome called **the Shelf** (also "the Knoll"), joined to the city by the raised **Causeway**.
  - The river **Ambrel** once fed the sea; it now ends in brackish marshes north of the city called **the Weeps**.
- **Northern Rim.** The **Varrow Range**: snow mountains, rock-salt mines (dead salt) and the mountain kingdom of **Hask**, a buyer of kept medicine. The town of **Ostmere** sits at the passes.
- **Eastern Rim.** **Quell** (pop. ~70,000), the glass city. It is home of the **Quell Lodge**, a rival academy founded in the Schism of 512 Y.E. that is more open, poorer and more radical. Quell glass is the best in the world.
- **Southern Rim.** **Harl** and the **Mardens**: farmland on dried river deltas, and the seat of the Order of the Returning Tide's mother house, **Tidewater Abbey**. Beyond them lies the red desert called **the Scald**.
- **The Deep.** Uninhabited. Salt-walkers (**the Wend**) cross it on seasonal routes and sing to the salt. **[SPOILER: the drowned island-city of Ilisse is held beneath the Deep. Its tallest bell-tower lies only ~60 fathoms below the surface, visible through clear deep salt in certain seasons (Book 3).]**

**Climate.**
- Continental: hot dry summers, cold clear winters with hard frosts.
- Autumn rains on the Rim are brief. The White itself almost never rains.
- Winds: the **Sere**, a dry east wind in autumn that carries salt dust into Ambry.

## 2. History (public, with [SPOILER] truths)

Calendar: **Years of the Ebb (Y.E.)**. Book One opens on **1 Russet 903 Y.E.**

- **Before the Ebb.**
  - *Public:* the Ostrine Sea, an inland sea, rich in fish. The Marrane coastal kingdoms traded on it. The island cities of the **Ostrin** were a sea-people with their own language; little is known of them, "lost with the sea."
  - **[SPOILER]** The Ostrin of Ilisse were a thalassocracy and the originators of salt-keeping. They kept fish, medicine, and their honoured dead. The Marrane fought a twenty-year war against them (c. 20 B.E.–0) and were losing.
- **0 Y.E., the Ebb.**
  - *Public:* in one night the sea withdrew, leaving salt. Most coastal people survived because the water *went*, not came. The Order of the Returning Tide teaches that the sea withdrew in judgement and will return when the Rim is worthy.
  - **[SPOILER]** A Marrane society of salt-workers, the **Quiet Company**, led by **Ysolde Marrane** and **Corrant Hask**, used stolen Ostrin craft and an enormous synchronised hold of some three thousand workers (most of whom died Settled) to perform the **Great Keeping**. It stilled the entire sea in a single instant, with the Ilisse fleet on it, the island cities in it, and some 200,000 Ostrin.
- **3 Y.E.** The Quiet Company founds **the Halcyon** on the Shelf "to study the gift of the Ebb." Ysolde is First Reader. Its motto: *What is kept is not lost.* **[SPOILER: its true founding purpose was to guard the Keeping and its secret. The inner circle is called **the Seal**.]**
- **c. 40–90 Y.E.** The first Brineries. Grain-work spreads.
- **140 Y.E.** The **Charter of the Grain**, granted by the Rim League. The Halcyon gains the monopoly on licensing grain-work.
- **212 Y.E.** The Salt Exchequer is founded in Ambry to tax grain.
- ****[SPOILER]** 230 Y.E.** The Seal destroys most surviving pre-Ebb records. This is publicly remembered as "the Brinestacks Fire."
- **341–349 Y.E.** The **Delving Wars**: a labour revolt of the miners. They end with the Delvers' Guild Accord.
- **512 Y.E.** The **Quell Schism**: a third of the Readers, led by **Aster Quell**, leave over secrecy to found the Lodge at Quell.
- **611 Y.E.** The **Red Grain murders**. A Reader, **Mattias Horne**, used human-salt keyings to make unopenable keepings of his victims. Sanguine Grain is banned.
- **788 Y.E.** The Great Bloom: a lung-fever epidemic. The Brineries overflow, and the Cold House opens as a charity brinery.
- **871 Y.E.** **"Sorle's Folly"**: Reader Benedek Sorle presents the Held Moment Hypothesis to the Conclave. He is demolished in debate, chiefly by a young Fathomer named **Orsolya Thane**, and censured.
- **892 Y.E.** Tobias Vail, 14, is kept at the Cold House with bloom fever.
- **896–903 Y.E.** Quickenings in the Halcyon's Fifth Fathom increase about threefold. This is publicly unknown.
- **902 Y.E.** Orsolya Thane, Warden of the Lower Fathoms, reopens the founders' Cistern lines and begins drawing stillness from the Cold House's charity keepings.
- **903 Y.E.** Book One.

## 3. Politics and law
- **Ambry**: a free city governed by **the Assize**, a council of 31 guild and ward representatives, with a **Lord Mayor** chosen annually. The real power sits with three bodies:
  1. **The Halcyon.** Holds the Charter. Licenses all grain-work and certifies grades. Its Proctors police grain crime.
  2. **The Salt Exchequer.** A civic-mercantile body that taxes and trades grain and holds export licences. It is led by the **Lord Exchequer, Aurel Ferrers** (Lucan's father).
  3. **The Delvers' Guild.** The miners' union. It controls labour and can halt supply.
- **The Rim Concord**: a loose treaty league of Rim cities (Ambry, Quell, Harl, Ostmere) for trade and mutual defence, with a weak Concord Diet.
- **Law** (Charter offences):
  - unlicensed keeping;
  - sale of Hush grain (felony);
  - falsified grading (fraud);
  - Sanguine Grain (capital);
  - improper return of a kept person (homicide).
- **Courts:** the Assize Court for city matters; the **Proctory Court** within the Halcyon for grain offences by its members.
- **Kept-person law.** A kept person is legally alive while their casing is clear. Next of kin may sign keeping and release. The **Stillborn clause** treats a person kept beyond return as legally dead: their estate passes and their casing becomes property of the brinery or the Halcyon.

## 4. Economy
- **Currency:** the **sol** (silver), divided into 20 **flecks** (copper). A gold **crown** is worth 25 sols and is rarely seen in Lowside.
- **Wages and prices:**
  - Hester's customs wage: 14 sols/month.
  - A dock labourer: about 7 sols.
  - The Cold House charity keeping fee: 6 sols/month.
  - A private brinery: 30+ sols/month.
  - Halcyon fees: 120 sols/year.
- **Grain prices:** Shelf, 2 flecks/dram; Middling, 1 sol/ounce; Deep, 12 sols/ounce; Hush grain is never legally sold, and its black-market price is about 100 sols/ounce.
- **Main trades:**
  - grain and kept goods;
  - kept light;
  - Quell glass;
  - Harl grain, meaning wheat (a constant pun: "Harl grain or Halcyon grain?");
  - Hask timber and furs;
  - brine-boiling, which produces dead salt for ordinary table use from Weeps marsh-brine;
  - landship-building.

## 5. Transport
- **Landships** ("salt-yachts"): wind-driven sledges on iron runners that sail the White. They range from two-person skiffs to great three-masted freighters, and they are fast, often 20+ knots in a good wind. Ambry's old harbour is now a **dry harbour**: quays standing above salt where landships moor, sails furled, runners chocked.
- **The Causeway**: a raised stone-and-salt road, 2 miles from Ambry's Eastgate to the Halcyon Gatehouse, lit at night with kept-light posts.
- **Ox-drays**: for overland Rim trade.
- **Brine-lifts**: counterweight cages in the Halcyon, balanced by brine tanks.
- **[SPOILER] The Cistern**: a network of founders'-era brine tunnels and pipes under Ambry. Officially the old municipal brine supply, bricked up in 400 Y.E. Its main line runs from the Great Cistern under Coldwater Street (now beneath the Cold House) east to the Halcyon's Fifth Fathom.

## 6. Cultures, peoples, naming
- **Rim Marrane (Ambry, Western Rim).** The majority. Names are Rim-English in flavour with Marrane survivals: Hester, Aldous, Tobias, Agathe, Corvin, Maudelaine, Halloran; surnames like Vail, Aske, Crisp, Pell, Fenn, Mallory, Gage, Callow, Ferrers.
  - Lowside speech is clipped and practical, with "I'll not," "wants doing," "ta," "chick" (an endearment).
  - Upper-ward speech is formal and Latinate.
- **Quellish (Eastern Rim).** Glassmakers; clipped, ironic, with a reputation for mercenariness and candour. Names: Saskia, Jory, Orme, Pip (Philippa), Aster.
- **Harlish (Southern Rim).** Farming and Tider country; slow, devout, hospitable. Names: Teodor (Teo), Anselm, Benedek (Sorle is of Harlish birth), Ama.
- **Haskish (mountains).** Names in -ik, -vo: Ivo, Radik.
- **The Wend** (salt-walkers). A nomad people of the White who hold the salt sacred and sing to it. They are reputed to hear the Roar better than anyone. Book 3.
- **Ostrin (lost) [SPOILER].** Language fragments survive in place names, in "Ostrine," and in the Wend songs. Ostrin phonology: open vowels, -ae, -ille, -or, liquid consonants (Ilisse, Thaelor, Vaimé). The first Ostrin word heard in Book One's final chapter is **"Aelith?"**, glossed in Book 4 as "Is it over?" / "Has it passed?"
- **Orsolya Thane**'s family were Haskish-Lowside, which explains "Orsolya."

## 7. Religion and philosophy
- **The Order of the Returning Tide ("Tiders").**
  - Teaching: the sea withdrew in judgement and will return when the Rim is reconciled.
  - Practice: they oppose deep mining as wounding the sea's bed. Their holy night is the **Ebbtide Vigil** (end of Rime), when the faithful walk out onto the White with candles to "meet the tide." They keep the dead **unkept**: they refuse brining, believing the kept cannot meet the returning sea.
  - Organisation: abbeys, hospices and charity. Mother Agathe of the Cold House is a lay Tider; the house tolerates brining as "the people's choice."
  - **[SPOILER: their prophecy is literally true, and their founding was by Marrane survivors who suspected the truth.]**
- **Civic Stillism.** The secular Halcyon ethos: preservation as civilisation, the past as a resource. "What is kept is not lost."
- **The Open Grain** (Quell): knowledge should be shared, and the Charter is a tyranny.
- Common oaths: "Salt and sorrow." "Tide take it." "Still as a Reader." "Sweet sea" (mild, Lowside).

## 8. Daily life, food, art
- **Food:**
  - **kept-fish**: perch and eels from Hask lakes, sold kept;
  - salt-crust bread;
  - **brine-pickles;**
  - **pinkwater shrimp paste;**
  - Harl wheat porridge;
  - **saltcake**: a sweet caramel cake with crunchy grain-free salt flakes, a Halcyon student staple;
  - Hask honey;
  - **bitter-orange from the Mardens**;
  - **"halcyon tea"**: black tea with a pinch of shelf salt and milk.
- **Drink:** small beer; Harl cider; "Quell clear" (a fierce spirit). Water is precious on the White.
- **Art:** salt sculpture (temporary, carved in the dry season); **kept-sound recitals**; Quell glass; Lantern Night light-painting; the **Rim ballads** (sea songs for a sea no one has seen).
- **Architecture:**
  - Ambry: rough grey stone and salt-glazed brick, with steep roofs against the occasional hail.
  - Halcyon: carved salt (smooth, translucent at edges, faintly pink in lamplight), lattice-grown arches and salt-glass windows.
- **Clothing:** wool year-round for grain-workers; the Halcyon gown is undyed grey wool with a coloured cord for Bench rank; customs officers wear blue.

## 9. Creatures and ecology
- **Salt-larks**: small white birds nesting on brine-pool margins.
- **Pale herons**: tall wading birds with pink crests that stand in pinkwater.
- **Brine shrimp**: blooming red in season.
- **Lurchers**: lean Rim hounds bred to sense sinks.
- **Weep crabs**: small green crabs of the brackish Weeps.
- **Salt-flies**: biting.
- **Salt roses**: crystal formations that "grow" in a single night after rare rains, a sign of luck.
- **Night-chime**: the singing of salt tiles at night.
- **Gulls:** none. Gulls do not exist in the Rim; they are known only from old paintings and ballads. **[In Ch. 2 Hester hears a gull in a reading and does not recognise the sound; Teo identifies it from a hymn-book woodcut description in Ch. 9. In Ch. 25 a living gull flies out of the vented sea.]**

## 10. Warfare and diplomacy
- There has been no major Rim war in 200 years.
- City watches; the Halcyon Proctors (about 80); Exchequer **excisemen**.
- Hask is a buyer of kept medicine and a source of mercenaries.
- Quell and Ambry are in permanent commercial rivalry; the Lodge is barred from the Charter.
- Book 5's civil war turns on Exchequer excisemen versus Delver militias versus a Halcyon split.

## 11. Technology
- Roughly a late eighteenth-century analogue. Printing presses; clocks and watches (Hask makers); canals in the Mardens; glass optics (Quell); early chemistry; no steam power. Hydraulics are used in the brine-lifts.
- Kept light reduces candle use among the wealthy.
- Firearms exist (matchlock-era, rare) but are not used in Book One.

## 12. Historical controversies (live debates in 903)
1. The Held Moment Hypothesis ("Sorle's Folly") versus the Mineral Theory.
2. Deep-mining quotas: Exchequer pressure to mine deeper versus Delver safety and Tider objection.
3. The fee economy and the charity brineries: Lowside agitation for a free keeping law.
4. The Quell Lodge's petition to break the Charter.
5. The Stillborn clause: lawyers dispute whether kept-beyond-return persons are dead.
6. The Brinestacks Fire of 230 Y.E.: was it an accident?
