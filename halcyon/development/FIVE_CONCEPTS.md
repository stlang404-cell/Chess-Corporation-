# Five Original Series Concepts — Development & Evaluation

Each concept covers: premise; academy; protagonist; magical mechanism; emotional foundation; principal conflict; central mystery; wider civilisation; seven-book potential; ultimate resolution.

---

## Concept A — THE HALCYON (salt that keeps time)

**Premise.** Nine hundred years ago an inland sea vanished in a single night and left behind a white plain of salt three hundred miles across. Salt from that plain does something no other salt does. Crystallising around a thing, it holds the thing *still* in time: the instant is preserved, readable, and can be returned. A whole civilisation now runs on mining, licensing and spending that salt.

**Academy.** The Halcyon is a university carved downward into a salt dome on the old seabed. Its levels are measured in *fathoms*: depth below the vanished waterline. It is a university, a regulator and a licensing monopoly all at once. It trains the physicians who "keep" the dying until cures exist, the assayers who certify salt, the archivists who read history out of crystal, and the glaziers who sell bottled daylight.

**Protagonist.** Hester Vail, 33, a customs-house assayer from the brine-boiling district. Eleven years ago she had her dying fourteen-year-old brother *kept* in a salt casing, against his own whispered wish. She has paid for his keeping ever since.

**Mechanism.** Three operations: **Keep, Read, Return.** The worker lends their own bodily stillness to the salt as its template. The universal cost is *arrears*: everything kept accrues pressure to resume, and that pressure must be paid somewhere.

**Emotional foundation.** Grief, and the difference between keeping and loving. Holding on versus letting go, played out at three scales: a sister, an institution, a civilisation.

**Principal conflict.** Book One: the city's kept sleepers are "weeping," and their casings are failing. A Reader who noticed is found dead, kept in salt.
Saga: the economy of the whole world consumes something it does not understand.

**Central mystery.** What is the Hollow Sea? *Truth:* the sea did not vanish. It was *kept*, stilled in one instant by the Halcyon's founders, and the people living in it were stilled too. Every grain mined and dissolved spends a little of their held moment. The founders stilled the sea to stop a war they were losing. They then wrote themselves into history as survivors of a natural disaster.

**Civilisation.** The Rim states around the salt plain; salt trade; the Exchequer; the Ebb faith, which prophesies the sea's return; a rival academy across the plain; nomadic salt-walkers; the mountain kingdoms that buy kept medicine.

**Seven-book potential.** Very high. It escalates from a city mystery, to political economy, to an expedition across the salt, to first contact with the held moment, to war, to a partial release, to the final reckoning.

**Ultimate resolution.** The world chooses to *return* the sea, slowly, over a generation. It accepts the end of salt magic as it is known, and the restoration of a people it erased. Hester completes the long return. She also lets her brother go: his keeping is finally *returned*, not recast.

## Concept B — THE ECHO CONSERVATORY

**Premise.** Every sound ever made persists, faintly, in stone. In a few canyons of perfect acoustics the dead can still be heard. Echo-scholars retrieve historical speech. They can also *re-voice* echoes so that the sounded event partly repeats. The echo of a bridge collapsing can collapse a bridge.

**Academy.** The Conservatory is cut into the walls of the Choral Gorge, with listening galleries, silence vaults and a "dead room" where nothing echoes.

**Protagonist.** A 40-year-old court stenographer who is going deaf.

**Mechanism.** Retrieval and re-voicing of persisted sound. The cost is that the scholar's own voice gradually fails to persist: they become unrecorded, unremembered.

**Emotional foundation.** Testimony, being heard, the ethics of recording the dead.

**Conflict / mystery.** An echo is found of a voice that has not spoken yet.

**Civilisation.** A legal culture built on echo-testimony.

**Seven-book potential.** Good, but the future-voice mystery tends toward a time-loop plot, which narrows it.

**Resolution.** The future voice is the protagonist's own last act.

## Concept C — THE POSTAL COLLEGIUM OF TRUE ADDRESS

**Premise.** Anything with a true address can be reached, by letter, by object, by person. Magic is the discovery and forgery of addresses. The imperial postal service is the most powerful institution on the continent.

**Academy.** A collegium for postmasters-arcane, built around an infinite sorting hall.

**Protagonist.** A 29-year-old dead-letter clerk who reads undeliverable mail.

**Mechanism.** Addresses as fundamental units of location-identity. The cost is that the worker loses their own address and becomes progressively harder to find, even by friends.

**Emotional foundation.** Connection, loneliness, the people the system loses.

**Conflict / mystery.** Letters are arriving addressed to places that do not exist.

**Seven-book potential.** Good. Whimsical, but risks feeling like a single clever idea stretched across seven books.

**Resolution.** The non-existent places are the erased provinces of a war the empire forgot.

## Concept D — THE SECOND LAMP

**Premise.** A second, invisible sun crosses the night sky. A few people perceive its radiance, and magic is the craft of catching and refracting it. The academy is an observatory-university on a mountain.

**Protagonist.** A 35-year-old lighthouse keeper.

**Mechanism.** Refraction of night-light through lenses and the eye. The cost is the gradual loss of ordinary sight.

**Emotional foundation.** Vision, faith, what we choose not to see.

**Conflict / mystery.** The second sun is dimming. *Truth:* it is an artefact, built.

**Seven-book potential.** Moderate. "The sun is artificial" is a familiar reveal in SF-fantasy.

---

## Concept E — THE WEIR COLLEGIUM

**Premise.** An invisible current, the Flux, flows through the land like water. Mages are its hydraulic engineers, building weirs, locks and sluices. Whoever controls the great weirs controls the land.

**Protagonist.** A 31-year-old canal lock-keeper.

**Mechanism.** Channelling and damming the Flux. The cost: dammed Flux pools in the engineer's body.

**Emotional foundation.** Control and flow, infrastructure and care.

**Conflict / mystery.** The upstream source is being diverted.

**Seven-book potential.** Moderate. It is mechanically elegant but emotionally cooler, and the "flowing energy" magic is a well-worn shape.

## Selection: Concept A, THE HALCYON

**Why it wins.** It does not win on having the most lore. Its mechanism *is* its theme. Keep / Read / Return maps exactly onto grief, history and restitution. The protagonist's private wound (keeping her brother) mirrors the civilisation's founding crime (keeping the sea), so every personal choice carries saga weight and every saga revelation lands as personal. The academy is irreplaceable: a university literally dug into the evidence of the crime.

**Weaknesses identified and refinements made:**
1. *"Stasis/preservation" is broad and easily static.* → Add the **arrears** law. Keeping is never free and never truly still. Pressure builds, and pressure produces events: weeping, quickenings, ruptures. Stillness becomes a source of plot.
2. *A salt-white world risks visual monotony.* → Vary the palette: brine pools tinted red and green by halophile algae (real-world grounded); kept-light lanterns of amber daylight; black lattice-glass; the red of forbidden human salt; the blue of deep ice-salt.
3. *Overlap with* The Salt Oracle *(mentor dies → investigation → save one or save the college).* →
   - The dead Reader is *not* Hester's mentor. She has known him eleven days.
   - The death is not a murder plot but a concealed manslaughter.
   - The dilemma's resolution *rejects* the binary through an insight: venting.
   - The central question is economic and historical, not a ghost/oracle question.
4. *Overlap with "turned to salt" imagery* (Lot's wife; *The Salt King*). → Our people are never *turned into* salt. They are *kept within* a casing of it, alive and paused, and can be returned. The casing is clear, like glass, not a pillar.
5. *Risk of a grim, joyless tone.* → Hester's dry wit; Teo's warmth; the daily pleasures of the academy (the Lantern Hour, salt-sugar pastries, the Ninth-Fathom choir); the comedy of exam culture.
6. *Risk that the saga truth is guessable early.* → Book One shows only that the deep salt is pressured and that something kept in it is alive (a creature). The *people* in the moment are not revealed until Book Four. The founders' guilt comes in Book Five.
