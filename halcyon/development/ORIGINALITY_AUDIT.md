# Originality Audit

This audit separates **broad genre conventions** (free for anyone: academies, mentors, exams, mysteries, magic with costs) from **distinctive expression** (specific combinations, institutions, artefacts, terminology and plot machinery that identify a particular franchise). Any element that drifted toward the second category was replaced.

## A. Harry Potter–adjacent risks, and the decisions taken

| Familiar element | Risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Students sorted into houses that compete for points | Highly distinctive HP machinery | **Removed.** No houses. Students join a *Bench*, a small research group under one Reader, chosen by mutual consent and examined publicly. Belonging is earned, not assigned. |
| Child protagonist entering at 11; boarding school | Core HP/YA shape | Protagonist is **33**, an employed adult with a dependent and a debt. Classmates range from 22 to 40s. Most lodge in a working dormitory; some commute; one is a widowed parent. |
| Orphan with a hidden heritage or destiny | HP, plus a broad cliché | **Rejected.** Hester's parents are a brine-boiler (alive, estranged) and a dead laundress. Her talent is a common aptitude made exceptional by 20 years of customs work. Her importance comes from choices and expertise. |
| Wands, spells, incantations | HP signature | No wands, no words. Magic is a bodily discipline (the *hold*), plus a material (stillsalt), plus a medium (brine). |
| Dark lord and followers | HP signature; generic | No dark lord. Antagonism is structural: the Halcyon itself, the Exchequer, a founding crime. Book One's antagonist is a protective, loved Warden whose reasoning is sound and whose choice is wrong. |
| Headmaster as wise protector | HP signature | The Chancellor is a political survivor who keeps the founding secret. She is not a guardian figure. |
| Secret chamber beneath the school | Broad convention, but HP has a famous one | Ours is not a hidden room with a monster. The *Hush* is the school's lowest layer, where the evidence of the crime is under pressure. It is known, sealed and administered, and its danger is physics, not a creature. |
| Sports tournament / house cup | HP | No sport. The academy's public spectacle is the **Midwinter Assay**, an open examination watched by the city. There is also **Lantern Night**, a civic festival of released summer light. |
| Magical prison, ministry | HP institutions | Our institutions are economic: the **Charter of the Grain** (a licensing monopoly), the **Salt Exchequer**, the **Delvers' Guild**. |

## B. Other franchise and recent-novel risks

| Source | Risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| *The Name of the Wind* (Rothfuss): a university, sympathy magic, "the Archives", a poor gifted student, tuition stress | Poor-scholar-at-university plus sympathetic links | Our "still runs to quick" law could read as sympathy. **Changed:** stillness travels only through physically continuous live brine, like heat through metal. There are no symbolic links and no "binding" words. The library is not a sealed archive with a mythic door; the Brinestacks are a working library of salt tablets. The money anxiety is a care fee, not tuition. |
| *Babel* (Kuang): an academy whose magic powers an empire's economy | Institution-as-extraction | The economic critique is a broad tradition (dark academia's "it is the house"). Ours differs in mechanism (preservation, not translation), in history (the extracted resource is a *people's held moment*), and in resolution (restoration, not revolution). We avoid colonial-translation imagery and silver bars. |
| *The Salt Oracle* (Wilson, 2025): a mentor's death; investigation; save the one or save the college | Structural overlap | The dead man has known Hester **16 days**; he is a sponsor, not a mentor. He is **not dead**: he is kept, which reverses the "dead mentor" shape. The dilemma is **dissolved by an insight** (venting), not decided. |
| *The Salt King* (Pulley, 2026); Lot's wife | "Turned to salt" | No one is transformed into salt. Keepings are clear casings around a living, paused person, and they can be returned. |
| *Piranesi* (Clarke): a house of tides | Tidal mystique | No tidal architecture; our sea is absent. Its *sounds* survive in the salt as a "roar", which is a different device. |
| *The Memory Police* (Ogawa): things vanish | Disappearance | Nothing vanishes in our world; things are *held*. |
| *Mistborn* (Sanderson): metals as magic resource; an oppressive establishment | Mineral magic | Ours is a single material with three operations and a cost paid in the worker's own body-time. The establishment is an academy, not a god-emperor. We do not use "burning" or ingestion. |
| Generic "academy built on a dead god" (various) | Overused | The academy is built in a salt dome on a seabed. The "secret beneath" is a crime of statecraft, not divinity. |

## C. Terminology check

Coined or repurposed terms: Halcyon (the academy), stillsalt/grain, the hold, Keep/Read/Return, arrears, quickening, the Stay, the Stay Choir, the Hush, Fathoms (as levels), Bench, Reader's Privilege, Assay, Lantern Hour, Lantern Night, kept light, the Roar, the Settle, lag, the Cold House, the Cistern, Tiders / the Order of the Returning Tide, the Ebb, the White, the Rim, landships, sols and flecks, the Seal.

Common-word checks:
- "Halcyon" is common English (and Greek myth). Its use here as an academy name and myth anchor is not tied to any known franchise.
- "Reader" as a faculty rank exists at real universities; that is appropriate.
- No term duplicates a distinctive coinage from a major fantasy franchise, as far as reasonable checking allows.

## D. Plot-structure check

- The academy murder mystery is a broad convention. We transform it: the "victim" is alive, the "murderer" kept rather than killed, and the motive is civic protection.
- Saga escalation follows our own logic, not the "returning villain, stronger each year" pattern. City mystery → institutional politics → expedition → first contact → civil war → engineering catastrophe → restitution.

## E. Voice

The narrative voice is developed independently (see STYLE_GUIDE.md): close-third, dry and exact, built on the protagonist's trade vocabulary of weights, tastes and grades. It does not imitate any living author's distinctive style.

## Residual risk

The broad pleasures of the genre (arrival at a wondrous school, first lessons, friendships, an exam, a hidden danger below) are kept deliberately. They are genre conventions, and the brief explicitly asks for that emotional appeal.
