The lead editor's decisions are below. Where this plan and a review disagree, follow this plan. Where this plan says "per CON #n", apply that review's suggested fix (adjusted to read well).

**Length constraint:** the book is ~114k words and must stay at or under 115k. Every span must finish at roughly its current length, plus or minus 3%. Pay for additions with cuts, especially of repeated beats, assayer similes and denial passages.

## A. Global decisions

### A1. Mystery pacing (DEV #2, RDR #1)
Hester begins **privately suspecting Orsolya at the end of Ch. 15**: W.L.F. is decoded, and the corkscrew hand is demonstrated in front of her. She **hides the suspicion** and tells no one. That fits her established flaw: she acts alone and withholds information "to protect people." She also has a real reason: she is on Orsolya's payroll, Orsolya is the Choir's mother, and she wants to be wrong.

From that point:
- **Ch. 16–18 become active concealment and testing.** She watches Orsolya and tests her lightly. She says "Dunmore" aloud while thinking "Orsolya."
- **Ch. 17** confirms the stays are fed, so she now *knows* that the Warden's stays drink the sleepers.
- **Ch. 18**'s dark moment includes the bind: exposing Orsolya means cutting the draw, and cutting the draw floods the Halcyon.
- **Ch. 19** is proof (the notebook, the hand, Aske alive), not revelation.

Remove the repeated "did not let herself think it" device. Keep it at most in Ch. 1 (Tib) and Ch. 17 (Ama).

Give the "O." red herring a little weight in Ch. 7–12: **Reader Odile Fane** (she warned Hester off the Quiet Rooms in Ch. 8). Hester considers her and rules her out with a fact. Keep this light.

### A2. Climax agency (DEV #1)
- **Ch. 21:** after Lucan's "the stays begin to starve", Hester names the gap herself. She bets that Orsolya will come and, once she sees the shaft take water, will hold the stays. The others oppose this as madness. She insists: "I know her. She'll not let them drown."
- **Ch. 24:**
  - Cut Orsolya's "Open your hand, chick."
  - Hester's release comes unprompted, from her own memory of Tib, her father, and "Open your hand" as *she* learned it.
  - **Hester opens first.** Orsolya's move to the trunk *answers* Hester's opening, which pays off the bet.
  - Give Orsolya one spoken line at the turn, tied to Coldbank: "*Shore it,*" she said. "*Shore it, lads.*"
  - Sorle's rescue happens after the vent is already running on its own.

### A3. The night of 16 Russet (DEV #3, CON #6)
- Aske went to the **Old Tap at the ninth bell** to shut it, meaning to be back for Hester at the tenth.
- Orsolya kept him there.
- Orsolya and Dunmore brought the casing up by trolley. They waited on the turn below Cell 3-17 while Hester stood at the door (that is when she heard the wheels and looked over the rail), then waited again while Sorle came and went.
- Sorle came at the half by the Readers' back stair, found the corridor dark, and went back *up* to the Second.
- They delivered the casing **a little after the half**.

Ch. 6, Ch. 12 and Ch. 20 must agree with this.

### A4. Backflow (CON #2)
- **Ch. 20:** "If he'd shut that Tap, the east stays would have starved inside the hour, and the gallery with them."
- **Ch. 25:** Aske's waking words become "—*you can't* hold it, Orsolya, it'll only grow—".

### A5. The rush rescue has a precedent (CON #3)
- **Ch. 21:** in the release training, Sorle or Annis Rule mentions that Physic damps a returner's rush by laying a still hand over theirs in shared brine ("still runs to quick; it runs into a racing heart as well as a quickening"). Optionally have Sorle demonstrate it on Hester after a small moth-release flush.
- **Ch. 24:** the floodwater has eaten the clear Hush floor and runs **thick with dissolved Hush grain, live brine to the elbow**. The Fathomers put their fingers in the **brine-slots** of the trunk. After Sorle wakes, Hester has a short lag beat.

### A6. The offer must cost something; restore the clock (DEV #4); real loss (RDR #2)
- **Ch. 21:** Hester asks Lucan and Teo, on their next pass under the Cold House, to **reopen Tib's sleeve**, so that he is drawn like the others until the cut. That is her refusal of Orsolya's offer, and it costs her.
- **Ch. 21:** Agathe's letter (~9 Rime) reports that the **Corrie twins'** casings (planted in Ch. 1), **Nell and Jem Corrie**, are crazing. Assay Night is now the *earliest* possible night, not a convenient one.
- **Ch. 22/23:** on 19 Rime **Jem Corrie comes back and dies**. Hester learns of it on the morning of the Assay. This is the price of waiting for the emptiest night: her choice, and she owns it.
- **Ch. 26:** Hester visits Jem's empty plinth. Nell survives.

### A7. Aldous (DEV #5)
In the **Ch. 21** Pans scene, replace the avoidance with 300–400 words. Hester asks why he didn't sign. Aldous answers in his idiom: her name was on the first form, so the last one has to be both of theirs.

### A8. Middle sag (DEV #6, RDR #6)
- **Ch. 8:** compress the sponsor refusals to one paragraph. Keep Crisp's line, Fane's warning, and Sorle's "charity cases from the dead".
- **Ch. 10:** remove the discovery of the drains and the torn ledger page. Keep the annealing lesson, Bett and Ruth, Lucan's presence and the ending.
- **Ch. 12:** Lucan produces the torn page at the bedside.
- **Ch. 14:** replace the "mislaid" letter with a **direct, quietly threatening scene with Deputy Chancellor Vey**. Vey returns nothing, praises her diligence, and reminds her that a sponsorless aspirant's place is in the Chancellor's gift. Cut one repeated Agathe "worse" letter.

### A9. Ending costs and complicity (RDR #2, DEV #8)
- **Hester's heart.** The rush leaves permanent damage. In Ch. 25 Annis Rule says her heart "keeps its own time now", ticking like a pan, and that another rush like it will stop it. This carries into Book 2.
- **Sorle.** His left hand is Settled cold and won't close (Ch. 24–25).
- **Ch. 25:**
  - Dramatise **Orsolya's confession to Gage with Hester present**, in 500–700 words. On the record she names the **Seedmonth 901** meeting with **Holm and Vey** ("manage it quietly").
  - Add a ~300-word beat where **Teo tells Hester what the Cistern was like** at the twelfth bell.
  - Compress other summary to make room.
- **Ch. 26:**
  - In the cell scene, Orsolya explains two things in three or four lines each:
    - the **seventh dish at the Assay**: she wanted Holm to hear it said aloud by someone they couldn't dismiss;
    - the **corkscrew lesson**: "part of me wanted somebody to see it."
  - Before the ward scene, Hester asks Mrs. Arden for Tib's form and writes in the margin "*Let me sleep*" with the date, putting his words on the record (DEV #9).

### A10. Voice and tics (RDR #4, #5, #9)
- "chick": at most ~20 in the whole book. Orsolya *drops* it at turning points.
- Assayer similes ("as she would have…", short weights, samples): at most one per chapter, and **none** in the big emotional scenes of Ch. 18, 20 and 24.
- Reduce these shapes:
  - "She did not X. She did not X now."
  - "It was not X. It was Y."
  - "Nobody said anything / spoke / answered."
  - "her face did a thing."
- Cut the "Somebody should write a ballad" roster line (Ch. 16).

### A11. Thornes (RDR #7)
Define the unit at first need, in **Ch. 5** or **Ch. 13**. It is Hester's private scale for the weight behind the eyes, named for old Mr. Thorne. Deep at a hundred and forty fathoms is about six thornes, and the *Wren* cargo eleven.

### A12. The academy thread (RDR #10)
Keep **Crisp's release practice** alive with brief beats in Ch. 15, Ch. 19 and Ch. 21: Hester practising, failing, and slowly improving, so that the Ch. 22 moth is earned.

### A13. Plant bloom timing (CON table)
Have Callow (Ch. 9) or Sorle (Ch. 14) mention in passing that a dead keeping blooms "in a month or so, whatever the grade."
