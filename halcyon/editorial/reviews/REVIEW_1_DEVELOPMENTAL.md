# Developmental Review 1: *What the Salt Keeps*

**Scope:** all 26 synopses; Ch. 1, 3, 6, 11, 13, 17, 18, 20–26 in full; openings, endings and plot-bearing passages of the rest.

## Verdict

Revise and resubmit. This is a strong book. The prose is controlled, the magic system is tactile and costly, and the emotional spine (a sister who kept her brother against his stated wish) is set up in Ch. 1, confessed in Ch. 18 and paid off at the climax. Orsolya Thane is a credible antagonist with an earned motive.

Three structural faults keep it from being ready:

1. At the climax, the vent succeeds because the antagonist fills a hole in Hester's plan.
2. The reader identifies Orsolya roughly eight chapters before Hester does, and the book bridges the gap by having Hester deny it again and again.
3. The personal stakes (Tib's clock, Aldous's threat to sign, Orsolya's offer) are defused offstage or by other people, not by Hester's choices.

All three can be fixed without touching what works.

## Top 10 Problems, Ranked by Severity

### 1. The climax depends on Orsolya, not Hester

> "And the moment we cut," said Lucan, "the stays begin to starve." (Ch. 21)
>
> "Open your hand, chick," she said. (Ch. 24)

**Why it matters.** The plan in Ch. 21 names the starving Fifth Fathom stays as a risk and never solves it. In Ch. 24 the Fifth holds only because Orsolya sends Brannoc and Iley to the trunk and holds it herself. Then the line that unlocks Hester's release comes from the antagonist, and it is the same line Orsolya used in Ch. 11. After that, Sorle saves Hester from the rush. Hester's turn about Tib is excellent but arrives after Orsolya's prompt, and since she released unaided at the Assay (Ch. 22), needing coaching again reads as regression.

**Revision.**
- In Ch. 21, after Lucan's line, have Hester name the gap and gamble that Orsolya will come and hold the stays once she sees the shaft take water, a bet on Orsolya's character that the others oppose.
- In Ch. 24, cut Orsolya's "Open your hand, chick" and the paragraph break after it. Let the memory of Tib come unprompted. Hester should open her hand first, and Orsolya's move to the trunk should answer it, so that it pays off Hester's bet.
- Keep Sorle's rescue, but place it after the vent is running on its own ("a river climbing"), so that it saves Hester's life, not the plan.

### 2. The reader is far ahead of Hester on Orsolya

> "It added up. It added up quite well, if you were careful which figures you carried." (Ch. 15)

**Why it matters.** By the end of Ch. 15 the reader has:
- the salt-mud and cold hands on the Causeway (Ch. 4);
- the widdershins spiral (Ch. 6);
- Orsolya knowing Coldwater Street (Ch. 11);
- W.L.F. decoded as *Warden of the Lower Fathoms* (Ch. 15);
- a demonstration of Orsolya's spiral hand (Ch. 15).

Ch. 17 then adds the left-leaning capitals, and Hester does not concede until Ch. 19. In between, the device "she did not let herself think it" recurs at least eight times (Ch. 6, 7, 13, 15 twice, 17, 19, 20). Denial is thematically apt, but this often it makes an expert assayer look obtuse and drains tension from Ch. 16–19.

**Revision.**
- Move the kept-candle spiral demonstration from Ch. 15 into Ch. 19, so that Hester recognises the hand from memory and in the same moment.
- Alternatively, end Ch. 15 with Hester privately suspecting Orsolya and deciding to hide it. Ch. 16–19 then become active concealment.
- Cut the left-leaning capitals from Ch. 17, which are redundant.
- Keep the "did not let herself" construction only in Ch. 1 (Tib) and Ch. 17 (Ama).

### 3. The timeline of Aske's keeping contradicts itself

> "He sent word he wanted to see me at the Tap," she said. "The sixteenth, the tenth bell." (Ch. 20)

**Why it matters.** Ch. 5 has Aske inviting Hester to his Cell at the tenth bell. Ch. 12 has him inviting Sorle for half past. Ch. 20 has him meeting Orsolya at the Old Tap, five fathoms down, also at the tenth bell. Orsolya and Dunmore then deliver the casing "at the half bell", exactly when Sorle says he found the corridor dark and empty. Careful readers will reconstruct this timeline.

**Revision.**
- Ch. 20: change "the tenth bell" to "the ninth bell", and "at the half bell" to "a little after the half."
- Ch. 12: have Sorle arrive at "a quarter past" and hear wheels below that he takes for porters. This doubles the clue and removes the collision.
- Recheck Gage's timing note in Ch. 6 against the new times.

### 4. Orsolya's offer costs Hester nothing

> She had not said yes. She had not needed to. (Ch. 20)

**Why it matters.** The book's best moral trap (Tib is safe because the murderer spared him) is never acted on. Ch. 22 asserts that Orsolya "knew there would be no answer to her offer", but Hester never refuses. Then the cut dries every casing, so the dilemma resolves itself. Meanwhile Tib's "weeks, not months" clock (Ch. 18) disappears in Ch. 20, and nothing replaces it. Ch. 21 spans fifteen unpressured days.

**Revision.**
- In Ch. 21, after "That's the account", have Hester ask Lucan and Teo to reopen Tib's sleeve on their next pass under the Cold House, so that he is drawn like the others until the cut. This turns refusal into an act with a cost and makes Ch. 22's line true.
- Add an Agathe letter in Ch. 21, around the ninth of Rime, reporting that the Corrie twins' casings (planted in Ch. 1) have begun to craze. This restores the clock, and Assay Night becomes the earliest possible night rather than a convenient one.

### 5. Aldous's threat to sign the release is defused offstage

> Then he put the pen down and went out. He did not sign. He did not come down to the ward. (Ch. 19)

**Why it matters.** Ch. 18 gives Aldous the book's hardest line ("You kept him for you") and a legal threat. The turn comes in a three-sentence letter, and Ch. 21 avoids it on purpose: "Neither of them said anything about the desk." The ending (Ch. 26) depends on Aldous accepting Hester's deferral, but the book never shows him deciding anything.

**Revision.**
- In Ch. 21's Pans scene, cut "Neither of them said anything about the desk at the Cold House, or the pen. It stood between them in the steam like a third person, and they let it."
- Replace it with 300–400 words in which Hester asks why he didn't sign, and Aldous answers in his own idiom: because her name was on the first form, and the last one has to be decided by both of them.

### 6. The middle sags (Ch. 7–10 and Ch. 14–16)

> "I'm full," she said, "and you're unfinished. Next pan, please." (Ch. 8)

**Why it matters.** Ch. 8 spends its first third on a round of six sponsor refusals. Ch. 9, 10 and 12 each re-deliver the same discovery: a line runs east from the Cold House to the Fifth Fathom (the Water Board map, then the drains on Lantern Night along with Lucan's "cistern maintenance" page, then Lucan's summary). The Bench deadline set in Ch. 6, the 21st of Rime, never bites. Sorle simply sponsors her in Ch. 14.

**Revision.**
- Compress the sponsor round in Ch. 8 to a single paragraph. Keep Crisp's line, Fane's warning about the Quiet Rooms (a plant), and Sorle's "I do not take charity cases from the dead"; cut Bassano's, Rusk's and Pym's refusals.
- Remove the drain-and-page discovery from Ch. 10, keeping the annealing seed, Bett and Ruth Linnow, and the sea-wall ending. Move Lucan's torn page to Ch. 12, where he can produce it at the bedside.
- Either drop the Bench deadline or make it real: Sorle sets a date for evidence, and the confiscated rod costs her that date.
- Target: cut 5,000–6,000 words across Ch. 8–10.

### 7. Orsolya's decisive turn is left opaque

> Perhaps only arithmetic. Orsolya had always had a gift for sums. (Ch. 24)

**Why it matters.** The antagonist's most important choice in the book is filtered through Hester guessing "perhaps" three times. Two other choices of Orsolya's are also unexplained:
- why she demonstrates her identifying spiral to the aspirant who is investigating her (Ch. 15);
- why she sets the quickened Hush dish for Hester's Assay (Ch. 22).

Unexplained, they make her look plot-driven.

**Revision.**
- In Ch. 24, give Orsolya one spoken line at the turn, tied to Coldbank. The narration already supplies the material: "if they'd only shored it."
- In the Ch. 26 cell scene, add an exchange of three or four lines in which Hester asks about the seventh dish and Orsolya answers. The best answer is that she wanted the panel, and Holm, to hear it said aloud by someone they couldn't dismiss. That also links to Holm's later grief.

### 8. The denouement is reported, not dramatized, and the institutions escape

> described the events of the night of the twenty-first of Rime as a natural quickening event of unusual magnitude. (Ch. 25)

**Why it matters.** The following all happen offstage or in summary:
- Orsolya's confession to Gage, relayed by "a red-eared young Proctor";
- Lucan at the Assize;
- the Cistern cut itself, Lucan's and Teo's whole climax, conveyed through Agathe's letter and a scab.

Holm and Vey ordered "manage it quietly" (Ch. 20) and made the rod disappear (Ch. 14), yet they face no scene of reckoning. An open Assize suits a series opener; unacknowledged complicity does not.

**Revision.**
- In Ch. 25, dramatize Orsolya's confession in 500–700 words, with Hester present. Have Orsolya name the Seedmonth 901 meeting with Holm and Vey on the record, so that the Assize thread is planted rather than summarised.
- Add a short beat (about 300 words) in which Teo tells Hester what the Cistern was like at the twelfth bell.

### 9. The ending risks endorsing keeping

> I'll not let you go today. I want you to know why, because you've a right to know. (Ch. 26)

**Why it matters.** The ending has Tib kept, Aske returned alive, and the sea returned alive from nine hundred years of keeping. Without a counterweight, the book's thesis tilts towards "keeping was right after all." Hester's promise is moving but is a deferral.

**Revision.** Add one concrete act in Ch. 26 that gives Tib's wish a place on the record. Before the ward scene, Hester asks the new director, Mrs. Arden, for Tib's form, and she writes into the margin his words, "*Let me sleep*", with the date. This answers Ch. 1's form, which "did not ask what the sleeper himself might have said," and makes her deferral accountable.

### 10. Continuity slips in a clue-driven book

> "Saskia showed us, the first day, from the Lantern gallery." (Ch. 21)

**Why it matters.** In Ch. 4 a Proctor names the Well-Cap, not Saskia. Hester's deductions rest on exact recall, so misquotation damages trust. Also:
- "kind this season" (Ch. 17) against "kind this autumn" (Ch. 19);
- Ch. 18 assigns the Physic's "arrears" verdict from Ch. 6 to Gage.

**Revision.** Ch. 21: change "Saskia showed us" to "The Proctor showed us", and "she said" to "he said." Make the Ch. 19 quote match Ch. 17 ("this season"). Ch. 18: change "Gage's voice" to "The Physic's voice." Then do a full quote-against-source check of every recalled line in Ch. 19 and 20.

## What Must NOT Be Changed

- **Ch. 1's opening:** the weeping beads, the forbidden taste ("quick"), the clear/grey rule, and the memory Hester steps round. The rule's inversion (Ch. 6, 19, 25) is the book's best clue.
- **Aldous's pan (Ch. 3), the full craft lesson (Ch. 21), and "Stand to the side".** This seed carries the climax. Also keep his lone applause at the Assay (Ch. 22).
- **Ch. 17, the Silent Supper.** Tib tasted in the stay-line and Ama dying mid-note is the book's best set piece.
- **Ch. 18 as a whole:** Bett Linnow's three minutes, "You kept him for you," the confession to Teo, "keeping two people," and the near-theft of the Hush residue ending in "She opened her hand."
- **Orsolya's confession and offer (Ch. 20),** especially her father's story and "Did your lad sign his own form, chick?" Do not soften her.
- **The release lesson (Ch. 21)** with Teo's Vigil candle ("You take your hands away from *round* it").
- **The gull, the crab and Holm's line** "We only hoped to be dead first" (Ch. 25), and Aske waking mid-sentence.
- **The Ch. 26 cell exchange** ("Did it hurt?" "Yes." "Good."), Aldous kissing the casing, and the closing hook of a voice in the new salt ("*Aelith?*").
- **The ensemble voices:** Teo, Saskia, Lucan and Sorle are each distinct.
- **The magic's costs** (lag, rush, the Settle) and the thorne unit. Every use of power in the book is paid for.
