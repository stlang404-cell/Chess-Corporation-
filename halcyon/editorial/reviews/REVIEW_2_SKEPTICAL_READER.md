# Review 2: The Skeptical Reader

**Book:** *What the Salt Keeps*
**Read:** Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 19, 23 and 26 in full. The first 60 and last 40 lines of every other chapter. I used the pass-2 synopses only to fill gaps in chapters 11, 13, 17, 18, 20–22, 24 and 25.

---

## Rating: 3.5 / 5

I opened this expecting another gifted-outsider-finds-a-conspiracy book. The first chapter got me anyway. "Hester Vail's brother had been fourteen for eleven years" is a real opening line, and the Cold House is a real place. A thirty-three-year-old customs assayer beats a teenager with a hidden bloodline, and the magic has laws that the plot actually uses.

It loses stars for three reasons. The mystery is solved by the reader about eleven chapters before the heroine solves it. The middle third keeps re-reporting the same bad news. And the ending lets almost everyone off. The prose is well above average but repeats its moves until the machinery shows.

---

## Top 10 complaints (ranked)

**1. The villain is obvious by Chapter 8, and Hester doesn't see it until Chapter 19.**
> "The Stays have been kind this season. Kinder than they've any right to be." (Ch. 8)

There's a note that says *Ask O.*, a warm Lowside mentor named Orsolya, salt-mud on her boots as she comes *from the city* in Ch. 4, and that line. Together they settle it. Hester's "stepping round" is meant as a flaw, but it makes the sharpest observer in the book the slowest. **Fix:** Give the O-red-herring real weight (Odile Fane is sitting right there; the Order of the Tide only gets a single line). Better: let Hester suspect Orsolya by Ch. 12–13 and make the middle about *proving* it while on her payroll.

**2. The ending costs almost nothing.**
> "'Well, of course you did,' said Corvin Aske, and began, absurdly, to laugh" (Ch. 25)

The mentor who was "killed" in Ch. 6 comes back with a stiff hand. The villain is Settled "for hours, perhaps a day," confesses, and gets a tender cell scene. Sorle is vindicated. Lucan's father resigns. Hester's father reconciles with her. The sea returns with crabs. Only two secondary characters die, both before the climax. **Fix:** Make one irreversible loss land at the climax: Sorle doesn't come back from the lag, or some of the charity sleepers come back badly when the draw is cut, or Aske stays kept.

**3. The cast is a familiar party of types.**
> "The Lord Exchequer's heir, a defrocked Tider, a widow with a barrow, and a customs officer with no job. Somebody should write a ballad." (Ch. 16)

The book recites its own party roster. Humming mentor removed early (Aske), gruff mentor with a dead wife (Sorle), haughty rich boy who thaws (Lucan), comic-relief baking monk (Teo), prickly widow with an adorable child (Saskia). **Fix:** Cut the ballad line. At the climax, give at least one companion a goal that pulls against Hester's.

**4. Tics are standing in for voice.**
> "'Oh, chick,' she said. 'Oh, chick.'" (Ch. 24)

"Chick" appears **42 times in 15 chapters**. Ama says "lamb," Agathe says "my dear," Hester says "Ta" (8 times) and "I'll not." The voices are distinct, but mostly because each speaker has a badge. Sorle and Teo share one rhythm of dry aphorism. **Fix:** Cut the pet names by half. Have Orsolya *drop* it at turning points, as Ch. 26 does once, to real effect.

**5. The assayer metaphors never stop.**
> "She noticed it, and set it down, as she would have set down a short weight." (Ch. 14)

Every thought gets weighed, graded, ledgered, or "set aside like a sample she had not the reagent for." Lovely in Ch. 1–2; by Ch. 14 a crutch; in the Ch. 20 confrontation it distances the scene that most needs closeness. **Fix:** Allow one customs simile per chapter at most, and none in the three biggest emotional scenes.

**6. The middle (Ch. 14–18) sags and leans on stock beats.**
> "I regret to tell you that the item you deposited on the fifteenth has been mislaid." (Ch. 14)

The evidence vanishes from the compromised cop's locker. Agathe's letters report "worse" again and again. Stay shifts run the same way each time. The mentor's coded notebook turns up hidden in the archive. **Fix:** Merge Ch. 14 and 15. Replace "mislaid" with a direct, threatening scene with Vey. Cut one Cold House letter.

**7. The climax is hard to follow, and its key unit is never defined.**
> "Forty thornes." (Ch. 23)

"Thornes" first shows up in Ch. 21 and is never explained. In Ch. 23–24, with taps, plug, timing, "Hold three. Open two," and two miles of simultaneous action, I reread to see who held what. **Fix:** Define the unit early, in Ch. 5 or in the Brinestacks. Give a Ch. 21 briefing the reader can tick off.

**8. The book raises a moral dilemma and then engineers its way out.**
> "Nobody in it was stupid and nobody was wrong" (Ch. 21)

Orsolya's case ("You know what it is to choose who drowns") is the strongest idea in the book. Then a third option, the vent, means nobody has to choose. (The same idea already appears in Ch. 16.) **Fix:** The vent should cost something, so that Hester really does choose who pays.

**9. Repeated sentence shapes give it a machine-made feel.**
> "Hester had stopped looking at the woman with the baby some years ago. She did not look now." (Ch. 1)

That "She did not X. She did not X now." pattern turns up twice in the first chapter alone. Add "It was not X. It was Y." (17), "her face did a thing / a complicated thing" (11), "Nobody said anything / spoke / answered" (14), and short one-word paragraphs like "Quick." **Fix:** Do a line-edit pass aimed at these shapes.

**10. The academy fades after the midpoint.**
> "She went to lectures. She sat in the Black Bench … and wrote down every word and heard none." (Ch. 20)

Chapters 4–5 sell the school beautifully. Then Crisp, Fane, Bassano and Radik become cameos. **Fix:** Keep one academic thread running to the Assay. Crisp's release lessons are the obvious one.

**Continuity slips I caught along the way:** Ennis is a young man with a moustache in Ch. 1 but "she" in Agathe's letter in Ch. 19. Sorle's wife Lise has a "broad face" in Ch. 12 and a "long face" in Ch. 14. Sorle is "tall … thin as a heron" in Ch. 5 but "smaller" (Ch. 11) and "small and old" (Ch. 12). Teo bakes on a smuggled spirit stove (Ch. 5–6), but in Ch. 16 fires are forbidden and he bakes with a kept-light jar. Hester heads her ledger "Declaration of Interest" in Ch. 7, but Ch. 16 calls it "*Suspects*." Ch. 16 says Orsolya served tea with Hask honey, but that was Aske's tea in Ch. 3. There's a stray comma splice in Ch. 13 ("vaulting, A short passage").

## Where I got bored / confused

- **Bored:** Ch. 14's arithmetic; the Ch. 22 Assay procession; the Ch. 21 planning meeting; the fourth "your lad is worse" letter.
- **Confused:** the geography of the Fifth, the trunk, the Old Tap and the Hush; "thornes"; why Sorle's theory survives Orsolya's warm-crust objection (never answered); Ch. 24 logistics.

## Characters

- **Alive:** Hester, above all. Her money sense is characterization done right. Saskia, whose "I do rent" and the annealing scene are the best secondary work in the book. Sorle. Callow. Aldous, who says almost nothing and lands "That'll do me."
- **Flat:** Vey (a smile in a corridor), Gage (the cop who's been told to stop looking), Dunmore (bland until he runs), Holm (a Settled figurehead who exists to deliver one line), Lucan's father.

## Is the academy a place I wanted to be?

Yes, for six chapters. The Lantern Hour, the First Taste, the Choir and the kept lightning bolt make it the most desirable school I've read in years. The book leaves it too soon.

## Magic

It's clear, interesting, and has rules. The costs (lag, the Settle, thirst) show on bodies, the Fifth Law is demonstrated before the plot needs it, and the outside-in casing clue is fair play. By the end, though, it bends to whatever the climax requires.

## Derivative / cliché

Poor outsider sponsored in; rich rival insulted at the entry ritual, later redeemed; early mentor "death"; mislaid evidence; archivist guarding the notebook; heist during the feast; exam passed with distinction; rot reaching the Chancellor; a near-confession from the rival; a sequel hook made of a mystery word ("*Aelith?*").

## What I loved

- **The first chapter.** Casings like "ice that had been taught some manners."
- **Taste as weight.** A new magic sense, used consistently.
- **Lantern Night.** Summer poured over a winter city. Pure wonder.
- **Sorle's Folly.** The idea that the sea never left but is *kept* is a big conceptual twist.
- **The gull.** Ch. 2 to Ch. 9 to Ch. 25: overused, but it pays off.
- **Saskia's annealing lesson.** "Cool it slow and the stress walks out. Cool it fast and it waits for you."
- **The casing confession in Ch. 26.** "*Let me sleep, Hes.*" Set up since Ch. 1, and earned. Aldous's kiss on the salt got me. The emotional ending *is* earned. The plot ending isn't.

## Would I buy Book Two?

Yes, in paperback, and because of Hester and the world, not the "*Aelith?*" hook. If Book Two keeps the prose, cuts the tics in half, and lets something stay broken, I'll buy it in hardback.
