## The most serious problems found, and what was done
1. **The reader identified the antagonist about eight chapters before the heroine did** (Reviews 1 and 2). The gap was covered by a repeated "she did not let herself think it" device.
   *Revision:* Hester now privately suspects Orsolya at the end of Ch. 15 and *conceals* it, which fits her established flaw of acting alone. Ch. 16–18 became active concealment. Ch. 19 is proof (the notebook, the casing's hand, and Aske alive) rather than revelation. A light red herring (Reader Odile Fane as "O.") was added, and the denial device was cut.
2. **The climax depended on the antagonist** (Review 1).
   *Revision:* In Ch. 21 Hester names the gap in her own plan and bets on Orsolya's character against her friends' objections. In Ch. 24 Hester releases first, unprompted. Orsolya's choice to hold the stays now *answers* Hester and pays off the bet.
3. **The ending cost too little** (Review 2).
   *Revision:* A sleeper, Jem Corrie, dies on 19 Rime. That is the price of Hester choosing to wait for the safest night, and she owns it. Hester's heart is permanently damaged; another major release will kill her (a Book Two constraint). Sorle's left hand is Settled. The founders' archive gallery is flooded.
4. **Moral dilemma dissolved without cost** (Reviews 1 and 2).
   *Revision:* Hester actively refuses Orsolya's private bargain by having Tib's line reopened, so her brother is drawn again until the cut. The deadline clock was restored.
5. **Offstage resolution** (Review 1).
   *Revision:* Orsolya's confession is now dramatised, with Hester present, and on the record names the Chancellor and Deputy Chancellor. Teo's account of the Cistern cut was added. Aldous's unsigned release is resolved on the page in his own voice.
6. **Magic introduced at the climax without precedent; a "backflow" that broke the Fifth Law** (Review 3).
   *Revision:* The rush-damping technique is planted and demonstrated in Ch. 21. The floodwater is established as live brine. The backflow line was replaced.
7. **Middle sag and repeated discoveries** (Reviews 1 and 2).
   *Revision:* The sponsor-refusal round was compressed, a duplicate discovery cut from Ch. 10, and the "mislaid evidence" letter replaced with a direct, threatening scene with the Deputy Chancellor.
8. **Prose tics** (Review 2).
   *Revision:*
   - "chick" was cut from 42 to 19 uses.
   - Assayer similes were limited to about one per chapter and removed from the three biggest emotional scenes.
   - Repeated sentence shapes ("It was not X. It was Y.", "Nobody said anything", "her face did a thing") were reduced.
   - The banned-phrase list was enforced; the automated scan finds 0 occurrences.

## Subjective assessment (lead editor; not a verified result)
- **Strengths:** the opening line and Ch. 1; the tactile, costed magic (taste-as-weight, lag, rush, the Settle); Lantern Night; the Silent Supper (Ch. 17); the Ch. 18 confession; a persuasive antagonist; the gull pay-off; and an ending that resolves the book's central conflict while opening the saga.
- **Remaining weaknesses** a reader may notice:
  - some supporting roles are archetypal (Vey, Gage and Dunmore are thin);
  - the central mystery is still guessable to genre-savvy readers before Hester proves it, though the narrative now frames this as her concealment, not obtuseness;
  - a few chapters (21, 25) carry dense logistics.
